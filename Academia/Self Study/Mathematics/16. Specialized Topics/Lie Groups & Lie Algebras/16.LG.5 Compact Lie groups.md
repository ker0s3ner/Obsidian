## Definition and Basic Examples

A **compact Lie group** is a Lie group that is compact as a topological space. Key examples: $SO(n)$, $SU(n)$, $U(n)$, and tori $T^n = (S^1)^n$.

---

## Why Compactness Matters

- **Haar measure** exists and is finite, so averaging arguments (as in finite group theory) work.
- Every finite-dimensional representation is **unitarizable** and decomposes into irreducibles (complete reducibility), by averaging an inner product over the group.
- The **maximal torus theorem**: every compact connected Lie group has a maximal torus $T$, and every element is conjugate to an element of $T$.

> [!example] Compact vs. non-compact behavior
> $SU(2)$ (compact) has only finite-dimensional unitary irreducibles. $SL(2,\mathbb{R})$ (non-compact) requires infinite-dimensional representations for a full unitary theory.
