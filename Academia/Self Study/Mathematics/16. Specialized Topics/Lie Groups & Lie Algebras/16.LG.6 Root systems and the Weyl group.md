## Cartan Subalgebras and Roots

For a semisimple complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, fix a **Cartan subalgebra** $\mathfrak{h}$ (maximal abelian, acting diagonally). Decomposing $\mathfrak{g}$ under the adjoint action of $\mathfrak{h}$ gives the **root space decomposition**:

$$\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$$

where $\Phi \subset \mathfrak{h}^*$ is the finite set of **roots**.

---

## The Weyl Group

The **Weyl group** $W$ is generated by reflections across hyperplanes orthogonal to each root. It acts on $\mathfrak{h}^*$ and permutes the root system $\Phi$.

| Type | Root system | Weyl group |
|------|-------------|------------|
| $A_{n-1}$ ($\mathfrak{sl}_n$) | $e_i - e_j$ | $S_n$ |
| $B_n$ ($\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}$) | $\pm e_i, \pm e_i \pm e_j$ | signed permutations |

> [!tip] Why root systems classify Lie algebras
> The combinatorics of the root system (encoded in a Dynkin diagram) completely determines the semisimple Lie algebra — this is the content of the Cartan–Killing classification.
