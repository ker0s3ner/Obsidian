## Semisimplicity

A Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is **semisimple** if it has no nonzero solvable ideals, equivalently (Cartan's criterion) if its **Killing form** $\kappa(X,Y) = \text{tr}(\text{ad}(X)\text{ad}(Y))$ is non-degenerate.

Every semisimple Lie algebra decomposes as a direct sum of **simple** Lie algebras (no nontrivial ideals).

---

## Cartan–Killing Classification

Simple complex Lie algebras fall into four infinite families plus five exceptions, encoded by **Dynkin diagrams**:

| Family | Algebra | Rank |
|--------|---------|------|
| $A_n$ | $\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}$ | $n$ |
| $B_n$ | $\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}$ | $n$ |
| $C_n$ | $\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}$ | $n$ |
| $D_n$ | $\mathfrak{so}_{2n}$ | $n$ |
| $E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2$ | exceptional | 6, 7, 8, 4, 2 |

> [!example] Why this is remarkable
> Every simple Lie algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ — an object built from continuous symmetry — reduces to a finite combinatorial gadget (a Dynkin diagram).

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is simple and identify its type.
2. Show the Killing form of an abelian Lie algebra is identically zero (hence not semisimple unless trivial).
