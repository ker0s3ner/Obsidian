## Definition

An **abelian category** is a category with a zero object, biproducts, kernels, cokernels, and where every monomorphism is a kernel and every epimorphism is a cokernel. This is the natural setting for homological algebra: it has enough structure to define exact sequences.

**Examples**: $R\text{-Mod}$ (modules over a ring), sheaves of abelian groups, chain complexes over an abelian category.

---

## Exact Sequences

A sequence $A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C$ is **exact at $B$** if $\text{im}(f) = \ker(g)$. A **short exact sequence**:

$$0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$$

means $A \hookrightarrow B$ is injective, $B \twoheadrightarrow C$ is surjective, and the image of the first map equals the kernel of the second.

> [!tip] Why abelian categories matter
> Every construction of classical homological algebra (kernels, exactness, Ext, Tor) makes sense verbatim in any abelian category — not just modules.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $\mathbf{Set}$ is not abelian (it lacks additive structure).
2. Verify that the category of finite abelian groups is abelian.
3. Prove that in an abelian category, every morphism factors as (epi) followed by (mono).
