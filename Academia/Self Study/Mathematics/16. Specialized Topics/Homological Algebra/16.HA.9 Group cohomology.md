## Definition

For a group $G$ and a $G$-module $M$, group cohomology $H^i(G, M)$ is defined as the derived functor of taking $G$-invariants:

$$H^i(G, M) = \text{Ext}^i_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$$

$H^0(G,M) = M^G$ (the invariants); $H^1(G,M)$ classifies **crossed homomorphisms** modulo principal ones.

---

## Interpretation of $H^1$ and $H^2$

- $H^1(G, M)$ measures obstructions to splitting a group extension with abelian kernel $M$ acted on by $G$.
- $H^2(G, M)$ classifies extensions $0 \to M \to E \to G \to 1$ up to equivalence — the analogue of $\text{Ext}^1$ for group extensions rather than module extensions.

> [!example] Trivial action case
> If $G$ acts trivially on $M = \mathbb{Z}$, then $H^1(G, \mathbb{Z}) = \text{Hom}(G, \mathbb{Z})$, recovering the abelianization pairing.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $H^0(G, M)$ for $G$ acting trivially on $M$.
2. Show that a crossed homomorphism $f: G \to M$ with $f(gh) = f(g) + g\cdot f(h)$ becomes principal when the extension splits.
3. Explain the role of $H^2(G,M)$ in classifying group extensions.
