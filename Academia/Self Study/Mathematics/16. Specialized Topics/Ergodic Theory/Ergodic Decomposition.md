## Statement

Every invariant measure $\mu$ for a measure-preserving system $(X, T)$ can be written as an average (integral) over **ergodic** invariant measures:

$$\mu = \int_{\mathcal{E}} \nu \, d\lambda(\nu)$$

where $\mathcal{E}$ is the set of ergodic measures and $\lambda$ is a measure on $\mathcal{E}$.

---

## Interpretation

Ergodic measures are the "extreme points" of the (convex) set of invariant measures — a non-ergodic system is literally a mixture of ergodic ones, each living on its own invariant subset.

> [!tip] Why this simplifies things
> Any question about a general invariant measure reduces to the ergodic case: Birkhoff's theorem applies directly on each ergodic component, and the decomposition assembles the full picture.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain why the set of invariant measures is convex.
2. Show that a system with exactly two disjoint invariant sets of positive measure has a nontrivial ergodic decomposition.
3. Relate the ergodic decomposition to extreme points of a convex set.
