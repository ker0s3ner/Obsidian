## Definition

A functor $F : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ is an **equivalence** if there exists $G : \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{C}$ with natural isomorphisms $GF \cong \text{id}_{\mathcal{C}}$ and $FG \cong \text{id}_{\mathcal{D}}$.

Equivalently, $F$ is an equivalence iff it is **fully faithful** and **essentially surjective** (every object of $\mathcal{D}$ is isomorphic to $F(A)$ for some $A$).

> [!note] Weaker than isomorphism
> Categories can be equivalent without being isomorphic — equivalence only requires objects to match up to isomorphism, not on the nose.

---

## Example

The category of finite-dimensional vector spaces is equivalent to the category with one object per natural number $n$ (representing $\mathbb{R}^n$) and morphisms given by matrices — a "skeleton" of the original category.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that a fully faithful, essentially surjective functor is an equivalence.
2. Give two categories that are equivalent but not isomorphic.
3. Explain why equivalence of categories preserves categorical properties (products, limits) up to isomorphism.
