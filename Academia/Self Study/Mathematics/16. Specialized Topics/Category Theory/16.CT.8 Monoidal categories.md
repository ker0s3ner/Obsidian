## Definition

A **monoidal category** is a category $\mathcal{C}$ equipped with a bifunctor $\otimes : \mathcal{C} \times \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{C}$, a unit object $I$, and natural isomorphisms for associativity and unit laws:

$$(A \otimes B) \otimes C \cong A \otimes (B \otimes C), \qquad I \otimes A \cong A \cong A \otimes I$$

subject to coherence conditions (the **pentagon** and **triangle identities**).

---

## Examples

| Category | $\otimes$ | Unit $I$ |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| $\mathbf{Set}$ | Cartesian product | singleton set |
| $\mathbf{Vect}_k$ | tensor product | $k$ |
| $R\text{-Mod}$ | tensor product over $R$ | $R$ |

---

## Braided and Symmetric Monoidal Categories

A monoidal category is **braided** if it has a natural isomorphism $A \otimes B \cong B \otimes A$ satisfying compatibility with $\otimes$, and **symmetric** if applying it twice gives the identity.

> [!example] Vector spaces
> $\mathbf{Vect}_k$ is symmetric monoidal via the swap map on the tensor product.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the pentagon identity holds for $(\mathbf{Set}, \times, \{*\})$.
2. Show that a monoid in $(\mathbf{Set}, \times)$ is an ordinary monoid.
3. Explain what a monoid object in a general monoidal category generalizes.
