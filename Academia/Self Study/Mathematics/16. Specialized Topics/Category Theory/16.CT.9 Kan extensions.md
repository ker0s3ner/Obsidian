## Definition

Given functors $F : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{E}$ and $K : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$, the **left Kan extension** $\text{Lan}_K F : \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{E}$ is the best approximation to a functor extending $F$ along $K$, defined by the universal property:

$$\text{Nat}(\text{Lan}_K F, G) \cong \text{Nat}(F, GK)$$

for any $G : \mathcal{D} \to \mathcal{E}$. The **right Kan extension** $\text{Ran}_K F$ is defined dually.

---

## Existence via Colimits

When $\mathcal{E}$ has enough colimits, the left Kan extension can be computed as:

$$(\text{Lan}_K F)(d) = \text{colim}_{(c, K(c) \to d)} F(c)$$

a colimit over the comma category — this generalizes the idea of "extending a function by its closest approximating colimit."

> [!tip] Slogan
> "All concepts are Kan extensions" — limits, colimits, and adjoint functors can all be phrased as special cases.
