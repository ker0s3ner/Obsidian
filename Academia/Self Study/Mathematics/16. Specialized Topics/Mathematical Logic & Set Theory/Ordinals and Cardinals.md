## Ordinals

An **ordinal** is a transitive set well-ordered by $\in$. Ordinals extend the natural numbers into the transfinite:

$$0, 1, 2, \ldots, \omega, \omega+1, \ldots, \omega \cdot 2, \ldots, \omega^2, \ldots$$

Every well-ordered set is order-isomorphic to a unique ordinal.

---

## Cardinals

A **cardinal** is an ordinal not in bijection with any smaller ordinal — it measures "size" rather than "order":

$$\aleph_0 < \aleph_1 < \aleph_2 < \cdots$$

$\aleph_0 = |\mathbb{N}|$; $2^{\aleph_0} = |\mathbb{R}|$ (the continuum).

> [!note] Ordinals vs. cardinals
> $\omega$ and $\omega+1$ are different ordinals but the same cardinal ($\aleph_0$) — cardinality forgets order, ordinals remember it.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $\omega + 1 \neq \omega$ as ordinals but $|\omega+1| = |\omega|$ as cardinals.
2. Explain why every finite ordinal equals its own cardinality.
3. Order the ordinals $\omega \cdot 2$, $\omega + 3$, $2 \cdot \omega$ from smallest to largest.
