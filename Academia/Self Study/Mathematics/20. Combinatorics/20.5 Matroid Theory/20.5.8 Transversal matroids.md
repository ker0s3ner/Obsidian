## Definition

Given a bipartite graph with parts $E$ and $S$, a subset $I \subseteq E$ is independent in the **transversal matroid** if $I$ can be matched into $S$ — i.e. there's a matching saturating $I$.

---

## Relation to Hall's Theorem

By **Hall's marriage theorem**, $I$ is independent iff every subset $I' \subseteq I$ satisfies $|N(I')| \geq |I'|$, where $N(I')$ is the neighborhood in $S$ — this is exactly the matroid exchange property, verified via Hall's condition.

> [!example] Not all matroids are transversal
> Transversal matroids are always representable (over any sufficiently large field) and are a proper subclass of representable matroids — e.g. the uniform matroid $U_{2,4}$ is representable but requires care to realize as transversal.

---

## Rank Function

$$r(I) = \min_{J \subseteq I} \left(|I \setminus J| + |N(J)|\right)$$

a matroid-theoretic reformulation of the deficiency version of Hall's theorem.
