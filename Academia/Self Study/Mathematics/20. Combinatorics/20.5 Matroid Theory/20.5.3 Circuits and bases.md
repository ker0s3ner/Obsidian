## Bases

A **basis** is a maximal independent set. By the exchange axiom (see [[20.5.1 Matroid axioms]]), all bases have the same size, equal to $r(E)$.

## Circuits

A **circuit** is a minimal dependent set — a set not in $\mathcal{I}$ all of whose proper subsets are in $\mathcal{I}$.

**Example — graphic matroid**: bases are spanning forests/trees, circuits are the cycles of the graph.

---

## Circuit Axioms (Alternative Definition)

A matroid can equivalently be defined by its collection of circuits $\mathcal{C}$, satisfying:

1. No circuit properly contains another.
2. **Circuit elimination**: if $C_1 \neq C_2 \in \mathcal{C}$ and $e \in C_1 \cap C_2$, there is a circuit $C_3 \subseteq (C_1\cup C_2)\setminus\{e\}$.

> [!note] Multiple equivalent axiomatizations
> Matroids can be defined via independent sets, bases, circuits, or rank function — a hallmark of a mature theory, each capturing the same combinatorial structure from a different angle.

## Practice Problems

1. List the circuits of the graphic matroid of $K_4$.
2. Verify the circuit elimination axiom for two triangles sharing an edge in $K_4$.
3. Show that every circuit has size $\leq r(E)+1$.
4. Explain how to recover the independent sets from the list of circuits alone.
