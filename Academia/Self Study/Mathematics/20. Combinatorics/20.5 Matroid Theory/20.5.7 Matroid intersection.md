## The Problem

Given two matroids $M_1, M_2$ on the same ground set $E$, find the largest set independent in **both**.

---

## Matroid Intersection Theorem

> [!example] Matroid Intersection Theorem (Edmonds)
> $$\max_{I \in \mathcal{I}_1 \cap \mathcal{I}_2} |I| = \min_{X\subseteq E} \big(r_1(X) + r_2(E\setminus X)\big)$$

This min-max formula is a matroid generalization of König's theorem (bipartite matching = vertex cover) and can be computed by a polynomial-time algorithm.

---

## Application: Bipartite Matching

Bipartite graph matching is exactly the intersection of two **partition matroids** (one enforcing "at most one edge per left vertex," the other "at most one edge per right vertex") — recovering König's theorem as a special case.

> [!note] Three matroids is hard
> Intersection of **three** matroids is NP-hard in general (it captures problems like Hamiltonian cycle existence) — the polynomial-time tractability is special to exactly two matroids.
