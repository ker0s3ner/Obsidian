## Definition

For a matroid $M=(E,\mathcal{I})$ with rank function $r$:

$$T_M(x,y) = \sum_{S \subseteq E} (x-1)^{r(E)-r(S)}(y-1)^{|S|-r(S)}$$

a two-variable polynomial encoding an enormous amount of structural information about $M$.

---

## Specializations

| $(x,y)$ | Recovers |
|---------|----------|
| $(1,1)$ | Number of bases |
| $(2,1)$ | Number of independent sets |
| $(1,2)$ | Number of spanning sets |
| $(2,2)$ | $2^{|E|}$ |

For **graphic matroids**, $T_M(x,y)$ specializes to the **chromatic polynomial** and the **reliability polynomial** of the graph along specific curves — one polynomial governing many combinatorial quantities.

> [!tip] Duality and the Tutte polynomial
> $T_{M^*}(x,y) = T_M(y,x)$ — matroid duality simply swaps the two variables, a clean reflection of [[20.5.2 Duality]] at the polynomial level.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $T_M(1,1)$ directly from the definition and confirm it counts bases.
2. Verify $T_{M^*}(x,y) = T_M(y,x)$ using the rank formula for the dual.
3. Look up how the chromatic polynomial of a graph arises as a specialization of its graphic matroid's Tutte polynomial.
4. Explain why the Tutte polynomial is considered a "universal" matroid invariant.
