## Representable Matroids

A matroid is **representable** (or **realizable**) over a field $\mathbb{F}$ if it is isomorphic to the linear (vector) matroid of some set of vectors in $\mathbb{F}^n$ — independence in the matroid matches linear independence of the corresponding vectors.

---

## Not All Matroids Are Representable

The **Vámos matroid** (8 elements, rank 4) is a classic example of a matroid that is not representable over **any** field — showing matroid theory is genuinely more general than linear algebra.

| Matroid class | Representable over |
|---|---|
| Graphic matroids | Every field (representable over $\mathbb{F}_2$ via incidence vectors, in fact all fields) |
| Uniform matroid $U_{2,n}$ ($n\geq 4$) | Not over $\mathbb{F}_2$ if $n > 3$ |
| Vámos matroid | No field |

> [!example] Fano matroid
> The Fano matroid (from the Fano projective plane, [[19.2.10 Finite projective planes]]) is representable over $\mathbb{F}_2$ but not over any field of characteristic $\neq 2$ — representability can be characteristic-dependent.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $U_{2,4}$ (any 2 of 4 elements independent) is not representable over $\mathbb{F}_2$.
2. Explain why graphic matroids are representable over every field.
3. Verify the Fano matroid's representability over $\mathbb{F}_2$ using the Fano plane's incidence vectors.
4. Discuss why the existence of non-representable matroids like Vámos justifies studying matroids abstractly, not just as vector configurations.
