## Dual Matroid

Given a matroid $M = (E, \mathcal{I})$ with rank function $r$, the **dual matroid** $M^*$ has the same ground set $E$, with independent sets:

$$I \in \mathcal{I}^* \iff E \setminus I \text{ contains a basis of } M$$

Equivalently, bases of $M^*$ are complements of bases of $M$.

---

## Rank Formula

$$r^*(X) = |X| - r(E) + r(E\setminus X)$$

**Example — graphic matroid**: for a connected planar graph $G$, the dual matroid $M(G)^*$ is (isomorphic to) the graphic matroid of the **planar dual graph** $G^*$ — matroid duality generalizes planar graph duality to non-planar and more abstract settings.

> [!tip] Double dual is the identity
> $(M^*)^* = M$ always — duality is an involution, exactly mirroring how planar dual-of-dual returns the original graph.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that bases of $M^*$ are complements of bases of $M$ using the rank formula.
2. Compute the dual of the graphic matroid of a triangle $K_3$.
3. Show that $(M^*)^* = M$ directly from the definition.
4. Explain the relationship between matroid duality and planar graph duality.
