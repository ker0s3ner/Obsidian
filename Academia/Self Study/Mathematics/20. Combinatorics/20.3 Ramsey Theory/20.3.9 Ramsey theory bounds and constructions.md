## Lower Bounds: Explicit vs. Probabilistic

The probabilistic lower bound $R(k,k) > 2^{k/2}$ from [[20.1.10 Probabilistic method in extremal combinatorics]] is not matched by any known **explicit** construction — the best known explicit constructions (e.g. via finite geometries or quasirandom graphs) give only exponentially weaker bounds, a long-standing gap in the field.

---

## Upper Bounds

The classical recursive bound $R(k,k) \leq \binom{2k-2}{k-1}$ (from $R(r,s) \le R(r-1,s)+R(r,s-1)$, [[20.3.1 Ramsey numbers]]) gives $R(k,k) = O(4^k)$. This has been improved only slightly over 70+ years — a 2023 breakthrough (Campos–Griffiths–Morris–Sahasrabudhe) achieved the first exponential improvement, $R(k,k) \le (4-\varepsilon)^k$.

> [!warning] The gap remains huge
> Even after decades of work, the best known bounds leave $R(k,k)$ trapped between roughly $2^{k/2}$ and $4^k$ — closing this gap is one of the most famous open problems in combinatorics.
