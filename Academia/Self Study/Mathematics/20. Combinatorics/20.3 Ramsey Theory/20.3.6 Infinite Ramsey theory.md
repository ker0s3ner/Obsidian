## Infinite Ramsey's Theorem

> [!example] Infinite Ramsey's Theorem
> For any coloring of the edges of the complete graph on a countably infinite vertex set $\mathbb{N}$ with finitely many colors, there is an **infinite** monochromatic complete subgraph.

Unlike the finite case, no explicit "threshold number" is needed — infinite Ramsey theory guarantees the monochromatic structure directly.

---

## Proof Sketch

Build an infinite sequence of vertices $v_1, v_2, \ldots$ by repeatedly restricting to an infinite subset on which $v_i$'s edges to all later vertices are a single color (possible since finitely many colors partition an infinite set into infinite pieces by pigeonhole) — a **diagonal/thinning** argument.

> [!tip] Compactness connects finite and infinite
> The infinite theorem can be used to *derive* the finite Ramsey theorem's existence (though not explicit bounds) via a compactness argument — infinite combinatorics often implies finite combinatorics for free, just without quantitative bounds.

2. Explain why the infinite case needs no explicit numerical bound, unlike $R(k,k)$.
