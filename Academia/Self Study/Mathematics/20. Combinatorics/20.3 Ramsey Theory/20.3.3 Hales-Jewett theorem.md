## Combinatorial Lines

In the $n$-dimensional cube $[t]^n$ (strings of length $n$ over an alphabet of $t$ symbols), a **combinatorial line** is a set of $t$ points obtained by fixing some coordinates and letting the rest vary together through $1,\ldots,t$ simultaneously.

---

## Statement

> [!example] Hales–Jewett Theorem (1963)
> For every $t$ and $r$, there exists $n$ such that any $r$-coloring of $[t]^n$ contains a monochromatic combinatorial line.

This is a purely combinatorial statement about high-dimensional combinatorial cubes, with **no arithmetic** built in — yet it implies van der Waerden's theorem as a corollary, by mapping combinatorial lines to arithmetic progressions via any injection $[t] \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}$.

> [!tip] "Arithmetic is a shadow of combinatorics"
> Hales–Jewett shows the phenomenon behind van der Waerden's theorem is not really about numbers at all — it's about combinatorial structure in high dimensions, and arithmetic progressions are just one instance of it.
