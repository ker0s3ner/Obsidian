## Statement

**Gallai's theorem** (also called the multidimensional van der Waerden theorem) extends van der Waerden's theorem from $\mathbb{Z}$ to $\mathbb{Z}^d$: for any finite subset $F \subset \mathbb{Z}^d$ and any finite coloring of $\mathbb{Z}^d$, there is a monochromatic homothetic copy of $F$ — that is, a set $a + \lambda F$ for some $a \in \mathbb{Z}^d$, $\lambda > 0$, monochromatic.

---

## Why This Generalizes Van der Waerden

A $k$-term arithmetic progression in $\mathbb{Z}$ is exactly a homothetic copy of $F = \{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}$. Gallai's theorem shows the same recurrence phenomenon holds for **any** finite pattern $F$, in any dimension — the shape doesn't matter, only that it's finite.

> [!note] Proof by induction on dimension
> Gallai's theorem is typically proved by inducting on the dimension $d$, using van der Waerden's theorem (dimension 1) as the base case and building up higher-dimensional monochromatic homothets from lower-dimensional ones.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that van der Waerden's theorem is the $d=1$ case of Gallai's theorem.
2. Describe what a homothetic copy of a triangle-shaped pattern $F \subset \mathbb{Z}^2$ looks like.
3. Sketch the inductive step reducing dimension $d$ to $d-1$.
4. Explain why Gallai's theorem is considered a "combinatorial" rather than "arithmetic" generalization.
