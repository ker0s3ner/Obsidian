## Definition

For a finite poset $P$ with a minimum element $\hat{0}$, the **Möbius function** $\mu: P \to \mathbb{Z}$ is defined recursively:

$$\mu(\hat{0}) = 1, \qquad \mu(x) = -\sum_{\hat{0} \leq y < x} \mu(y) \quad (x \neq \hat{0})$$

---

## Möbius Inversion on Posets

If $g(x) = \sum_{y \leq x} f(y)$, then:

$$f(x) = \sum_{y \leq x} \mu(y,x)\, g(y)$$

**Example — divisor lattice**: taking $P = (\mathbb{N}, |)$ recovers the classical number-theoretic Möbius function $\mu(n)$ and the classical Möbius inversion formula.

> [!example] Boolean lattice
> For $P = \mathcal{P}(\{1,\ldots,n\})$, $\mu(A,B) = (-1)^{|B \setminus A|}$ — this recovers inclusion–exclusion as a special case of Möbius inversion.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $\mu(n)$ for $n = 1,\ldots,12$ using the divisor-lattice recursion and check against the number-theoretic definition.
2. Verify $\mu(A,B) = (-1)^{|B\setminus A|}$ for a small Boolean lattice example.
3. Derive the inclusion–exclusion principle as a special case of Möbius inversion on the Boolean lattice.
4. Explain why $\mu$ depends only on the interval $[y,x]$'s isomorphism type, not on $P$ globally.
