## Zorn's Lemma

> [!example] Zorn's Lemma
> If every chain (totally ordered subset) in a nonempty poset $P$ has an upper bound in $P$, then $P$ has a maximal element.

---

## Equivalence with the Axiom of Choice

Zorn's lemma, the **Axiom of Choice**, and the **Well-Ordering theorem** (every set can be well-ordered) are all logically equivalent over ZF set theory — each can be derived from either of the others.

| Statement | Role |
|-----------|------|
| Axiom of Choice | Choice function exists for any family of nonempty sets |
| Zorn's Lemma | Chains bounded $\implies$ maximal element exists |
| Well-Ordering | Every set admits a well-order |

> [!tip] Typical use
> Zorn's lemma is the standard tool for proving existence of maximal objects without explicit construction — e.g. every vector space has a basis, every proper ideal is contained in a maximal ideal, every field has an algebraic closure.

## Practice Problems

1. Use Zorn's lemma to prove every vector space has a basis.
2. Show that Zorn's lemma implies every nonempty poset has a maximal chain (Hausdorff maximal principle).
3. Explain intuitively why the Axiom of Choice is needed to guarantee upper bounds for *infinite* chains.
4. State the well-ordering theorem and describe an (unintuitive) well-order on $\mathbb{R}$ that this equivalence guarantees exists.
