## Definition

A **Boolean algebra** is a lattice $(B, \vee, \wedge)$ that is:

- **Distributive**: $x\wedge(y\vee z) = (x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge z)$
- **Bounded**: has a minimum $0$ and maximum $1$
- **Complemented**: every $x$ has a unique $\bar{x}$ with $x\vee\bar{x}=1$, $x\wedge\bar{x}=0$

**Example**: $(\mathcal{P}(X), \cup, \cap, {}^c)$ — the power set with complement — is the archetypal Boolean algebra.

---

## Stone's Representation Theorem

> [!example] Stone Representation Theorem
> Every Boolean algebra is isomorphic to an algebra of clopen sets of some (Stone) topological space — every abstract Boolean algebra can be realized concretely as a "power-set-like" structure.

This is the order-theoretic ancestor of the propositional-logic idea that every Boolean algebra models classical propositional logic (join = OR, meet = AND, complement = NOT).

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the distributive law for $(\mathcal{P}(X), \cup, \cap)$.
2. Show that $(\mathbb{N}, \text{lcm}, \gcd)$ is a lattice but **not** a Boolean algebra (no complements in general).
3. Explain the correspondence between Boolean algebra operations and propositional logic connectives.
4. State Stone's representation theorem and give an intuitive reason it should be true for finite Boolean algebras.
