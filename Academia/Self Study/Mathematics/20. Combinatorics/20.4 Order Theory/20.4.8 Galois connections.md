## Definition

A **Galois connection** between posets $(A,\leq)$ and $(B,\leq)$ is a pair of order-reversing (or order-preserving, in the "monotone" variant) maps $f: A \to B$, $g: B \to A$ satisfying:

$$f(a) \leq b \iff a \leq g(b)$$

---

## Prototypical Example

For a field extension $L/K$, $f$ sends a subgroup $H$ of the Galois group to its fixed field $L^H$, and $g$ sends an intermediate field $F$ to $\text{Gal}(L/F)$ — the correspondence at the heart of [[15.3 Field Theory/15.3.2 Galois theory]] is exactly a Galois connection (which is where the general concept gets its name).

---

## Closure Operators

Composing $f$ then $g$ gives a **closure operator** $c = g\circ f$ on $A$: $a \leq c(a)$, $c(c(a))=c(a)$, and $c$ is monotone. Every closure operator arises this way from some Galois connection.

> [!note] Ubiquity
> Galois connections appear throughout mathematics: topological closure and interior, the ideal–variety correspondence in algebraic geometry, and the polar/dual correspondence in convex geometry are all instances of the same pattern.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the Galois connection axiom for the field-extension example with a specific subgroup and fixed field.
2. Show that $c = g \circ f$ is idempotent using only the defining inequality.
3. Identify the Galois connection underlying the variety–ideal correspondence in algebraic geometry.
4. Explain why Galois connections always relate an order-reversing pair, if you set them up via inclusion-reversing maps.
