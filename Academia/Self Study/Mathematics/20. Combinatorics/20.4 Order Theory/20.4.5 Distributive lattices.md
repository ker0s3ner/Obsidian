## Definition

A lattice is **distributive** if:

$$x \wedge (y \vee z) = (x \wedge y) \vee (x \wedge z) \quad \text{for all } x,y,z$$

(equivalently, the dual law with $\vee,\wedge$ swapped also holds automatically).

---

## Birkhoff's Representation Theorem

> [!example] Birkhoff's Theorem
> Every finite distributive lattice is isomorphic to the lattice of **down-sets** (order ideals) of some finite poset $P$, ordered by inclusion.

This gives a complete structural classification: distributive lattices are exactly "shadows" of ordinary posets, recovered via their down-set lattices.

> [!note] $M_3$ and $N_5$ as obstructions
> A lattice is distributive iff it contains no sublattice isomorphic to $M_3$ (the diamond) or $N_5$ (the pentagon) — a finite forbidden-substructure characterization, analogous to Kuratowski's theorem for planar graphs.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that the divisor lattice of $30$ is distributive.
2. Identify the poset $P$ whose down-set lattice gives the Boolean lattice $\mathcal{P}(\{1,2,3\})$.
3. Exhibit the pentagon lattice $N_5$ and confirm it is not distributive.
4. Explain why every distributive lattice is automatically modular (see [[20.4.6 Modular lattices]]).
