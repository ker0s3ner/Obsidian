## Statement

The maximum size of an **antichain** (no element contains another) in the Boolean lattice $\mathcal{P}(\{1,\ldots,n\})$ is:

$$\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor}$$

achieved by taking all subsets of the middle size $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

---

## Proof via LYM Inequality

The **Lubell–Yamamoto–Meshalkin (LYM) inequality** states that for any antichain $\mathcal{A}$:

$$\sum_{A \in \mathcal{A}} \frac{1}{\binom{n}{|A|}} \leq 1$$

Since each term is minimized when $|A|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ (the largest binomial coefficient), this immediately bounds $|\mathcal{A}| \leq \binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$.

> [!tip] Connection to Dilworth's theorem
> Sperner's theorem is a special case of the chain-decomposition idea in Dilworth's theorem (mentioned in [[20.4.1 Partial orders]]): the Boolean lattice decomposes into exactly $\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ symmetric chains, one of which any antichain can meet at most once.

1. Verify the LYM inequality directly for $n=3$ using the antichain of all 2-element subsets.
2. Show the middle-layer antichain achieves the bound $\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ exactly.
