## Definition

A **lattice** is a poset $(L, \leq)$ in which every pair of elements $x,y$ has a **join** $x \vee y$ (least upper bound) and a **meet** $x \wedge y$ (greatest lower bound).

---

## Algebraic Characterization

Equivalently, $(L,\vee,\wedge)$ satisfies for all $x,y,z$:

| Law | Statement |
|-----|-----------|
| Commutativity | $x\vee y = y\vee x$, $x\wedge y = y\wedge x$ |
| Associativity | $(x\vee y)\vee z = x\vee(y\vee z)$, similarly for $\wedge$ |
| Absorption | $x\vee(x\wedge y) = x$, $x\wedge(x\vee y)=x$ |

The order is recovered by $x \leq y \iff x \wedge y = x$.

> [!example] Standard examples
> - $(\mathcal{P}(X), \subseteq)$: join = union, meet = intersection
> - $(\mathbb{N}, |)$: join = lcm, meet = gcd
> - Subgroup lattice of a group: join = generated subgroup, meet = intersection

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the absorption law for $(\mathcal{P}(X), \cup, \cap)$.
2. Show that every finite lattice has a unique minimum and maximum element.
3. Draw the Hasse diagram of the divisor lattice of 30 and identify all joins/meets.
4. Explain why not every poset is a lattice — give a poset where some pair has no least upper bound.
