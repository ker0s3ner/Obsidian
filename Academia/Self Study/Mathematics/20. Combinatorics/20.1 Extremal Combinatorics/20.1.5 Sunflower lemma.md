## Sunflowers

A **sunflower** (or $\Delta$-system) with $p$ petals is a collection of sets $S_1,\ldots,S_p$ whose pairwise intersections are all equal to a common **core** $Y$ (possibly empty); the "petals" $S_i \setminus Y$ are pairwise disjoint.

---

## Erdős–Ko Sunflower Lemma

> [!example] Sunflower Lemma
> Any family of more than $k!(p-1)^k$ sets, each of size $\leq k$, contains a sunflower with $p$ petals.

The bound guarantees that once a family of bounded-size sets is large enough, it *must* contain this highly structured configuration — impossible to avoid by clever construction alone.

> [!note] The Sunflower Conjecture
> Erdős and Rado conjectured the true bound is $C_p^k$ for some constant $C_p$ — far smaller than $k!(p-1)^k$. A 2021 breakthrough (Alweiss–Lovett–Wu–Zhang) proved a bound of roughly $(\log k)^k$, close to but not resolving the conjecture.

## Practice Problems

1. Construct a sunflower with 3 petals from a small explicit family of 3-element sets.
2. Explain why bounding set size $\leq k$ is essential — unbounded sets can avoid sunflowers entirely.
3. State the sunflower conjecture and describe why it remains open.
4. Discuss why the sunflower lemma is a key tool in circuit complexity lower bounds.
