## Hybrid Extremal Problem

**Ramsey–Turán theory** asks Turán's question — maximum edges avoiding $K_{r+1}$ — under an *extra* Ramsey-type constraint: the graph must also have no large independent set.

$$RT(n, K_{r+1}, o(n)) = \max\{e(G) : |V(G)|=n,\ K_{r+1}\not\subseteq G,\ \alpha(G) = o(n)\}$$

where $\alpha(G)$ is the independence number.

---

## Contrast with Ordinary Turán Numbers

For $K_3$: the ordinary Turán number is $\lfloor n^2/4 \rfloor$ (achieved by a bipartite graph, which has a huge independent set of size $n/2$). Forbidding large independent sets forces a completely different, sparser extremal graph — showing the independence constraint genuinely changes the answer.

> [!note] Szemerédi's contribution
> Szemerédi (using an early form of the regularity method) determined $RT(n, K_4, o(n)) = n^2/8 + o(n^2)$ — one of the original motivations for developing [[20.1.2 Szemerédi regularity]].

## Practice Problems

1. Explain why the ordinary Turán-extremal graph for $K_3$ fails the small-independent-set constraint.
2. Compare $RT(n, K_3, o(n))$ to the ordinary Turán number $ex(n, K_3)$.
3. Discuss why the regularity lemma was originally developed to attack this class of problems.
4. Give an intuitive reason why forbidding large independent sets should decrease the maximum edge count.
