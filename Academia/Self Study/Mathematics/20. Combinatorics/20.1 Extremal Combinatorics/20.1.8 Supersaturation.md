## Beyond the Extremal Threshold

Turán-type theorems say a forbidden substructure (e.g. $K_{r+1}$) can be avoided up to a certain edge density. **Supersaturation** asks: once you exceed that threshold, how *many* copies of the forbidden structure are forced?

---

## Erdős–Simonovits Supersaturation Theorem

> [!example] Supersaturation
> For any $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists $\delta > 0$ such that any graph on $n$ vertices with at least $(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\text{ex}(n,H)$ edges contains at least $\delta n^{|V(H)|}$ copies of $H$.

Just past the Turán threshold, forbidden copies don't merely appear — they proliferate at a rate proportional to the total possible count.

---

## Application: Removal Lemmas

Supersaturation is the engine behind **removal lemmas** (e.g. the triangle removal lemma from [[20.1.2 Szemerédi regularity]]): if a graph has few copies of $H$, removing few edges can eliminate them all — because many copies would otherwise be forced by supersaturation.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain intuitively why exceeding the Turán threshold by a fixed fraction forces a positive fraction of all possible copies.
2. State the triangle removal lemma and explain its link to supersaturation.
3. For triangles ($H = K_3$), describe what supersaturation implies just above $ex(n, K_3) = \lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$.
4. Discuss why supersaturation constants often depend on the regularity lemma's tower-type bounds.
