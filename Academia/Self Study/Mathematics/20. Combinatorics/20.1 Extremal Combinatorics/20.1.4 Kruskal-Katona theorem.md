## Shadows

The **shadow** $\partial\mathcal{F}$ of a family $\mathcal{F}$ of $k$-sets is the family of all $(k-1)$-subsets contained in some member of $\mathcal{F}$. The Kruskal–Katona theorem bounds how small a shadow can be, given $|\mathcal{F}|$.

---

## Statement

Write $|\mathcal{F}| = \binom{a_k}{k} + \binom{a_{k-1}}{k-1} + \cdots$ in its unique **cascade representation** ($a_k > a_{k-1} > \cdots \geq 0$). Then:

$$|\partial \mathcal{F}| \geq \binom{a_k}{k-1} + \binom{a_{k-1}}{k-2} + \cdots$$

Equality holds for families of the form "all $k$-subsets of the first $m$ elements, in a canonical order" (**colex-initial segments**).

> [!tip] Colex order does the heavy lifting
> The extremal families are always initial segments in the **colexicographic order** on $k$-sets — a purely combinatorial ordering that makes the shadow-minimization problem tractable.

1. Compute the cascade representation of $|\mathcal{F}| = 10$ for $k = 3$.
2. Verify the shadow bound for the colex-initial segment of size 10 with $k=3$.
3. Explain why minimizing the shadow is equivalent to a compression argument.
4. Relate Kruskal–Katona to bounding the size of antichains in the Boolean lattice.
