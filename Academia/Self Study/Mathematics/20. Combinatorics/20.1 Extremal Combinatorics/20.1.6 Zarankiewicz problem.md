## The Problem

$z(m,n;s,t)$ is the maximum number of 1's in an $m \times n$ 0-1 matrix containing no all-1's $s \times t$ submatrix — equivalently, the maximum edges in a bipartite graph with parts of size $m,n$ avoiding $K_{s,t}$.

---

## Kővári–Sós–Turán Bound

For the symmetric case $z(n,n;2,2)$ (no $K_{2,2}$, i.e. no 4-cycle):

$$z(n,n;2,2) \leq \frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{4n-3}\right)n = O(n^{3/2})$$

matching constructions from finite projective planes (see [[19.2.10 Finite projective planes]]) which achieve $\Theta(n^{3/2})$.

> [!example] Incidence graphs of projective planes
> The point–line incidence graph of a projective plane of order $q$ has no $K_{2,2}$ and achieves essentially the optimal edge count — a striking use of finite geometry to solve an extremal graph problem.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that avoiding $K_{2,2}$ is equivalent to: any two vertices on one side share at most one common neighbor.
2. Derive the Kővári–Sós–Turán bound using a double-counting argument on paths of length 2.
3. Explain how a projective plane's incidence structure avoids $K_{2,2}$.
4. What is known about $z(n,n;3,3)$, and how does the difficulty compare to the $s=t=2$ case?
