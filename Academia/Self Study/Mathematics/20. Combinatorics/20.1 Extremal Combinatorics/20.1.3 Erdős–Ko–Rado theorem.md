## Statement

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a family of $k$-element subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ that is **intersecting** (any two sets in $\mathcal{F}$ share an element). If $n \geq 2k$:

$$|\mathcal{F}| \leq \binom{n-1}{k-1}$$

with equality achieved by fixing one element and taking all $k$-sets containing it (a **star**).

---

## Why the Threshold $n \geq 2k$

When $n < 2k$, any two $k$-subsets of an $n$-set automatically intersect (pigeonhole), so the intersecting condition is vacuous and the whole family $\binom{n}{k}$ is achievable — the interesting bound only kicks in once disjoint $k$-sets are possible.

> [!example] Proof via cyclic permutations (Katona's proof)
> Arrange $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ in a circle. Each cyclic rotation admits at most $k$ pairwise-intersecting arcs of length $k$ from $\mathcal{F}$; averaging over all rotations recovers the bound.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the bound is tight by counting the star family's size directly.
2. Show the theorem is false (or vacuous) when $n < 2k$.
3. Sketch Katona's cyclic-permutation proof in your own words.
4. Compare the EKR bound to the total number of $k$-subsets $\binom{n}{k}$ as $n \to \infty$.
