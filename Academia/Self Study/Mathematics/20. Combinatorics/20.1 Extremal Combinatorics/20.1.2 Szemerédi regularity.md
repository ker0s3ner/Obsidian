## Regularity

A pair of disjoint vertex sets $(A,B)$ in a graph is **$\varepsilon$-regular** if for every $A' \subseteq A$, $B' \subseteq B$ with $|A'| \geq \varepsilon|A|$, $|B'| \geq \varepsilon|B|$:

$$|d(A',B') - d(A,B)| < \varepsilon$$

where $d(X,Y)$ is edge density between $X$ and $Y$. Regularity means edges are "spread evenly" — no dense or sparse sub-pocket.

---

## Szemerédi's Regularity Lemma

> [!example] Regularity Lemma (1975)
> For every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is $M(\varepsilon)$ such that the vertices of **any** graph can be partitioned into at most $M(\varepsilon)$ parts of nearly equal size, all but an $\varepsilon$-fraction of pairs of parts being $\varepsilon$-regular.

This is a structure theorem for *arbitrary* graphs — no matter how complicated, the graph looks like a "blow-up" of a small, well-behaved model (the **reduced graph**).

> [!tip] Why it matters
> The lemma is the key tool behind the graph-theoretic proof of Szemerédi's theorem on arithmetic progressions and behind the **triangle removal lemma**, which underlies much of extremal and additive combinatorics.
