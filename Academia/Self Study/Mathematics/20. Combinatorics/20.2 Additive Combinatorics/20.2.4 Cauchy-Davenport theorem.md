## Statement

For nonempty $A, B \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ with $p$ prime:

$$|A+B| \geq \min(p, |A|+|B|-1)$$

The same bound as the integers ([[20.2.1 Sumsets]]), now holding in a finite cyclic group of **prime** order.

---

## Why Primality Is Essential

The theorem fails for composite modulus: in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ with $n$ composite, take $A=B=$ the subgroup of index $d | n$; then $A+B=A$, so $|A+B|=|A|$, violating the bound whenever $2|A|-1 > |A|$.

> [!example] Combinatorial Nullstellensatz proof
> A slick modern proof uses the **Combinatorial Nullstellensatz** (Alon): consider the polynomial $\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)$ over a well-chosen set of variables and extract a nonzero coefficient, forcing the sumset to be large.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the theorem for $A=B=\{0,1\}$ in $\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Construct a counterexample in $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ using a proper subgroup.
3. Sketch how the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz gives a proof of Cauchy–Davenport.
4. Compare this bound to the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv theorem on zero-sum sequences.
