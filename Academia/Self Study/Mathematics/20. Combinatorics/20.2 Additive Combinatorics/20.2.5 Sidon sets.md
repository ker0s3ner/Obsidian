## Definition

A **Sidon set** $A$ is a set of integers where all pairwise sums $a+b$ ($a \leq b \in A$) are distinct — equivalently, all differences are distinct. Sidon sets are the "most spread out" sets: no additive structure at all.

---

## Maximum Size

A Sidon set in $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ has size at most $\sqrt{n} + O(n^{1/4})$, and this is essentially tight — constructions using finite fields (e.g. Singer's construction) achieve $\sqrt{n} - O(n^{0.5-\delta})$.

> [!note] Contrast with small doubling
> Sidon sets have **maximal** doubling: $|A+A| = \binom{|A|+1}{2}$, the largest possible — the exact opposite extreme from the small-doubling sets governed by [[20.2.2 Freiman's theorem]].

---

## Application: Perfect Difference Sets

Sidon sets give optimal **radar/synchronization codes** and are used to construct sparse rulers — physical rulers with the fewest marks that can still measure every integer distance up to their length.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that $\{1, 2, 5, 11\}$ is a Sidon set by checking all pairwise sums are distinct.
2. Show that a Sidon set of size $k$ has at most $\binom{k}{2}+k$ distinct sums, and that this bound is achieved.
3. Explain why maximal doubling and Freiman-type structure are opposite extremes.
4. Research Singer's construction of Sidon sets using finite fields.
