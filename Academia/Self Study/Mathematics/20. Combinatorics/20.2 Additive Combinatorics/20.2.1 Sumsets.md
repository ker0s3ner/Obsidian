## Definition

For subsets $A, B$ of an abelian group:

$$A + B = \{a+b : a \in A, b \in B\}$$

**Additive combinatorics** studies how the size and structure of $A+B$ relate to those of $A$ and $B$.

---

## Basic Bounds

For finite $A, B \subset \mathbb{Z}$:

$$|A|+|B|-1 \leq |A+B| \leq |A||B|$$

The lower bound is achieved when $A, B$ are arithmetic progressions with the same common difference; the upper bound when sums rarely coincide.

> [!example] Structure from small sumsets
> If $|A+A|$ is close to $|A|$ (small **doubling**), $A$ must be close to an arithmetic progression — this is the heuristic behind [[20.2.2 Freiman's theorem]].

---

## Doubling Constant

$$K = \frac{|A+A|}{|A|}$$

Small $K$ signals additive structure; large $K$ signals "randomness."

---

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the lower bound $|A+B| \geq |A|+|B|-1$ for two arithmetic progressions with equal common difference.
2. Compute $|A+A|$ for $A = \{0, 1, 3, 7\}$ and its doubling constant.
3. Construct a set with doubling constant close to $|A|$ (near the upper bound).
4. Explain intuitively why small doubling should imply structure.
