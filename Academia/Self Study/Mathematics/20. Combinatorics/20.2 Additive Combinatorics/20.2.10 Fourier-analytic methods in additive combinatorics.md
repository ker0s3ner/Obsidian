## The Discrete Fourier Transform

For $A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$, the Fourier coefficients of its indicator function are:

$$\hat{1_A}(\xi) = \sum_{x \in \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}} 1_A(x)\, e^{-2\pi i x\xi/N}$$

The size of $\hat{1_A}(\xi)$ for $\xi \neq 0$ measures how far $A$ is from behaving like a random set of the same density.

---

## Detecting Additive Structure

If $A$ is **Fourier-uniform** (all nonzero $\hat{1_A}(\xi)$ small), it behaves pseudorandomly, and the count of 3-APs in $A$ is close to what you'd expect in a random set of the same density. If some $\hat{1_A}(\xi)$ is large, $A$ correlates with an arithmetic progression — this dichotomy is the engine of Roth's theorem ([[20.2.6 Roth's theorem]]).

> [!warning] Limits of linear Fourier analysis
> Ordinary Fourier analysis only detects correlation with *linear* phases $e^{2\pi i x\xi/N}$ — it is powerless against 4-term (and longer) progressions, which require **higher-order Fourier analysis** (Gowers norms) to handle.
