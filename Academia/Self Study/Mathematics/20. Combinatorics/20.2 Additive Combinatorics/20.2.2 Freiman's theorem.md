## Statement

If $A \subset \mathbb{Z}$ is finite with bounded doubling, $|A+A| \leq K|A|$, then $A$ is contained in a **generalized arithmetic progression** (GAP) of bounded dimension and size:

$$A \subseteq \{x_0 + n_1 x_1 + \cdots + n_d x_d : 0 \le n_i < N_i\}$$

with $d$ and $\prod N_i$ bounded purely in terms of $K$ (independent of $|A|$).

---

## Why This Is Remarkable

Freiman's theorem converts a purely quantitative hypothesis (small sumset) into strong **qualitative structure** (an explicit multi-dimensional progression) — a template for much of modern additive combinatorics: "small doubling $\implies$ algebraic structure."

> [!note] Effective bounds
> The original bounds on $d$ and the $N_i$ in terms of $K$ were extremely weak; a long sequence of improvements (Ruzsa, and more recently a nearly-optimal polynomial bound by Green–Ruzsa and then Schoen) tightened them substantially.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that an arithmetic progression itself has doubling constant $K \approx 2$.
2. Explain in your own words why bounded doubling should force $A$ into a low-dimensional GAP.
3. Give an example of a set with large doubling that is genuinely unstructured (e.g. a random set).
4. Discuss why the *dimension* $d$ of the GAP, not just its size, is the crucial structural parameter.
