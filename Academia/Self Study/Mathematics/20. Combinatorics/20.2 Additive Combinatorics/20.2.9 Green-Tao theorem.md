## Statement

> [!example] Green–Tao Theorem (2004)
> The primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions.

This cannot follow directly from Szemerédi's theorem ([[20.2.7 Szemerédi's theorem on APs]]), because the primes have density $0$ in $\mathbb{N}$ (by the prime number theorem, roughly $N/\log N$ primes below $N$) — Szemerédi's theorem requires *positive* density.

---

## The Transference Principle

Green and Tao's key innovation is a **transference principle**: they show a relative version of Szemerédi's theorem holds inside any sufficiently "pseudorandom" set (one without unusual correlations), and then prove the primes can be majorized by such a pseudorandom set (via Goldston–Yıldırım-type estimates), effectively "transferring" the density-based theorem to a sparse setting.

> [!tip] A recipe, not just a result
> This transference technique has since been reused to find other patterns in the primes and in different sparse sets — the primes example was the first, most famous application of a much more general method.
