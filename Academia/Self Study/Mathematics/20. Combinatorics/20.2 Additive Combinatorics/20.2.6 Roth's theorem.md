## Statement

Any subset $A \subseteq \{1,\ldots,N\}$ with

$$|A| \geq \frac{CN}{\log\log N}$$

(for a suitable constant $C$) contains a nontrivial 3-term arithmetic progression $a, a+d, a+2d$ with $d \neq 0$.

---

## Fourier-Analytic Proof

Roth's original proof (1953) uses the **circle method**: if $A$ has no 3-APs, its Fourier coefficients $\hat{1_A}(\xi)$ must all be small, which forces a density increment on a sub-progression — iterating this increment argument a bounded number of times gives a contradiction once density is high enough.

> [!tip] Density increment strategy
> This "either find structure, or gain density and recurse" strategy — used here and refined in the proof of [[20.2.7 Szemerédi's theorem on APs]] — is one of the most important proof templates in additive combinatorics.

---

## Improvements

The bound on $|A|$ needed has been improved dramatically since 1953; a 2020 breakthrough (Bloom–Sisask) pushed it to $N/(\log N)^{1+c}$, closer to the conjectured true threshold related to Behrend's construction of large 3-AP-free sets.

1. Explain the role of Fourier coefficients in detecting arithmetic progressions.
2. Describe, at a high level, the "density increment" argument.
3. Look up Behrend's construction and state the size of the largest known 3-AP-free subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$.
