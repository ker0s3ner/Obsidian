## Statement

For every $k \geq 3$ and $\delta > 0$, any subset $A \subseteq \{1,\ldots,N\}$ with $|A| \geq \delta N$ contains a $k$-term arithmetic progression, provided $N$ is large enough (depending on $k, \delta$).

This generalizes Roth's theorem ([[20.2.6 Roth's theorem]], the case $k=3$) to arithmetic progressions of **any** length.

---

## Multiple Proofs, Multiple Fields

Szemerédi's original 1975 proof was a purely combinatorial tour de force, and it is where [[20.1.2 Szemerédi regularity]] was invented as a lemma. Since then, three independent proofs have emerged:

| Approach | Key idea |
|----------|----------|
| Combinatorial (Szemerédi) | Regularity lemma + graph counting |
| Ergodic (Furstenberg, 1977) | Multiple recurrence in dynamical systems |
| Fourier/hypergraph (Gowers, 2001) | Higher-order Fourier analysis, hypergraph regularity |

> [!example] Furstenberg correspondence
> Furstenberg's proof reformulates the density statement as a statement about **recurrence** in measure-preserving dynamical systems — an unexpected bridge between combinatorics and ergodic theory.

## Practice Problems

1. State Roth's theorem as the $k=3$ special case of Szemerédi's theorem.
2. Explain informally what "multiple recurrence" means in Furstenberg's ergodic reformulation.
3. Why does proving the theorem for larger $k$ require progressively higher-order tools (e.g. hypergraph regularity)?
4. Discuss the significance of the Green–Tao theorem as an extension of Szemerédi's theorem to the primes.
