## Statement

If $|A+B| \leq K|A|$, then for all positive integers $m,n$:

$$|nB - mB| \leq K^{m+n}|A|$$

where $nB$ denotes the $n$-fold sumset $B + \cdots + B$. In particular:

$$|A+A| \leq K|A| \implies |kA| \leq K^k |A|$$

so bounded doubling propagates, with controlled degradation, to all iterated sumsets and difference sets.

---

## Why Iteration Matters

Many arguments in additive combinatorics need control not just on $A+A$ but on $A+A+A$, $A-A$, etc. Plünnecke–Ruzsa guarantees that if the *first* sumset is small, none of these iterated sums or differences can blow up uncontrollably.

> [!tip] Graph-theoretic proof
> Plünnecke's original proof uses **commutative graphs** (layered bipartite-like graphs modeling repeated addition) and a clever counting argument — Ruzsa later gave a purely combinatorial (Plünnecke-free) proof via "Ruzsa triangle inequality."

## Practice Problems

1. Derive $|A-A| \leq K^2|A|$ from the hypothesis $|A+A|\leq K|A|$.
2. State the Ruzsa triangle inequality $|A-C| \le \frac{|A-B||B-C|}{|B|}$ and use it to sketch a proof of the $m=n=1$ case.
3. Explain why controlling iterated sumsets is necessary for the proof of Freiman's theorem.
4. Compute an explicit bound for $|3A|$ given $|A+A| \le 2|A|$.
