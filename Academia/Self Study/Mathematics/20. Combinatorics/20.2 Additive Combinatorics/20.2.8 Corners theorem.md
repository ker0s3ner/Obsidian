## Statement

Any subset $A \subseteq [N]\times[N]$ with density $\delta > 0$ (i.e. $|A| \geq \delta N^2$) contains a **corner**:

$$(x,y), \quad (x+d, y), \quad (x, y+d) \qquad d \neq 0$$

provided $N$ is large enough depending on $\delta$.

---

## Two-Dimensional Analogue of Roth

The corners theorem is the 2D analogue of Roth's theorem on 3-APs ([[20.2.7 Szemerédi's theorem on APs]]) and is proved using essentially the same **triangle removal lemma** machinery from [[20.1.2 Szemerédi regularity]] — in fact, the corners theorem and the triangle removal lemma are equivalent.

> [!note] Ajtai–Szemerédi origins
> Ajtai and Szemerédi (1974) proved the corners theorem before Szemerédi's full arithmetic-progression theorem, and it was one of the motivations for developing removal-lemma techniques.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain why a corner is the natural 2D generalization of a 3-term arithmetic progression.
2. Sketch how the triangle removal lemma implies the corners theorem.
3. Give an example of a positive-density set in $[N]^2$ and locate a corner in it.
4. Discuss why proving quantitatively strong bounds for the corners theorem remains an active research area.
