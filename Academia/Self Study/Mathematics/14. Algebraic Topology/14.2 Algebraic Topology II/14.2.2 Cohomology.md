## Definition

**Cohomology** dualizes homology: given the chain complex $(C_n, \partial_n)$, form the **cochain complex** $C^n = \text{Hom}(C_n, G)$ with dual maps $\delta^n : C^n \to C^{n+1}$, and set:

$$H^n(X; G) = \ker(\delta^n) / \text{im}(\delta^{n-1})$$

---

## Universal Coefficient Theorem

Cohomology relates to homology (with $\mathbb{Z}$ coefficients) via:

$$0 \to \text{Hom}(H_n(X), G) \to H^n(X;G) \to \text{Ext}(H_{n-1}(X), G) \to 0$$

so when homology is free (no torsion), $H^n(X;G) \cong \text{Hom}(H_n(X), G)$ — cohomology is literally the dual vector space of homology.

---

## Why Bother With a Dual Theory?

> [!tip] Cohomology has a ring structure
> Unlike homology, cohomology groups $H^*(X)$ combine into a **graded ring** under the cup product (see [[14.2.7 Cup products and ring structure]]), giving far more structure to distinguish spaces than homology alone.
