## Simplicial/Singular Chains

Homology assigns abelian groups $H_n(X)$ to a space, built from chain complexes: $C_n(X)$ (free abelian group on $n$-simplices) with boundary maps $\partial_n : C_n \to C_{n-1}$ satisfying $\partial_{n-1}\partial_n = 0$.

$$H_n(X) = \ker(\partial_n) / \text{im}(\partial_{n+1})$$

Elements of $\ker \partial_n$ are **cycles**; elements of $\text{im}\,\partial_{n+1}$ are **boundaries**. Homology measures cycles that aren't boundaries — "holes."

## Basic Computations

| Space | $H_0$ | $H_1$ | $H_2$ |
|-------|-------|-------|-------|
| Point | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $S^1$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $0$ |
| $S^2$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $0$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ |
| $T^2$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $\mathbb{Z}^2$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ |

> [!tip] $H_0$ counts components
> $H_0(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}^k$ where $k$ is the number of path components — the simplest possible reading of homology.

---

## Homotopy Invariance

Homotopy equivalent spaces have isomorphic homology groups. Unlike homotopy groups $\pi_n$, homology groups are (relatively) easy to compute via cellular chain complexes on CW structures.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $H_0(X)$ for $X$ with three connected components.
2. Verify $H_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ matches $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ — explain why they agree here but generally differ.
3. Use a CW structure on $T^2$ to justify $H_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$.
4. Explain why $\partial_{n-1} \circ \partial_n = 0$ is essential for homology to be well-defined.
