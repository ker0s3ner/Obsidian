## The Sequence

For $X = U \cup V$ with $U, V$ open:

$$\cdots \to H_n(U \cap V) \to H_n(U) \oplus H_n(V) \to H_n(X) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_{n-1}(U \cap V) \to \cdots$$

This is the homological analogue of Van Kampen's theorem — decompose $X$ into pieces, compute their homology, and stitch the answer together.

---

## Worked Example: The Sphere

**Compute $H_n(S^2)$**, covering $S^2$ by two open hemispheres $U, V$ (each contractible), overlapping in an annulus $U \cap V \simeq S^1$:

$$H_n(U) = H_n(V) = 0 \ (n \geq 1), \qquad H_n(U \cap V) \cong H_n(S^1)$$

The Mayer–Vietoris sequence collapses to give $H_2(S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, $H_1(S^2) = 0$, matching the general formula for spheres.

## Practice Problems

1. Use Mayer–Vietoris to compute $H_n(S^n)$ inductively from $H_n(S^{n-1})$.
2. Verify $H_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ by covering the torus with two overlapping open sets.
3. Explain why the boundary map $\partial : H_n(X) \to H_{n-1}(U \cap V)$ is needed to make the sequence exact.
