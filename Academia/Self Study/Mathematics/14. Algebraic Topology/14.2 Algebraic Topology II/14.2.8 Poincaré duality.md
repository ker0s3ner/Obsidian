## Statement

For a closed, oriented $n$-manifold $M$:

$$H^k(M) \cong H_{n-k}(M) \quad \text{for all } k$$

Homology and cohomology in "complementary" dimensions are isomorphic — a deep symmetry unique to manifolds (general spaces have no such relation).

---

## Consequences

> [!example] Betti number symmetry
> Betti numbers satisfy $b_k = b_{n-k}$. For a surface $\Sigma_g$ ($n=2$): $b_0 = b_2 = 1$, and $b_1 = 2g$ — automatically symmetric around the middle.

Poincaré duality is why the cup product pairing $H^k(M) \times H^{n-k}(M) \to H^n(M) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is **nondegenerate** — the intersection form central to 4-manifold topology (see [[13.5.7 4-manifolds (intro)]]).

## Practice Problems

1. Verify Betti number symmetry $b_k = b_{n-k}$ for $S^2$.
2. Check $b_1 = 2g$ against Poincaré duality's symmetry for a genus-$g$ surface.
3. Explain why closedness and orientability are both necessary hypotheses (think about $\mathbb{R}^n$ or $\mathbb{R}P^2$ over $\mathbb{Z}$).
