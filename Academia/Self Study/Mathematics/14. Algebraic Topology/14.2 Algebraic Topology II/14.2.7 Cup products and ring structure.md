## The Cup Product

Cohomology classes can be multiplied: for $\alpha \in H^p(X)$, $\beta \in H^q(X)$, the **cup product** gives:

$$\alpha \smile \beta \in H^{p+q}(X)$$

making $H^*(X) = \bigoplus_n H^n(X)$ into a graded ring, with $\alpha \smile \beta = (-1)^{pq} \beta \smile \alpha$ (graded commutativity).

---

## Why It Distinguishes Spaces That Homology Cannot

> [!example] Torus vs. wedge of spheres
> $T^2$ and $S^1 \vee S^1 \vee S^2$ have isomorphic homology groups in every degree, but different cup product structures — in $T^2$, the two degree-1 generators cup together to give the degree-2 generator; in the wedge, all products of degree-1 classes vanish.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the graded commutativity formula for two degree-1 classes: $\alpha \smile \beta = -\beta \smile \alpha$.
2. Explain why $H^*(X)$ having a ring structure is strictly more information than the groups $H^n(X)$ alone.
3. Look up the cup product structure of $\mathbb{R}P^n$ with $\mathbb{Z}/2$ coefficients.
