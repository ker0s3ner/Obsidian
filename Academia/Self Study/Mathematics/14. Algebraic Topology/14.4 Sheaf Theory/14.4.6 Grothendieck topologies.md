## Generalizing "Open Cover"

A **Grothendieck topology** on a category $\mathcal{C}$ specifies, for each object $U$, which families of morphisms $\{U_i \to U\}$ count as "covers" — abstracting the covering-by-open-subsets idea to categories with no underlying topological space, like categories of schemes.

---

## Sites and Sheaves

A category with a Grothendieck topology is a **site**; sheaves on a site satisfy the usual gluing axioms with "cover" reinterpreted via the chosen topology. The étale topology on schemes (using étale morphisms as covers) is the motivating example for [[14.4.3 Étale cohomology]].

> [!tip] Same axioms, more general covers
> Nothing about sheaf gluing axioms actually requires open sets — only a well-behaved notion of "covering family," which a Grothendieck topology supplies abstractly.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain how the usual open-cover topology on a topological space is a special case of a Grothendieck topology.
2. Describe informally what makes the étale topology different from the Zariski topology on the same scheme.
3. Look up the axioms a Grothendieck topology must satisfy (stability under pullback, composition of covers, etc.).
