## Definition

Given a presheaf $\mathcal{F}$, its **sheafification** $\mathcal{F}^+$ is the "closest" sheaf approximating it: the unique sheaf with a universal map $\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F}^+$ inducing isomorphisms on all stalks.

$$(\mathcal{F}^+)_x \cong \mathcal{F}_x \quad \text{for all } x$$

---

## Construction

$\mathcal{F}^+(U)$ consists of functions assigning to each $x \in U$ a germ in $\mathcal{F}_x$, that are **locally** represented by an actual section of $\mathcal{F}$ — this repairs the gluing axiom while preserving stalks exactly.

> [!tip] Kernels and cokernels of sheaf maps
> Sheafification is why the kernel of a sheaf morphism is automatically a sheaf, but the naive "presheaf cokernel" is not — you must sheafify it to get the correct sheaf-theoretic cokernel.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain why stalks of $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{F}^+$ must agree, using the direct limit definition of stalks.
2. Show that if $\mathcal{F}$ is already a sheaf, $\mathcal{F}^+ \cong \mathcal{F}$.
3. Give an example of a presheaf whose naive quotient fails the sheaf axioms, requiring sheafification.
