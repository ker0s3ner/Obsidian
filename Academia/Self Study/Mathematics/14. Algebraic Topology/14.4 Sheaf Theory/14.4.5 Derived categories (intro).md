## Motivation

Sheaf cohomology is really the derived functor of global sections — organizing this systematically leads to the **derived category** $D(X)$, whose objects are chain complexes of sheaves, with morphisms that identify complexes with the same cohomology (quasi-isomorphisms) as isomorphisms.

---

## Why Work in the Derived Category

> [!tip] Complexes as the natural unit
> In $D(X)$, a single object can encode an entire resolution at once, and derived functors (like $R\Gamma$, giving sheaf cohomology) become ordinary functors on $D(X)$ rather than a separate construction for each sheaf.

This formalism underlies modern algebraic geometry (coherent sheaf cohomology), and Grothendieck's six-functor formalism for étale cohomology.
