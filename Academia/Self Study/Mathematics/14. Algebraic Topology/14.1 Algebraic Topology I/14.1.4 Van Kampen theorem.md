## Statement

Let $X = U \cup V$ with $U, V$ open, path-connected, $U \cap V$ path-connected and containing the basepoint. Then:

$$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(U) *_{\pi_1(U \cap V)} \pi_1(V)$$

the **amalgamated free product** — glue the two groups together, identifying the images of $\pi_1(U \cap V)$.

---

## Special Case: Trivial Intersection Group

If $U \cap V$ is simply connected, the formula reduces to a plain free product:

$$\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(U) * \pi_1(V)$$

> [!example] Wedge of circles
> $S^1 \vee S^1$ (figure eight): cover by two open sets each deformation-retracting to one circle, overlapping in a simply-connected neighborhood of the wedge point. Van Kampen gives $\pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}$, the free group on 2 generators.

---

## Why It's Powerful

Van Kampen's theorem lets you compute $\pi_1$ of a complicated space by decomposing it into simpler pieces whose fundamental groups (and their overlap) are already known — the standard tool for computing fundamental groups of CW complexes.

## Practice Problems

1. Use Van Kampen to compute $\pi_1(S^1 \vee S^1 \vee S^1)$.
2. Show that $\pi_1(S^n) = \{0\}$ for $n \geq 2$ using Van Kampen with two overlapping hemispheres.
3. Compute $\pi_1(T^2)$ using a CW decomposition and Van Kampen.
4. Explain why the theorem requires $U \cap V$ to be path-connected.
