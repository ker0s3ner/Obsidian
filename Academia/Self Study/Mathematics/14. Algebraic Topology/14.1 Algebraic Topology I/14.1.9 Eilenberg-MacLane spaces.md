## Definition

An **Eilenberg–MacLane space** $K(G,n)$ is a space with exactly one nonzero homotopy group:

$$\pi_n(K(G,n)) \cong G, \qquad \pi_k(K(G,n)) = 0 \text{ for } k \neq n$$

For $n \geq 2$, $G$ must be abelian (see [[14.1.5 Higher homotopy groups]]).

---

## Representing Cohomology

> [!tip] Cohomology classifying spaces
> $K(G,n)$ represents cohomology: $H^n(X; G) \cong [X, K(G,n)]$ — cohomology classes are exactly homotopy classes of maps into the right Eilenberg–MacLane space.

**Example**: $K(\mathbb{Z}, 1) \cong S^1$; $K(\mathbb{Z}/2, 1) \cong \mathbb{R}P^\infty$.
