## Definition

For $n \geq 1$, the $n$-th homotopy group is:

$$\pi_n(X, x_0) = [(S^n, \text{pt}), (X, x_0)]$$

homotopy classes of basepoint-preserving maps $S^n \to X$, with group operation from concatenation (generalizing loop concatenation for $\pi_1$).

$$\pi_n(X) \text{ is abelian for } n \geq 2$$

---

## Why $n \geq 2$ Forces Abelian

> [!tip] The Eckmann–Hilton argument
> For $n \geq 2$, the concatenation operation can be performed in two independent directions on the $n$-sphere (unlike $n=1$, where there's only one), and these two operations must agree and be commutative — a purely categorical argument, not specific to topology.

---

## Computing Higher Homotopy Groups Is Hard

Unlike $\pi_1$, higher homotopy groups are notoriously difficult:

> [!warning] $\pi_n(S^k)$ for $n > k$ is often nonzero
> $\pi_3(S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the **Hopf map** — spheres have "higher homotopy" long after you'd expect them to be contractible-looking.

---

## Long Exact Sequence of a Fibration

For a fibration $F \to E \to B$:

$$\cdots \to \pi_n(F) \to \pi_n(E) \to \pi_n(B) \to \pi_{n-1}(F) \to \cdots$$

the main computational tool for higher homotopy groups (see [[14.1.8 Fibrations and the long exact sequence]]).

## Practice Problems

1. Explain the Eckmann–Hilton argument for why $\pi_2(X)$ is abelian.
2. Show that a contractible space has all $\pi_n$ trivial.
3. Look up the Hopf map $S^3 \to S^2$ and explain why it generates $\pi_3(S^2)$.
4. Use the long exact sequence of the Hopf fibration $S^1 \to S^3 \to S^2$ to relate $\pi_n(S^3)$ and $\pi_n(S^2)$.
