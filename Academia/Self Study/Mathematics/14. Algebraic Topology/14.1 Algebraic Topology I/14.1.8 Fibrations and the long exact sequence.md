## Fibrations

A map $p : E \to B$ is a **fibration** if it satisfies the **homotopy lifting property**: any homotopy of maps into $B$ can be lifted to a homotopy of maps into $E$, given a lift at time 0. Write $F = p^{-1}(b_0)$ for the fiber.

---

## The Long Exact Sequence

Every fibration $F \to E \to B$ produces a long exact sequence of homotopy groups:

$$\cdots \to \pi_n(F) \to \pi_n(E) \to \pi_n(B) \xrightarrow{\partial} \pi_{n-1}(F) \to \cdots \to \pi_0(E) \to \pi_0(B) \to 0$$

> [!example] Hopf fibration
> $S^1 \to S^3 \to S^2$ gives, since $\pi_n(S^1) = 0$ for $n \geq 2$: $\pi_n(S^3) \cong \pi_n(S^2)$ for all $n \geq 3$ — a genuinely surprising identification.
