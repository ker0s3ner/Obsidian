## Bayes' Theorem for Parameters

Treat the parameter $\theta$ itself as random, with a **prior** distribution $\pi(\theta)$ encoding belief before seeing data. Given data $x$ with likelihood $f(x \mid \theta)$:

$$\pi(\theta \mid x) = \frac{f(x \mid \theta)\,\pi(\theta)}{\int f(x \mid \theta')\,\pi(\theta')\,d\theta'} \propto f(x \mid \theta)\,\pi(\theta)$$

$\pi(\theta \mid x)$ is the **posterior** — updated belief after seeing the data.

---

## Conjugate Priors

A prior is **conjugate** to a likelihood if the posterior belongs to the same family, making updates closed-form.

| Likelihood | Conjugate prior | Posterior |
|------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Binomial$(n,\theta)$ | Beta$(\alpha,\beta)$ | Beta$(\alpha + x, \beta + n - x)$ |
| Poisson$(\theta)$ | Gamma$(\alpha,\beta)$ | Gamma$(\alpha + \sum x_i, \beta + n)$ |
| Normal (known variance) | Normal | Normal |

**Example — coin flips**: prior $\theta \sim \text{Beta}(1,1)$ (uniform), observe $7$ heads in $10$ flips. Posterior is $\text{Beta}(8, 4)$, with posterior mean $8/12 = 2/3$ — pulled from the raw $0.7$ estimate toward the prior.

## Point and Interval Estimates

- **Posterior mean**: $\mathbb{E}[\theta \mid x]$ — a natural Bayesian point estimate
- **Maximum a posteriori (MAP)**: $\arg\max_\theta \pi(\theta \mid x)$
- **Credible interval**: an interval $[a,b]$ with $\int_a^b \pi(\theta \mid x)\,d\theta = 1-\alpha$

> [!warning] Credible interval ≠ confidence interval
> A 95% credible interval says "there's a 95% probability $\theta$ lies here, *given the data and prior*." A frequentist confidence interval makes no probability statement about $\theta$ at all — only about the procedure across repeated sampling. Don't conflate the two.

---

## Choice of Prior

- **Informative prior**: encodes real prior knowledge (e.g. from previous studies)
- **Uninformative/flat prior**: minimal influence, lets the data dominate
- **Jeffreys prior**: an invariant uninformative prior, $\pi(\theta) \propto \sqrt{I(\theta)}$, using the Fisher information $I(\theta)$

As sample size grows, the likelihood dominates the prior and the posterior concentrates around the true $\theta$ regardless of prior choice (for any prior with positive density there) — this is the Bayesian analogue of consistency.

1. Derive the posterior for a Binomial likelihood with a Beta$(\alpha,\beta)$ prior.
2. For a Normal likelihood with known variance $\sigma^2$ and Normal prior $N(\mu_0, \tau^2)$, find the posterior mean as a weighted average of the prior mean and sample mean.
3. Explain why the MAP estimate coincides with the MLE when the prior is flat.
