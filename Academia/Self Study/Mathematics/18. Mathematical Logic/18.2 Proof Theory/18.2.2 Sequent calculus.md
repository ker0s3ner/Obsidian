## Sequents

A **sequent** $\Gamma \vdash \Delta$ asserts that if every formula in $\Gamma$ holds, then at least one formula in $\Delta$ holds. Read: "the conjunction of $\Gamma$ implies the disjunction of $\Delta$."

---

## Structural and Logical Rules

Sequent calculus separates rules into:

- **Structural rules**: weakening (adding a formula to either side), contraction (removing a duplicate), exchange
- **Logical rules**: one introduction rule *per side* for each connective (left rules introduce into $\Gamma$, right rules into $\Delta$)

**Example — right rule for $\land$:**

$$\frac{\Gamma \vdash \Delta, \varphi \qquad \Gamma \vdash \Delta, \psi}{\Gamma \vdash \Delta, \varphi \land \psi}$$

---

## The Cut Rule

$$\frac{\Gamma \vdash \Delta, \varphi \qquad \varphi, \Gamma' \vdash \Delta'}{\Gamma, \Gamma' \vdash \Delta, \Delta'}$$

This "lemma-application" rule lets you use an intermediate formula $\varphi$ as a stepping stone. Whether it can always be eliminated is the subject of [[18.2.3 Cut elimination]].

> [!tip] Why sequents, not just formulas
> Working with sequents (multiple formulas on each side) makes the symmetry between assumptions and conclusions explicit, and makes rules like cut and the classical law of excluded middle fall out naturally from structural symmetry.
