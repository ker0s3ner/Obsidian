## Definition

A **Hilbert-style system** proves theorems from a small set of logical axiom schemas plus a single inference rule, modus ponens:

$$\frac{\varphi \qquad \varphi \to \psi}{\psi}$$

**Typical axiom schemas:**

$$\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi), \qquad (\varphi \to (\psi \to \chi)) \to ((\varphi \to \psi) \to (\varphi \to \chi))$$

---

## Trade-offs vs Natural Deduction

| System | Axioms | Rules | Proofs |
|--------|--------|-------|--------|
| Hilbert-style | Many schemas | Essentially just modus ponens | Compact to state, tedious to write |
| Natural deduction | None (or few) | Many introduction/elimination rules | Closer to informal reasoning |

Hilbert systems are minimal and easy to reason *about* metamathematically (e.g. proving the Deduction Theorem: $T \cup \{\varphi\} \vdash \psi \iff T \vdash \varphi \to \psi$), but writing actual proofs in them is unwieldy compared to [[18.2.1 Natural deduction]].

## Practice Problems

1. Prove $\varphi \to \varphi$ using only the two axiom schemas above and modus ponens.
2. State the Deduction Theorem and explain why it's not a primitive rule in Hilbert systems (unlike natural deduction's built-in discharge).
3. Compare the length of a Hilbert-style proof of $\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi)$ to its natural deduction counterpart.
