## Gentzen's Cut-Elimination Theorem

> [!example] Cut Elimination (Gentzen, 1935)
> Every sequent derivable using the cut rule is derivable **without** it. The cut rule is admissible but not necessary.

---

## Why This Matters

A cut-free proof has the **subformula property**: every formula appearing in the proof is a subformula of the conclusion. This means proof search in a cut-free system is far more constrained — you never need to "guess" an unrelated intermediate lemma, which is exactly what makes automated theorem proving and proof-theoretic consistency arguments tractable.

**Consequence — consistency of arithmetic fragments**: cut elimination is the engine behind Gentzen's proof of the consistency of Peano Arithmetic (relative to transfinite induction up to $\varepsilon_0$), since cut-free proofs of $\bot$ are syntactically impossible to construct.

---

## Proof Idea

Cut elimination proceeds by induction on the complexity of the cut formula $\varphi$ and the height of the derivation, repeatedly pushing cuts upward past logical rules until they disappear or reduce to cuts on simpler formulas.
