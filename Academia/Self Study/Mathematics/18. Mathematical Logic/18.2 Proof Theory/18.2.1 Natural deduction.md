## Definition

**Natural deduction** is a proof system built from **introduction** and **elimination** rules for each logical connective, designed to mirror informal mathematical reasoning.

---

## Core Rules

| Connective | Introduction | Elimination |
|------------|--------------|-------------|
| $\land$ | from $\varphi$, $\psi$ derive $\varphi \land \psi$ | from $\varphi \land \psi$ derive $\varphi$ (or $\psi$) |
| $\to$ | assume $\varphi$, derive $\psi$, conclude $\varphi \to \psi$ | from $\varphi$ and $\varphi \to \psi$ derive $\psi$ (modus ponens) |
| $\lor$ | from $\varphi$ derive $\varphi \lor \psi$ | case split: from $\varphi \lor \psi$, and derivations of $\chi$ from each disjunct, derive $\chi$ |
| $\neg$ | assume $\varphi$, derive $\bot$, conclude $\neg\varphi$ | from $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ derive $\bot$ |

---

## Discharging Assumptions

> [!example] $\to$-Introduction
> To prove $\varphi \to \psi$, temporarily **assume** $\varphi$, derive $\psi$ using that assumption, then "discharge" the assumption — the final proof of $\varphi \to \psi$ depends on nothing beyond the original open assumptions.

This bookkeeping of open/discharged assumptions is what distinguishes natural deduction from simpler axiomatic (Hilbert-style) systems.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Give a natural deduction proof of $\varphi \to (\psi \to \varphi)$.
2. Prove $(\varphi \to \psi) \to (\neg\psi \to \neg\varphi)$ using natural deduction rules.
3. Explain why $\to$-introduction requires "discharging" the assumption $\varphi$.
4. Derive $\varphi \lor \neg\varphi$ using proof by contradiction ($\neg$-elimination and $\bot$).
