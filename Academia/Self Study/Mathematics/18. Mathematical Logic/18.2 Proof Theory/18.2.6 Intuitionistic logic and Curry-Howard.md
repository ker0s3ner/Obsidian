## Intuitionistic Logic

**Intuitionistic logic** rejects the unrestricted law of excluded middle ($\varphi \lor \neg\varphi$) and proof by contradiction for existence claims — a proof of $\exists x\, \varphi(x)$ must *construct* a witness, not merely derive a contradiction from its absence.

This is exactly [[18.2.1 Natural deduction]] with the classical $\neg\neg$-elimination rule removed.

---

## Curry–Howard Correspondence

> [!example] Propositions as Types
> Intuitionistic proofs correspond exactly to typed programs:
>
> | Logic | Programming |
> |-------|-------------|
> | Proposition | Type |
> | Proof | Program (term) |
> | $\varphi \to \psi$ | Function type |
> | $\varphi \land \psi$ | Product type (pair) |
> | $\varphi \lor \psi$ | Sum type |
> | Proof normalization | Program evaluation |

A proof of $\varphi \to \psi$ literally *is* a function transforming evidence for $\varphi$ into evidence for $\psi$ — this correspondence underlies proof assistants like Coq and Agda.
