## Goal

**Ordinal analysis** measures the strength of a formal theory $T$ by finding the smallest ordinal $\alpha$ such that transfinite induction up to $\alpha$ proves $T$'s consistency — the theory's **proof-theoretic ordinal**.

---

## Gentzen's Result for Peano Arithmetic

> [!example] Gentzen (1936)
> Peano Arithmetic's proof-theoretic ordinal is $\varepsilon_0$ (the smallest ordinal with $\varepsilon_0 = \omega^{\varepsilon_0}$). Transfinite induction up to $\varepsilon_0$ proves PA's consistency, but PA itself cannot prove induction that far — this is exactly what Gödel's second incompleteness theorem forbids.

The proof uses [[18.2.3 Cut elimination]]: cut-free proofs of $\bot$ don't exist, and assigning ordinals to proof trees tracks how the elimination procedure terminates.

---

## Why This Is Useful

Ordinal analysis gives a fine-grained ruler for comparing theories' strength beyond simple "$T_1$ proves more than $T_2$" — two theories can be mutually incomparable in provable sentences yet comparable in proof-theoretic ordinal.
