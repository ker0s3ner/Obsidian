## Definitions

| Class | Definition |
|-------|-------------|
| $P$ | Decidable in polynomial time by a deterministic TM |
| $NP$ | Decidable in polynomial time by a **nondeterministic** TM (equivalently: solutions are polynomial-time *verifiable*) |

$$P \subseteq NP$$

Whether this containment is strict is the **P vs NP problem** — open, and one of the Clay Millennium Prize Problems.

---

## NP-Completeness

A problem is **NP-complete** if it's in $NP$ and every $NP$ problem reduces to it in polynomial time. Cook–Levin (1971): **SAT** (Boolean satisfiability) is NP-complete — the first, and archetypal, example.

> [!tip] Why NP-completeness matters practically
> If *any* NP-complete problem has a polynomial-time algorithm, then $P = NP$ and *every* NP problem does too. This is why thousands of practical problems (scheduling, routing, packing) are shown NP-complete rather than attacked directly — a fast algorithm for one would solve them all.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain the equivalence between "polynomial-time verifiable" and "decidable by nondeterministic polynomial-time TM."
2. Why does showing a new problem NP-complete (by reduction from SAT) tell you it's "as hard as" every NP problem?
3. Contrast $P$ vs $NP$ (a complexity question) with decidability (a computability question) — why is $P \subseteq NP \subseteq$ decidable but the reverse inclusions are separate questions?
