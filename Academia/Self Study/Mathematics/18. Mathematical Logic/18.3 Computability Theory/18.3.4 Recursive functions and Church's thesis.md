## Primitive Recursive Functions

Built from base functions (zero, successor, projections) closed under **composition** and **primitive recursion**:

$$f(x,0) = g(x), \qquad f(x,n+1) = h(x,n,f(x,n))$$

These capture "obviously terminating" computation — but not everything computable (Ackermann's function grows faster than any primitive recursive function).

---

## μ-Recursive Functions

Adding the **minimization operator** $\mu y[\,g(x,y)=0\,]$ (smallest $y$ making $g$ zero, undefined if none exists) gives the full class of **general recursive functions** — computations that may fail to halt.

---

## Church–Turing Thesis

> [!example] Church–Turing Thesis
> The following independently-developed notions of "computable" all coincide: Turing machines, $\mu$-recursive functions, Church's $\lambda$-calculus, and Post's production systems.

This isn't a theorem (there's no formal definition of "algorithm" to prove it against) but an empirical/philosophical claim, supported by every attempt at a rigorous alternative model of computation landing in the same class.
