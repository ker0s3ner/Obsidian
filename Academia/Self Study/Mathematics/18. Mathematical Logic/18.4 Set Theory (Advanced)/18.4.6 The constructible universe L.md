## Definition

Gödel's **constructible universe** $L$ is built in stages $L_0 = \emptyset$, $L_{\alpha+1} = \text{Def}(L_\alpha)$ (all subsets of $L_\alpha$ definable by a formula with parameters from $L_\alpha$), $L_\lambda = \bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda} L_\alpha$ at limits, and $L = \bigcup_\alpha L_\alpha$ over all ordinals.

---

## L Satisfies ZFC + GCH

> [!example] Gödel's Theorem (1938)
> $L$ is a model of ZFC, and moreover satisfies the **Generalized Continuum Hypothesis**. This proves $\text{Con(ZFC)} \implies \text{Con(ZFC + GCH)}$ — half of the independence result completed by Cohen's forcing (see [[18.4.2 Forcing]]), which supplied the other half.

---

## "The Minimal Model" Intuition

$L$ contains only sets that are explicitly *definable* from below — nothing is added "for free" via unrestrained Choice. This makes $L$ maximally simple and rigid: it satisfies strong combinatorial principles (like $\diamondsuit$) that fail in richer models, but it's also "too small" to contain large cardinals of high consistency strength — a tension explored throughout inner model theory.
