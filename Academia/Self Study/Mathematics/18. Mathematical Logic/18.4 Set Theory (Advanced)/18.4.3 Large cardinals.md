## Motivation

ZFC cannot prove its own consistency (Gödel's second incompleteness theorem). **Large cardinal axioms** posit the existence of sets so large they can't be constructed from below, giving a hierarchy of increasingly strong theories beyond ZFC.

---

## A Sample of the Hierarchy

| Cardinal | Rough idea |
|----------|-------------|
| Inaccessible | Cannot be reached by power sets or unions of smaller cardinals |
| Measurable | Admits a nontrivial $\{0,1\}$-valued measure on all its subsets |
| Supercompact | Extremely strong reflection properties |

Each level implies the consistency of ZFC plus all weaker large cardinal axioms — they form a linearly ordered hierarchy of "consistency strength."

> [!note] Why anyone cares
> Large cardinals aren't just set-theoretic curiosities: their existence settles many questions independent of ZFC (via forcing, see [[18.4.2 Forcing]]), including regularity properties of sets of reals studied in [[18.4.4 Descriptive set theory]].

## Practice Problems

1. Explain why "cannot be reached by power sets or unions of smaller cardinals" makes inaccessible cardinals hard to prove exist from ZFC alone.
2. What does it mean for one large cardinal axiom to have "greater consistency strength" than another?
3. Why can't ZFC prove the existence of an inaccessible cardinal (relate to Gödel's second incompleteness theorem)?
