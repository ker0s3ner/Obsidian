## Cardinal Operations

For infinite cardinals, addition and multiplication trivialize:

$$\kappa + \lambda = \kappa \cdot \lambda = \max(\kappa,\lambda)$$

but **exponentiation** $\kappa^\lambda$ is genuinely subtle — this is where the Continuum Hypothesis ($2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1$) lives.

---

## Cofinality and König's Theorem

The **cofinality** $\text{cf}(\kappa)$ of a cardinal is the least length of a sequence of smaller ordinals converging to it.

> [!example] König's Theorem
> $\kappa^{\text{cf}(\kappa)} > \kappa$ always — in particular $2^{\aleph_0} \neq \aleph_\omega$, since $\text{cf}(\aleph_\omega) = \omega$. This is essentially the **only** ZFC-provable restriction on the value of $2^{\aleph_0}$; Cohen's forcing shows almost anything else consistent.

---

## What ZFC Does and Doesn't Decide

ZFC determines $\kappa + \lambda$ and $\kappa \cdot \lambda$ completely, but leaves $2^\kappa$ almost entirely open beyond König's constraint — forcing (see [[18.4.2 Forcing]]) can push $2^{\aleph_0}$ to essentially any cardinal of the right cofinality.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $\aleph_0 + \aleph_1 = \aleph_1$ directly from a bijection argument.
2. Explain why $\text{cf}(\aleph_\omega) = \omega$ rules out $2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_\omega$ via König's theorem.
3. Contrast how much ZFC pins down $\kappa \cdot \lambda$ versus $2^\kappa$.
