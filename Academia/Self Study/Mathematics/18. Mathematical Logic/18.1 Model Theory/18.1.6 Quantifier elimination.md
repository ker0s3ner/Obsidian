## Definition

A theory $T$ **admits quantifier elimination** if every formula $\varphi(\bar x)$ is $T$-equivalent to a quantifier-free formula in the same free variables.

---

## Why It's Powerful

Quantifier-free formulas are decidable by direct computation — no search over possible witnesses is needed. Quantifier elimination is how several classical decidability results are proved:

| Theory | Quantifier elimination result |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| Real closed fields | Tarski: $(\mathbb R, +, \cdot, <)$ admits QE, giving decidability of real algebraic geometry |
| Dense linear orders (no endpoints) | Admits QE |
| Presburger arithmetic $(\mathbb Z, +, <)$ | Admits QE (though $(\mathbb Z,+,\cdot)$ does not — undecidable) |

---

## Example

**Real closed fields**: the statement $\exists x\, (ax^2+bx+c=0)$ is $T$-equivalent to the quantifier-free $b^2 - 4ac \geq 0$ (when $a \neq 0$) — the quadratic formula's discriminant condition *is* the eliminated quantifier.

> [!tip] Contrast with undecidable theories
> Peano Arithmetic does **not** admit quantifier elimination (and is undecidable) — quantifier elimination is a strong structural property, not automatic.
