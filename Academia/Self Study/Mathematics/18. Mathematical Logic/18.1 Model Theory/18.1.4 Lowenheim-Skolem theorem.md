## Statement

> [!example] Löwenheim–Skolem Theorem
> If a first-order theory $T$ has an infinite model, it has models of **every** infinite cardinality $\geq |\mathcal L|$.

Two directions are usually distinguished: **downward** (a model has an elementary substructure of any smaller infinite cardinality $\geq |\mathcal L|$) and **upward** (a model has elementary extensions of any larger cardinality, via [[18.1.3 Compactness theorem]]).

---

## Skolem's Paradox

> [!warning] A famous "paradox"
> ZFC has an infinite model, so by Löwenheim–Skolem it has a **countable** model — yet ZFC proves the existence of uncountable sets! The resolution: "uncountable" inside the countable model means "no bijection with $\omega$ *in that model*" — a relative, not absolute, notion.

---

## Consequence: No First-Order Categoricity for Infinite Structures

Since models of every infinite cardinality exist, no first-order theory with an infinite model can be **categorical** (have exactly one model up to isomorphism) — this is why $(\mathbb N, <)$ and $(\mathbb Z, <)$ can be elementarily equivalent without being isomorphic.
