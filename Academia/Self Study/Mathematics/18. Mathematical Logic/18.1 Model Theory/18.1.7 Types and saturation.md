## Types

A **type** $p(\bar x)$ over a structure $\mathcal M$ is a maximal consistent set of formulas (with parameters from $M$) — informally, a complete description of a *hypothetical* element, possibly not realized in $\mathcal M$ itself.

---

## Realizing Types

A type $p$ is **realized** in $\mathcal M$ if some $a \in M$ satisfies every formula in $p$; otherwise it's **omitted**.

**Example**: in $(\mathbb Q, <)$, the type "$x > n$ for every $n \in \mathbb N$" is consistent but omitted — no rational number is larger than every natural number.

---

## Saturation

$\mathcal M$ is **$\kappa$-saturated** if every type over a parameter set of size $< \kappa$ is realized in $\mathcal M$. Saturated models are rich enough that no "missing element" a type describes can escape them.

> [!tip] Why saturation matters
> Saturated models make abstract model-theoretic arguments concrete: instead of reasoning about types syntactically, you can pick an actual realizing element and argue directly — this is the backbone of stability theory.
