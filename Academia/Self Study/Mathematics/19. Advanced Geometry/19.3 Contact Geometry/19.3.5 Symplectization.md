## From Contact to Symplectic

Given a contact manifold $(M, \alpha)$ of dimension $2n+1$, the **symplectization** is $M \times \mathbb{R}$ (or $M \times \mathbb{R}_+$) with symplectic form:

$$\omega = d(e^t \alpha)$$

where $t$ is the coordinate on $\mathbb{R}$. This turns every contact manifold into the boundary-type structure of a one-dimension-higher symplectic manifold.

---

## Why This Matters

Contact geometry is often described as the **odd-dimensional shadow** of symplectic geometry: many contact phenomena (Legendrian submanifolds, Reeb dynamics) are best understood by lifting to the symplectization and using symplectic tools (Lagrangian submanifolds, Hamiltonian flows) there.

> [!tip] Cone construction
> $(M\times\mathbb{R}_+, d(t\alpha))$ realizes $M$ as the "boundary at infinity" of a symplectic cone — this is the geometric picture behind contact manifolds arising as boundaries of symplectic fillings.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that $\omega = d(e^t\alpha)$ is closed and non-degenerate given $\alpha$ is a contact form.
2. Explain why the symplectization has one more dimension than $M$, and why that dimension must be even overall.
3. Show that a Legendrian submanifold of $M$ lifts to a Lagrangian cone in the symplectization.
4. Relate symplectization to the notion of a "symplectic filling" of a contact manifold.
