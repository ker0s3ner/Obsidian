## Motivation: Dissipative Systems

Classical (symplectic) Hamiltonian mechanics conserves energy exactly. **Contact Hamiltonian systems** generalize this to model systems with friction or damping, where energy is not conserved but evolves in a controlled way.

---

## Contact Hamiltonian Vector Field

Given a contact form $\alpha$ on phase space and a Hamiltonian function $H$, the contact Hamiltonian vector field $X_H$ is defined by:

$$\alpha(X_H) = -H, \qquad d\alpha(X_H, \cdot) = dH - (R_\alpha H)\,\alpha$$

where $R_\alpha$ is the Reeb field from [[19.3.6 Reeb vector fields]]. Unlike symplectic Hamiltonian flow, this flow does **not** preserve $H$ in general — it evolves according to a controlled dissipation law.

> [!example] Damped harmonic oscillator
> The equations of a damped oscillator can be written as a contact Hamiltonian system, with the non-conservation of $H$ exactly capturing energy loss to friction.

## Practice Problems

1. Derive the contact Hamiltonian equations of motion in local coordinates.
2. Show that when the dissipation term vanishes, the contact Hamiltonian flow reduces to a symplectic one.
3. Model a damped harmonic oscillator as a contact Hamiltonian system.
4. Explain why contact geometry, not symplectic geometry, is the natural setting for thermodynamics and dissipative systems.
