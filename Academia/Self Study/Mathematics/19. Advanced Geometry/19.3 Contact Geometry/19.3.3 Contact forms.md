## Contact Form vs. Contact Structure

A contact structure $\xi$ is defined only up to which hyperplane field it is; a **contact form** $\alpha$ is a specific choice of 1-form with $\xi = \ker\alpha$. Rescaling $\alpha \mapsto f\alpha$ for any nonvanishing function $f$ gives the same $\xi$ but a different contact form.

---

## Coorientation

A choice of contact form gives $\xi$ a **coorientation** (a consistent choice of "positive side"). Not every contact structure is coorientable, but on simply connected manifolds it always is.

> [!note] Gray stability
> Contact structures are far more rigid under deformation than general plane fields: **Gray's stability theorem** says any smooth family of contact structures on a closed manifold is related by ambient diffeomorphisms — contact structures don't "deform" in an essential way on compact manifolds.

---

## Contactomorphisms

A diffeomorphism $\phi: M \to M$ is a **contactomorphism** if $\phi^*\alpha = f\alpha$ for some nonvanishing $f$ — it need not preserve $\alpha$ exactly, only the hyperplane field $\xi$.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $\alpha$ and $2\alpha$ define the same contact structure but are different contact forms.
2. Explain what it means for a contact structure to fail to be coorientable.
3. State Gray's stability theorem and contrast it with the flexibility of general distributions.
4. Verify that a given diffeomorphism of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a contactomorphism for $\alpha = dz - y\,dx$.
