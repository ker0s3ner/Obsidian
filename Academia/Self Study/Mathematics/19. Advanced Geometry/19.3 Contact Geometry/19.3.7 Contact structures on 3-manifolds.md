## Why Dimension 3 Is Special

In dimension 3, contact structures are exactly maximally non-integrable **plane fields** (rather than higher-dimensional hyperplane fields), and there is a rich, largely classified theory — dimension 3 sits at the sweet spot between too rigid (dimension 1, no contact structures exist) and too flexible/unclassified (higher dimensions).

---

## Existence

Every closed orientable 3-manifold admits a contact structure (Martinet's theorem) — indeed, infinitely many non-isotopic ones. This contrasts with symplectic structures, which exist only on a restricted class of even-dimensional manifolds.

> [!note] Two flavors
> Contact structures on 3-manifolds split into two fundamentally different types — see [[19.3.8 Overtwisted vs tight contact structures]] — a dichotomy with no known higher-dimensional analogue as clean as this one.
