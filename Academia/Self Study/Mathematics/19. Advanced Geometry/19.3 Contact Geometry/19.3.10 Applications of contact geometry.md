## Thermodynamics

The space of thermodynamic states (pressure, volume, temperature, entropy) naturally carries a contact structure, with the first law of thermodynamics $dU = TdS - PdV$ playing the role of a contact form — equilibrium states correspond to Legendrian submanifolds.

---

## Geometric Optics

The space of light rays in an optical medium carries a contact structure; Legendrian submanifolds correspond to **wavefronts**, and caustics (where wavefronts develop singularities) are governed by the singularity theory of Legendrian fronts, as sketched in [[19.3.4 Legendrian knots]].

---

## Control Theory

Nonholonomic constraints (e.g. a rolling ball, a car that can't move sideways) are naturally described by a distribution that is often contact or closely related — the reachable-set analysis uses the same non-integrability ideas as [[19.3.1 Contact structures]].

> [!tip] Common thread
> In every application, the contact structure encodes a constraint (energy balance, ray direction, mechanical constraint) that is locally restrictive but globally allows reaching anywhere — the same tension that makes contact structures "maximally non-integrable."

## Practice Problems

1. Write the first law of thermodynamics as a contact form and identify the Legendrian submanifold of equilibrium states.
2. Explain why caustics in optics correspond to singularities of Legendrian front projections.
3. Model the constraint of a rolling coin without slipping and identify a contact-like structure.
4. Discuss why "maximal non-integrability" is exactly the right notion for these constrained but globally reachable systems.
