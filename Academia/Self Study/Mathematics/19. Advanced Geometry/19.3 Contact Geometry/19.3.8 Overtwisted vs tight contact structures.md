## Overtwisted Contact Structures

A contact structure on a 3-manifold is **overtwisted** if it contains an embedded disk whose boundary is Legendrian with zero **twisting** relative to the disk — an "overtwisted disk." Once one exists, Eliashberg's theorem shows the classification of overtwisted structures reduces to pure homotopy theory (formal data only).

---

## Tight Contact Structures

A contact structure with **no** overtwisted disk is **tight**. Tight contact structures are the geometrically interesting ones — their classification is a genuinely hard geometric problem, not reducible to homotopy theory.

| Type | Classified by |
|------|---------------|
| Overtwisted | Homotopy classes of plane fields (Eliashberg) |
| Tight | Genuine geometric/analytic invariants — much harder |

> [!example] Standard contact structure on $\mathbb{R}^3$/$S^3$
> The standard contact structure ($\alpha = dz - y\,dx$, or its analogue on $S^3$) is tight — a foundational example used to test every new invariant in the subject.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain what an overtwisted disk is and why its existence "collapses" the classification problem.
2. State Eliashberg's theorem for overtwisted contact structures.
3. Why is proving a given contact structure is tight generally much harder than showing it's overtwisted?
4. Give an example of a manifold admitting both a tight and an overtwisted contact structure.
