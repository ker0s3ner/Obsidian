## Definition

A **contact structure** on a $(2n+1)$-dimensional manifold $M$ is a maximally non-integrable hyperplane field $\xi \subset TM$ — locally the kernel of a 1-form $\alpha$ (a **contact form**) satisfying:

$$\alpha \wedge (d\alpha)^n \neq 0 \text{ everywhere}$$

---

## Non-Integrability

"Maximally non-integrable" means $\xi$ admits **no** integral submanifold of dimension $> n$ — the opposite extreme from a foliation, where the hyperplane field is fully integrable (Frobenius). Contact structures are the odd-dimensional analogue of symplectic structures.

> [!example] The standard contact structure on $\mathbb{R}^3$
> $\alpha = dz - y\,dx$, with $\xi = \ker\alpha$ spanned by $\partial_y$ and $\partial_x + y\partial_z$. Check: $d\alpha = -dy\wedge dx$, so $\alpha \wedge d\alpha = dz\wedge dy \wedge dx \neq 0$.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the contact condition $\alpha \wedge d\alpha \neq 0$ for $\alpha = dz - y\,dx$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$.
2. Explain why a contact structure can only exist on an odd-dimensional manifold.
3. Show that no 2-dimensional integral submanifold of $\xi = \ker(dz - y\,dx)$ exists.
4. Contrast "maximally non-integrable" with an integrable (foliated) plane field.
