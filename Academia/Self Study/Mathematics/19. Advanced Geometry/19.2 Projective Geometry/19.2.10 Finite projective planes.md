## Order of a Finite Projective Plane

A finite projective plane of **order** $n$ has $n^2+n+1$ points, $n^2+n+1$ lines, $n+1$ points per line, and $n+1$ lines per point.

**Example — the Fano plane** ($n=2$): 7 points, 7 lines, each line has 3 points — the smallest projective plane, coordinatized over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

---

## Existence

Whenever $n$ is a prime power, $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_n)$ gives a projective plane of order $n$. Whether **non-prime-power** orders (like $n=10$, $n=12$) can occur is a famously hard question — $n=10$ was ruled out only by massive computer search (1989).

> [!warning] Bruck–Ryser theorem
> If $n \equiv 1$ or $2 \pmod 4$ and $n$ is not a sum of two squares, no projective plane of order $n$ exists — this rules out $n = 6$, for instance.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that the Fano plane has 7 points and 7 lines using the order formula with $n=2$.
2. Show that $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_3)$ has order 3, and count its points and lines.
3. Apply the Bruck–Ryser criterion to rule out order $n=6$.
4. Explain why the order-10 non-existence proof required computer assistance.
