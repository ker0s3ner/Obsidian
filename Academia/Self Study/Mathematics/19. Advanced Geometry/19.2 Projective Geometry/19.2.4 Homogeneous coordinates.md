## Definition

A point of $\mathbb{P}^n$ is an equivalence class of nonzero vectors $(x_0, \ldots, x_n) \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ under scaling:

$$(x_0, \ldots, x_n) \sim (\lambda x_0, \ldots, \lambda x_n), \qquad \lambda \neq 0$$

Written $[x_0 : \cdots : x_n]$.

---

## Relating to Affine Coordinates

The affine plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ embeds via $(x,y) \mapsto [x:y:1]$. Points with $z = 0$ have no affine counterpart — they are the **points at infinity**.

**Example**: $[2:4:1]$ corresponds to $(2,4)$; $[1:2:0]$ is a point at infinity in the direction of slope 2.

> [!warning] Coordinates are not unique
> $[1:2:3]$ and $[2:4:6]$ are the *same* projective point. Never treat homogeneous coordinates as if they were ordinary vectors — only ratios matter.

## Practice Problems

1. Convert the affine point $(3, -1)$ to homogeneous coordinates and back.
2. Determine whether $[2:-4:6]$ and $[1:-2:3]$ represent the same point.
3. Find the point at infinity corresponding to the direction of the line $y = 3x$.
4. Explain why homogeneous coordinates let a single formula describe both finite and infinite points.
