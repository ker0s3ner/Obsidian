## Statement

Let $A, B, C$ lie on one line and $A', B', C'$ lie on another. Then the three points

$$AB' \cap A'B, \qquad BC' \cap B'C, \qquad CA' \cap C'A$$

are collinear.

---

## Relation to Coordinatizing Fields

Pappus's theorem holds in $\mathbb{P}^2(K)$ exactly when the coordinatizing division ring $K$ is **commutative** — i.e. a field. This makes Pappus's theorem the projective-geometric fingerprint of commutativity of multiplication.

> [!note] Pappus implies Desargues
> A classical result (Hessenberg's theorem) states that any projective plane satisfying Pappus's theorem automatically satisfies Desargues' theorem ([[19.2.6 Desargues theorem]]) — but not conversely.

## Practice Problems

1. Draw an example configuration and verify Pappus's collinearity conclusion.
2. State the dual of Pappus's theorem.
3. Explain, informally, why commutativity of the coordinatizing field is needed for Pappus to hold.
4. Look up Hessenberg's theorem and summarize why Pappus is strictly stronger than Desargues.
