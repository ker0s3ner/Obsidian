## Projective Conics

A projective conic in $\mathbb{P}^2$ is the zero set of a homogeneous quadratic form:

$$Q(x,y,z) = ax^2+by^2+cz^2+dxy+eyz+fzx = 0$$

Unlike the affine classification (ellipse, parabola, hyperbola), **all smooth projective conics are equivalent** under projective transformations — the affine distinctions arise only from where the conic meets the line at infinity.

---

## Poles and Polars

Given a smooth conic $C$ and a point $P$, the **polar line** of $P$ is the line through the two points of tangency of lines from $P$ to $C$ (if $P$ is outside $C$). This pole–polar correspondence is a duality specific to the conic.

> [!example] Harmonic conjugates via conics
> A point $P$, its polar line, and any secant line through $P$ meeting $C$ at $X,Y$ produce a harmonic set: the polar meets the secant at the harmonic conjugate of $P$ with respect to $X,Y$ — connecting back to [[19.2.2 Cross-ratio]].

## Practice Problems

1. Show that a smooth affine ellipse and hyperbola become projectively equivalent once points at infinity are included.
2. Find the polar line of the origin with respect to $x^2+y^2=1$.
3. Explain why a parabola is a conic tangent to the line at infinity.
4. Verify the harmonic conjugate property for a specific point and secant.
