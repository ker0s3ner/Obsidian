## Definition

The **real projective plane** $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R})$ is the set of lines through the origin in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Equivalently, it's $\mathbb{R}^2$ with a "line at infinity" added, so every pair of lines — even parallel ones — meets.

---

## Points and Lines Symmetrically

A point is $[x:y:z]$ (not all zero, up to nonzero scaling — see [[19.2.4 Homogeneous coordinates]]). A line is $\{[x:y:z] : ax+by+cz=0\}$ for some $(a,b,c) \neq (0,0,0)$. This symmetry between the equations for points and lines is the seed of [[19.2.3 Duality]].

> [!example] Points at infinity
> The line at infinity is $z = 0$. Two Euclidean lines with the same slope, which never meet in the affine plane, meet at a specific point on this line at infinity — recovering "every two lines meet."

---

## Axioms of a Projective Plane

Abstractly, a projective plane is a set of points and lines satisfying:

1. Any two distinct points lie on exactly one line.
2. Any two distinct lines meet in exactly one point.
3. There exist four points, no three collinear.

## Practice Problems

1. Find the point at infinity where the lines $y = 2x$ and $y = 2x + 1$ meet in $\mathbb{P}^2$.
2. Verify the three axioms hold for the smallest finite projective plane (the Fano plane, 7 points).
3. Show that axiom 2 fails in the ordinary Euclidean plane.
4. Explain why $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R})$ can be thought of as a hemisphere with antipodal boundary points identified.
