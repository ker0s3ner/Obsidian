## Definition

A **projective transformation** (homography) of $\mathbb{P}^n$ is induced by an invertible linear map on the underlying vector space:

$$[x_0 : \cdots : x_n] \longmapsto [Ax_0 : \cdots : Ax_n], \qquad A \in GL_{n+1}(\mathbb{R})$$

Since scaling $A$ by a constant gives the same map on $\mathbb{P}^n$, the group of projective transformations is $PGL_{n+1}(\mathbb{R}) = GL_{n+1}(\mathbb{R})/\{\text{scalars}\}$.

---

## What Is Preserved

Projective transformations preserve **collinearity** and **cross-ratio** ([[19.2.2 Cross-ratio]]), but not distances, angles, or ratios of distances along non-parallel directions.

> [!example] Perspective drawing
> A camera or an artist's perspective projection is a projective transformation — parallel railway tracks (meeting at a vanishing point) are the classic image of a point at infinity being mapped to a finite point.

## Practice Problems

1. Write down the matrix of the projective transformation that swaps the point at infinity with the origin on a line.
2. Show that projective transformations map lines to lines.
3. Verify that cross-ratio is preserved under a specific $3\times 3$ homography of your choice.
4. Explain why projective transformations, unlike affine ones, can map finite points to points at infinity.
