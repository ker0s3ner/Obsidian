## Definition

For four collinear points $A, B, C, D$, the **cross-ratio** is:

$$(A,B;C,D) = \frac{AC \cdot BD}{AD \cdot BC}$$

using signed lengths along the line.

---

## Invariance Under Projection

The cross-ratio is the fundamental invariant of projective geometry: it is preserved under any **projective transformation** (see [[19.2.5 Projective transformations]]), unlike ordinary distances or ratios of lengths, which projection destroys.

> [!tip] Why cross-ratio and not distance
> Projecting a scene onto a picture plane distorts lengths and angles, but four collinear points' cross-ratio survives — this is why cross-ratio underlies both classical projective proofs and computer vision's single-view geometry.

---

## Harmonic Conjugates

If $(A,B;C,D) = -1$, then $C$ and $D$ are **harmonic conjugates** with respect to $A, B$ — a classically important special configuration, e.g. the relationship between a conic, a pole, and its polar line.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $(A,B;C,D)$ for points at positions $0, 1, 2, 4$ on a line.
2. Show that swapping $A \leftrightarrow B$ replaces the cross-ratio by its reciprocal.
3. Verify that harmonic conjugates satisfy $(A,B;C,D) = (A,B;D,C)^{-1}$.
4. Explain why cross-ratio, not distance, is the natural invariant for projective transformations.
