## The Principle of Duality

In $\mathbb{P}^2$, points and lines play symmetric roles in the axioms (see [[19.2.1 Projective planes]]). Swapping "point" $\leftrightarrow$ "line" and "lies on" $\leftrightarrow$ "passes through" in any true projective statement produces another true statement — its **dual**.

> [!example] A dual pair
> **Statement**: two distinct points determine a unique line.
> **Dual**: two distinct lines determine a unique point.
> Both are axioms of the projective plane — each is the other's dual.

---

## Dual of Desargues' Theorem

[[19.2.6 Desargues theorem]] is **self-dual**: its dual statement is logically equivalent to itself, which is part of why it plays such a central structural role in projective geometry.

---

## Duality via Homogeneous Coordinates

A point $[x:y:z]$ and a line $ax+by+cz=0$ are dual under the pairing $(x,y,z) \leftrightarrow (a,b,c)$ — literally the same triple of numbers interpreted two ways.
