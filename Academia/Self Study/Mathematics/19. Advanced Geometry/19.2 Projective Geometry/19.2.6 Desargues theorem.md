## Statement

Two triangles $ABC$ and $A'B'C'$ are **in perspective from a point** $O$ (the lines $AA'$, $BB'$, $CC'$ are concurrent at $O$) if and only if they are **in perspective from a line** — the intersections $AB \cap A'B'$, $BC \cap B'C'$, $CA \cap C'A'$ are collinear.

---

## Self-Duality

Swapping "perspective from a point" and "perspective from a line" under [[19.2.3 Duality]] turns Desargues' theorem into itself — a rare and structurally important property.

> [!note] Desarguesian planes
> A projective plane in which Desargues' theorem holds is called **Desarguesian**. Every plane coordinatized over a field (like $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{R})$) is Desarguesian, but some finite projective planes are not — Desargues' theorem is genuinely a coordinatizability criterion.

## Practice Problems

1. Draw two triangles in perspective from a point and verify the perspective-from-a-line conclusion.
2. State the dual of Desargues' theorem and confirm it matches the original.
3. Explain why non-Desarguesian planes cannot be coordinatized by a field.
4. Use Desargues' theorem to justify a construction in perspective drawing.
