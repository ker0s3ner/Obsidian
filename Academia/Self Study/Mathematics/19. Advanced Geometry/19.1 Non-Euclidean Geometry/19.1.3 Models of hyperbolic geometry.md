## Poincaré Disk Model

Points are the open unit disk $\{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$. The hyperbolic metric is:

$$ds^2 = \frac{4(dx^2 + dy^2)}{(1 - x^2 - y^2)^2}$$

**Lines** are diameters or circular arcs meeting the boundary circle at right angles.

> [!tip] Conformal but not distance-true
> The Poincaré disk preserves angles (it's conformal) but distorts distances — regions near the boundary represent enormous hyperbolic distances, which is why Escher's *Circle Limit* tilings look denser near the edge.

---

## Poincaré Half-Plane Model

Points are $\{(x,y) : y > 0\}$ with metric:

$$ds^2 = \frac{dx^2 + dy^2}{y^2}$$

**Lines** are vertical rays and semicircles centered on the boundary $y = 0$.

---

## Klein (Beltrami) Model

Points are again the open unit disk, but **lines are straight Euclidean chords** — not conformal, but geodesics look like ordinary line segments. See [[19.1.10 Klein model and comparisons]] for how to convert between models.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that the Poincaré disk metric blows up as $|z| \to 1$.
2. Show that a diameter of the Poincaré disk is a hyperbolic geodesic.
3. Convert a point from the Poincaré disk model to the Klein model.
4. Explain why the half-plane and disk models are isometric to each other.
