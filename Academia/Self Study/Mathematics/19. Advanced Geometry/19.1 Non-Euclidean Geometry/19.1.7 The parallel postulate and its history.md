## Euclid's Fifth Postulate

Euclid's original statement is awkward: if a line crossing two other lines makes interior angles on one side summing to less than two right angles, those two lines meet on that side. **Playfair's axiom** is the modern equivalent: through a point not on a line, exactly one parallel exists.

---

## Two Millennia of Attempted Proofs

Mathematicians tried for centuries to derive the fifth postulate from the other four. Notable attempts:

- **Proclus** (5th century) — assumed parallel lines stay equidistant, which is itself equivalent to the postulate
- **Saccheri** (1733) — assumed its negation and tried to reach a contradiction, unknowingly discovering hyperbolic geometry along the way
- **Lambert** — studied quadrilaterals with three right angles, finding the fourth angle is acute exactly when the postulate fails

---

## Resolution: Independence

In the 1820s–30s, **Bolyai**, **Lobachevsky**, and **Gauss** independently realized the postulate is **logically independent** of the other four — both its affirmation and negation give consistent geometries. **Beltrami** (1868) later gave a rigorous model (equivalent to the disk model in [[19.1.3 Models of hyperbolic geometry]]), proving hyperbolic geometry is as consistent as Euclidean geometry.

> [!note] Why this mattered
> This resolved a 2000-year-old open problem and showed that "geometry" is not a single fixed truth about space — it depends on which axioms you adopt. This directly inspired the axiomatic view of mathematics.
