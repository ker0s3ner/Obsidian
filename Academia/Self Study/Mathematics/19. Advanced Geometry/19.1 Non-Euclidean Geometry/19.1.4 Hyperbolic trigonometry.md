## Hyperbolic Law of Cosines

For a hyperbolic triangle with sides $a, b, c$ (opposite angles $A, B, C$):

$$\cosh c = \cosh a \cosh b - \sinh a \sinh b \cos C$$

Note the use of $\cosh, \sinh$ in place of the Euclidean $\cos, \sin$ for the sides.

---

## Hyperbolic Law of Sines

$$\frac{\sinh a}{\sin A} = \frac{\sinh b}{\sin B} = \frac{\sinh c}{\sin C}$$

---

## Right Triangles

For a right angle at $C$ ($C = \pi/2$):

$$\cosh c = \cosh a \cosh b$$

This is the hyperbolic analogue of the Pythagorean theorem.

> [!note] Small-triangle limit
> As $a, b, c \to 0$, $\cosh x \approx 1 + x^2/2$ and $\sinh x \approx x$, and both laws above reduce to their familiar Euclidean counterparts.

## Practice Problems

1. Derive the Euclidean Pythagorean theorem as the small-triangle limit of $\cosh c = \cosh a \cosh b$.
2. Solve a hyperbolic right triangle given legs $a = 1$, $b = 1$.
3. Show the hyperbolic law of sines is consistent with the angle-sum defect formula.
4. Explain why hyperbolic triangles are fully determined by their three angles (unlike Euclidean triangles).
