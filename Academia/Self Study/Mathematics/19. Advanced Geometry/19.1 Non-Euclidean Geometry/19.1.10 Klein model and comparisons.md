## The Klein Model

Also called the **Beltrami–Klein model**: points are the open unit disk, and hyperbolic **lines are straight Euclidean chords** of the disk. Distances are given by:

$$d(P,Q) = \frac{1}{2}\left|\ln \frac{|AQ||PB|}{|AP||QB|}\right|$$

where $A, B$ are the ideal endpoints of the chord through $P, Q$ (a cross-ratio — see [[19.2.2 Cross-ratio]]).

---

## Converting Between Models

| From | To | Map |
|------|----|-----|
| Klein $(x,y)$ | Poincaré disk | $\left(\dfrac{x}{1+\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}}, \dfrac{y}{1+\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}}\right)$ |
| Poincaré disk $(u,v)$ | Klein | $\left(\dfrac{2u}{1+u^2+v^2}, \dfrac{2v}{1+u^2+v^2}\right)$ |

> [!tip] Trade-off between models
> The Klein model has straight lines but distorts angles; the Poincaré disk preserves angles but bends lines into arcs. Neither model can do both — a consequence of the sphere/hyperbolic plane not being isometrically flat.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that a straight chord through the center of the Klein disk is also a diameter in the Poincaré disk.
2. Convert the Klein-model point $(0.5, 0)$ to the Poincaré disk.
3. Explain why the Klein model is convenient for visualizing geodesic triangles, despite distorting angles.
4. Verify the cross-ratio distance formula gives $d(P,P) = 0$.
