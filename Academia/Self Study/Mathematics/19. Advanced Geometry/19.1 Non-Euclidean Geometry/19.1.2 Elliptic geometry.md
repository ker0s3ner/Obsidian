## No Parallels at All

**Elliptic geometry** replaces Euclid's fifth postulate with the opposite extreme: through a point not on a line $\ell$, **no** line exists that fails to meet $\ell$ — every pair of lines intersects.

---

## Constant Positive Curvature

The model is a surface of constant Gaussian curvature $K = +1$ (the sphere, with antipodal points identified so that "lines" — great circles — meet in exactly one point instead of two).

**Triangle angle sum**: for a spherical/elliptic triangle with angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ and area $A$:

$$A = \alpha + \beta + \gamma - \pi$$

so the angle sum **exceeds** $\pi$ — the opposite of the hyperbolic case.

> [!example] Comparing the three geometries
> | Geometry | Curvature | Parallels through external point | Angle sum |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Hyperbolic | $K < 0$ | Infinitely many | $< \pi$ |
> | Euclidean | $K = 0$ | Exactly one | $= \pi$ |
> | Elliptic | $K > 0$ | None | $> \pi$ |

## Practice Problems

1. Show that two great circles on a sphere always intersect in exactly two antipodal points.
2. Compute the area of a spherical triangle with all right angles.
3. Explain why elliptic geometry requires identifying antipodal points to make "lines" meet only once.
4. Contrast the parallel behavior of hyperbolic, Euclidean, and elliptic geometry.
