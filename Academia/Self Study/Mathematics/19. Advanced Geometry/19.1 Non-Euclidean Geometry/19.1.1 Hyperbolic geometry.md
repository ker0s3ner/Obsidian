## The Parallel Postulate Fails

Euclid's fifth postulate says that through a point not on a line, exactly one parallel line exists. **Hyperbolic geometry** replaces this with: through a point not on a line $\ell$, there are **infinitely many** lines not meeting $\ell$.

## Constant Negative Curvature

Hyperbolic geometry is the geometry of a surface with constant Gaussian curvature $K = -1$. Distances grow exponentially — the circumference of a circle of radius $r$ is:

$$C(r) = 2\pi \sinh(r)$$

which grows much faster than the Euclidean $2\pi r$.

> [!note] Triangles have angle sum less than $\pi$
> For a hyperbolic triangle with angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ and area $A$:
> $$A = \pi - (\alpha + \beta + \gamma)$$
> This is the **Gauss–Bonnet** relation in constant curvature $-1$ — see [[19.1.5 Gauss-Bonnet theorem]].

## Practice Problems

1. Show that as curvature $K \to 0$, hyperbolic formulas reduce to Euclidean ones.
2. Compute the area of an ideal hyperbolic triangle (all angles $= 0$).
3. Explain why hyperbolic circles have circumference growing exponentially, not linearly, in radius.
4. Why must every hyperbolic triangle have angle sum strictly less than $\pi$?
