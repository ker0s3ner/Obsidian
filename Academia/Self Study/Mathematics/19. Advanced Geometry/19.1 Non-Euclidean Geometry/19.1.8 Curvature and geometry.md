## Gaussian Curvature as the Unifying Parameter

All three constant-curvature geometries are governed by a single number $K$:

| $K$ | Geometry | Local model |
|-----|----------|-------------|
| $K < 0$ | Hyperbolic | Poincaré disk (rescale to $K=-1$) |
| $K = 0$ | Euclidean | The plane |
| $K > 0$ | Elliptic/spherical | Sphere of radius $1/\sqrt{K}$ |

---

## Curvature Is Intrinsic

Gauss's **Theorema Egregium** shows curvature can be computed from measurements *within* the surface (distances and angles), without reference to how it sits in space — this is why a flat piece of paper ($K=0$) cannot be wrapped around a sphere ($K>0$) without stretching or tearing.

> [!tip] A quick intrinsic test
> Measure the circumference of a small circle of intrinsic radius $r$. If it's less than $2\pi r$, curvature is positive there; if more, curvature is negative; if exactly $2\pi r$, curvature is zero.
