## Definition

For a fixed prime $p$, the **$p$-adic valuation** of a nonzero integer $n$ is the exponent of $p$ in its prime factorization:

$$v_p(n) = \max\{k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} : p^k \mid n\}$$

Extended to $\mathbb{Q}$ by $v_p(a/b) = v_p(a) - v_p(b)$, and $v_p(0) = \infty$.

---

## Properties

| Property | Statement |
|----------|-----------|
| Multiplicativity | $v_p(xy) = v_p(x) + v_p(y)$ |
| Ultrametric inequality | $v_p(x+y) \geq \min(v_p(x), v_p(y))$, with equality if $v_p(x) \neq v_p(y)$ |

The ultrametric inequality is why $p$-adic geometry behaves so differently from ordinary geometry: **every triangle is isosceles**.

---

## From Valuation to Metric

$$|x|_p = p^{-v_p(x)}, \qquad d_p(x,y) = |x - y|_p$$

This defines an **ultrametric**: $d_p(x,z) \leq \max(d_p(x,y), d_p(y,z))$, strictly stronger than the ordinary triangle inequality.

> [!example] Example
> $v_5(50) = 2$ since $50 = 2 \cdot 5^2$, so $|50|_5 = 5^{-2} = 1/25$ — "small" in the 5-adic sense despite being a large integer.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $v_2(96)$, $v_3(96)$.
2. Show the ultrametric inequality forces every triangle in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ to be isosceles (two sides equal).
3. Prove $v_p(n!) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \lfloor n/p^i \rfloor$ (Legendre's formula).
4. Use $v_p$ to determine how many trailing zeros $100!$ has in base 10.
