## Statement

> [!example] Hensel's Lemma
> Let $f \in \mathbb{Z}_p[x]$. If $a \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ satisfies $f(a) \equiv 0 \pmod p$ and $f'(a) \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$, then there is a **unique** $\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ with $f(\alpha) = 0$ and $\alpha \equiv a \pmod p$.

This is the $p$-adic analogue of Newton's method: a simple root mod $p$ "lifts" uniquely to an exact root in $\mathbb{Z}_p$.

---

## Lifting Construction

Newton iteration lifts the approximate root step by step:

$$a_{n+1} = a_n - \frac{f(a_n)}{f'(a_n)}$$

converging $p$-adically to the exact root, doubling the precision (number of correct $p$-adic digits) at each step.

---

## Example

Solve $x^2 = 2$ in $\mathbb{Q}_7$: since $2$ is a QR mod $7$ ($3^2 = 9 \equiv 2$), and $f'(3) = 6 \not\equiv 0 \pmod 7$, Hensel's lemma lifts $a_0 = 3$ to an exact square root of $2$ in $\mathbb{Z}_7$.

> [!tip] Contrast with $\mathbb{R}$
> $\sqrt2 \notin \mathbb{Q}$, but $\sqrt2 \in \mathbb{Q}_7$ — different completions of $\mathbb{Q}$ contain different algebraic numbers.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $3^2 \equiv 2 \pmod 7$ and use Hensel's lemma to argue $\sqrt 2 \in \mathbb{Q}_7$.
2. Explain why the condition $f'(a) \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$ is necessary (consider $f(x)=x^2$ at $a=0$, $p=2$).
3. Use Newton iteration to compute the next digit of $\sqrt2$ in $\mathbb{Z}_7$ beyond $a_0=3$.
