## Definition

For a polynomial $f(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n a_i x^i$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the **Newton polygon** is the lower convex hull of the points $(i, v_p(a_i))$ for $a_i \neq 0$.

---

## Reading Off Roots

> [!example] Newton Polygon Theorem
> The slopes of the Newton polygon's segments give exactly the $p$-adic valuations of the roots of $f$ (in an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_p$), and the horizontal length of each segment counts how many roots have that valuation.

**Example** — for $f(x) = x^2 - p$: points $(0, 1)$ and $(2, 0)$, a single segment of slope $-1/2$, predicting both roots have valuation $1/2$ — consistent with $x = \pm\sqrt p$ living in a ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

---

## Why This Matters

Newton polygons let you determine irreducibility and ramification of extensions $\mathbb{Q}_p(\alpha)/\mathbb{Q}_p$ purely combinatorially, without solving for roots explicitly — a computational shortcut for the ramification questions of [[17.2.5 Ramification]] in the local setting.

## Practice Problems

1. Draw the Newton polygon of $f(x) = x^3 - p^2$ and read off the valuations of its roots.
2. Explain why a single-segment Newton polygon with non-integer slope forces $f$ to be irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.
3. Compare the Newton polygon of $x^2 - p$ to that of $x^2 - 1$; explain the difference in root valuations.
