## Motivation

$\mathbb{R}$ is the completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ with respect to the usual absolute value. The **$p$-adic numbers** $\mathbb{Q}_p$ are the completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ with respect to a *different* notion of distance — the $p$-adic absolute value — built around divisibility by a fixed prime $p$.

---

## p-adic Absolute Value

$$|x|_p = p^{-v_p(x)}$$

where $v_p(x)$ is the **$p$-adic valuation** (see [[17.4.2 p-adic valuation]]): the exponent of $p$ in $x$'s factorization. Numbers highly divisible by $p$ are considered "small."

> [!example] Ostrowski's Theorem
> Every nontrivial absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to either the usual $|\cdot|$ or some $|\cdot|_p$. There are no other options — the real and $p$-adic completions exhaust all ways to complete $\mathbb{Q}$.

---

## Construction of $\mathbb{Q}_p$

$\mathbb{Q}_p$ is the completion of $\mathbb{Q}$ under $|\cdot|_p$, analogous to how $\mathbb{R}$ completes $\mathbb{Q}$ under the usual metric. Every element has a unique expansion:

$$x = \sum_{i=k}^{\infty} a_i p^i, \qquad a_i \in \{0, 1, \ldots, p-1\}, \ k \in \mathbb{Z}$$

The **$p$-adic integers** $\mathbb{Z}_p$ are those $x$ with $k \geq 0$ — the closed unit ball.

---

## Strange Convergence

> [!warning] Convergence looks backwards
> The series $1 + p + p^2 + p^3 + \cdots$ **converges** in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (to $\frac{1}{1-p}$), even though it diverges in $\mathbb{R}$ — because $|p^n|_p = p^{-n} \to 0$.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $|60|_2$, $|60|_3$, $|60|_5$.
2. Show $1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + \cdots$ converges to $-1$ in $\mathbb{Q}_2$.
3. Explain why $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is the closed unit ball $\{x : |x|_p \leq 1\}$.
