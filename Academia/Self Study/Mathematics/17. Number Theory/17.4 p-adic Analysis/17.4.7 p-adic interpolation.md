## Motivation

Classical functions defined by real/complex analytic continuation (like $\zeta(s)$) sometimes admit a **$p$-adic continuation** instead — a genuinely different function agreeing with the classical one only at special (e.g. negative integer) points.

---

## p-adic Zeta Functions

Kubota and Leopoldt constructed a **$p$-adic zeta function** $\zeta_p(s)$ interpolating the values $\zeta(1-n)$ for $n \equiv 0 \pmod{p-1}$ (values that are rational, by Euler's formula for $\zeta$ at negative integers), but as a genuinely $p$-adic analytic function of $s \in \mathbb{Z}_p$.

> [!note] Why interpolate at all
> $\zeta(1-n)$ for even $n$ are rational multiples of Bernoulli numbers. The $p$-adic construction packages infinitely many of these rational values into a single continuous $p$-adic function — the beginning of Iwasawa theory.
