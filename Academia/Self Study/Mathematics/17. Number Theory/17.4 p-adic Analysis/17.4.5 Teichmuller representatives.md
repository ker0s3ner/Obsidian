## Definition

Every nonzero $x \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ has a unique **Teichmüller representative** $\omega(x) \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ — the unique $(p-1)$-th root of unity congruent to $x$ mod $p$.

Concretely, $\omega(x) = \lim_{n\to\infty} x^{p^n}$, and it satisfies $\omega(x)^{p-1} = 1$.

---

## Why This Exists

By Hensel's lemma (see [[17.4.3 Hensel's lemma]]), the polynomial $y^{p-1} - 1$ has a simple root mod $p$ for each nonzero residue mod $p$ — since $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is cyclic of order $p-1$ and the derivative $(p-1)y^{p-2}$ is nonzero on nonzero residues. Hensel's lemma lifts each of these $p-1$ roots uniquely to $\mathbb{Z}_p$.

---

## Digit Expansion

Teichmüller representatives give a clean multiplicative set of "digits": every unit $x \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ can be written uniquely as:

$$x = \omega(x) \cdot u, \qquad u \equiv 1 \pmod p$$

splitting $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ as a direct product of the finite cyclic group of $(p-1)$-th roots of unity and the pro-$p$ group $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$.
