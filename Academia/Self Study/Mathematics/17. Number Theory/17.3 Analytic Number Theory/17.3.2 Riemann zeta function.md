## Definition

For $\text{Re}(s) > 1$:

$$\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^s}$$

---

## Euler Product

> [!example] Euler Product Formula
> $$\zeta(s) = \prod_{p \text{ prime}} \frac{1}{1 - p^{-s}}, \qquad \text{Re}(s) > 1$$

This single identity is the bridge between analysis and primes: the zeta function "sees" every prime simultaneously, which is why its analytic behavior encodes prime distribution (see [[17.3.1 Prime number theorem]]).

---

## Analytic Continuation

$\zeta(s)$ extends to a **meromorphic function** on all of $\mathbb{C}$, with a single simple pole at $s=1$ (residue $1$). The extension satisfies the **functional equation**:

$$\zeta(s) = 2^s \pi^{s-1} \sin\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)\Gamma(1-s)\,\zeta(1-s)$$

relating values at $s$ and $1-s$.

---

## Zeros

- **Trivial zeros**: at $s = -2, -4, -6, \ldots$ (from the $\sin$ factor)
- **Nontrivial zeros**: lie in the **critical strip** $0 < \text{Re}(s) < 1$

> [!warning] The Riemann Hypothesis
> Conjectures that every nontrivial zero has $\text{Re}(s) = 1/2$ exactly. Unproven — one of the Millennium Prize Problems, with deep consequences for the error term in the PNT.

---

## Special Values

| $s$ | $\zeta(s)$ |
|-----|------------|
| $2$ | $\pi^2/6$ |
| $4$ | $\pi^4/90$ |
| $-1$ | $-1/12$ (via analytic continuation) |
| $0$ | $-1/2$ |

## Practice Problems

1. Derive the Euler product from the sieve of Eratosthenes intuition (each factor $\frac{1}{1-p^{-s}}$ removes one prime's multiples).
2. Show $\zeta(2) = \pi^2/6$ implies there are infinitely many primes.
3. Explain why $\zeta(-1) = -1/12$ requires analytic continuation, not the original series.
4. Locate the trivial zeros and explain why they don't come from the Euler product region.
