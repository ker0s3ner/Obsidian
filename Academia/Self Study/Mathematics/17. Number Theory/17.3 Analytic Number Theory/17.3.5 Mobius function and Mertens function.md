## Möbius Function

$$\mu(n) = \begin{cases} 1 & n = 1 \\ (-1)^k & n \text{ is a product of } k \text{ distinct primes} \\ 0 & n \text{ has a squared prime factor}\end{cases}$$

**Key identity**: $\sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d) = \begin{cases}1 & n=1 \\ 0 & n>1\end{cases}$ — this is exactly what powers **Möbius inversion**.

---

## Möbius Inversion

If $g(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} f(d)$, then:

$$f(n) = \sum_{d \mid n} \mu(d)\, g(n/d)$$

**Example**: since $n = \sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)$, inversion recovers $\varphi(n) = \sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\cdot n/d$.

---

## Mertens Function

$$M(x) = \sum_{n \leq x} \mu(n)$$

> [!note] Mertens Conjecture (disproved)
> Once conjectured $|M(x)| < \sqrt x$ for all $x>1$; disproved in 1985. The true growth rate of $M(x)$ is deeply tied to the zeros of $\zeta(s)$ — the Riemann Hypothesis is **equivalent** to $M(x) = O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $\mu(n)$ for $n = 1, \ldots, 12$.
2. Use Möbius inversion to express $\varphi(n)$ as a sum over divisors of $n$.
3. Compute $M(10)$ directly from the values of $\mu$.
4. Explain the connection between $M(x)$'s growth rate and the Riemann Hypothesis.
