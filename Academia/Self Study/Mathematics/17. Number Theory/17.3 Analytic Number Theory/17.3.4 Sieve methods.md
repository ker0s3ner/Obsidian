## Sieve of Eratosthenes, Revisited

The oldest sieve: remove multiples of each prime up to $\sqrt N$ to find all primes $\leq N$. Modern **sieve methods** generalize this into a quantitative tool for bounding the size of sets after removing "unwanted" residues.

---

## Legendre's Sieve

To count integers up to $x$ coprime to a set of primes $P$:

$$\Phi(x, P) = \sum_{d \mid \prod_{p \in P} p} \mu(d) \left\lfloor \frac{x}{d}\right\rfloor$$

using Möbius inversion — but this becomes computationally useless once $|P|$ is large (inclusion-exclusion blows up).

---

## Brun's Sieve and the Sieve of Eratosthenes–Legendre Bound

Brun's refinement truncates the inclusion-exclusion sum, trading exactness for a usable **upper bound**. This is how one proves results like:

> [!example] Brun's Theorem
> The sum of reciprocals of twin primes converges — a weaker but rigorous relative of the (still open) twin prime conjecture.

---

## Selberg's Sieve

A quadratic-form optimization over sieve weights, giving sharper upper bounds than Brun's method and used in Chen's theorem (every large even number is a sum of a prime and a number with at most two prime factors).
