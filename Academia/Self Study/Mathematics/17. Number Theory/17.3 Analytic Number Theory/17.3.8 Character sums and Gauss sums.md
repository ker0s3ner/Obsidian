## Character Sums

For a Dirichlet character $\chi$ mod $q$, a **character sum** is $\sum_{n=1}^{N} \chi(n)$. Since $\sum_{n=1}^q \chi(n) = 0$ for nontrivial $\chi$, these sums exhibit cancellation — the Pólya–Vinogradov inequality bounds partial sums by $O(\sqrt q \log q)$.

---

## Gauss Sums

$$g(\chi) = \sum_{n=0}^{q-1} \chi(n)\, e^{2\pi i n/q}$$

**Example** — for the Legendre symbol $\chi = \left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)$:

$$g(\chi) = \sum_{n=0}^{p-1}\left(\frac np\right)e^{2\pi in/p}, \qquad |g(\chi)| = \sqrt p$$

This exact evaluation of $|g(\chi)|$ is one ingredient in Gauss's own proof of quadratic reciprocity (see [[17.1.9 Quadratic residues]]).

---

## Why They Matter

Gauss sums link additive structure ($e^{2\pi in/q}$) to multiplicative structure ($\chi$) — the same bridge exploited by Dirichlet L-functions and by the functional equation of $\zeta(s)$ (see [[17.3.2 Riemann zeta function]]).

2. Compute the Gauss sum for the Legendre symbol mod $5$.
