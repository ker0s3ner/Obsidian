## Goal

The **Hardy–Littlewood circle method** estimates the number of ways to write an integer as a sum with constraints (e.g. as a sum of primes, or of $k$-th powers), by encoding the counting problem as an integral over the unit circle.

---

## Setup

To count representations of $N$ as $a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_k$ with each $a_i$ from a set $A$, form the **generating function**:

$$F(\theta) = \sum_{a \in A} e^{2\pi i a\theta}$$

Then the count is:

$$r(N) = \int_0^1 F(\theta)^k e^{-2\pi i N\theta} \, d\theta$$

---

## Major and Minor Arcs

Split $[0,1]$ into:

- **Major arcs**: near rationals $a/q$ with small $q$ — where $F(\theta)$ is large and can be estimated precisely
- **Minor arcs**: everywhere else — where cancellation makes the contribution negligible

**Example application**: Vinogradov's theorem (every sufficiently large odd number is a sum of three primes) is proved exactly this way.
