## Definition

A **Dirichlet series** is a series of the form:

$$F(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_n}{n^s}$$

The Riemann zeta function is the case $a_n = 1$ for all $n$.

---

## Dirichlet Characters

A **Dirichlet character** mod $q$ is a group homomorphism $\chi: (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times \to \mathbb{C}^\times$, extended to $\mathbb{Z}$ by setting $\chi(n) = 0$ when $\gcd(n,q) > 1$.

---

## Dirichlet L-functions

$$L(s, \chi) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\chi(n)}{n^s} = \prod_{p} \frac{1}{1 - \chi(p)p^{-s}}$$

These generalize $\zeta(s)$ (the case $\chi \equiv 1$) and are the key tool in proving primes are equidistributed among residue classes.

---

## Dirichlet's Theorem on Primes in Arithmetic Progressions

> [!example] Dirichlet's Theorem
> For coprime $a, q$, there are infinitely many primes $p \equiv a \pmod q$.

The proof shows $L(s,\chi) \neq 0$ at $s=1$ for nontrivial $\chi$ — directly analogous to how $\zeta(s) \neq 0$ at $\text{Re}(s)=1$ drives the PNT in [[17.3.1 Prime number theorem]].

---

## Euler Products for General Dirichlet Series

Any **multiplicative** sequence $a_n$ (i.e. $a_{mn} = a_m a_n$ for coprime $m,n$) gives a Dirichlet series with an Euler product:

$$F(s) = \prod_p \left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{a_{p^k}}{p^{ks}}\right)$$

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\chi_4(n)$ (the nontrivial character mod 4) gives $L(s,\chi_4) = \sum (-1)^{(n-1)/2}/n^s$ over odd $n$.
2. Show that the Möbius function $\mu(n)$ gives $\sum \mu(n)/n^s = 1/\zeta(s)$.
3. Explain why Dirichlet characters must be multiplicative for the Euler product to hold.
4. Use Dirichlet's theorem to argue there are infinitely many primes ending in the digit 7 (base 10).
