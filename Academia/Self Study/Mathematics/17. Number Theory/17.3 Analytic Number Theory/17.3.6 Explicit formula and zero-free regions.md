## The Explicit Formula

Riemann's **explicit formula** expresses the prime-counting function in terms of the nontrivial zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta(s)$:

$$\psi(x) = x - \sum_{\rho} \frac{x^\rho}{\rho} - \ln(2\pi) - \frac12\ln(1-x^{-2})$$

This literally writes the primes as a sum over zeros — the zeros are not just an obstruction to proving the PNT, they encode the "error term" of the prime distribution exactly.

---

## Zero-Free Regions

The original proof of the Prime Number Theorem needs only that $\zeta(s) \neq 0$ on the line $\text{Re}(s)=1$. Stronger **zero-free regions** (regions to the left of $\text{Re}(s)=1$ known to contain no zeros) give better error terms in the PNT.

> [!note] The Riemann Hypothesis as the extreme case
> RH claims the zero-free region can be pushed all the way to $\text{Re}(s) > 1/2$ — the strongest possible statement, giving the best possible error term $O(\sqrt x \ln x)$.
