## Definition

$$\varphi(n) = |\{1 \leq k \leq n : \gcd(k,n) = 1\}|$$

the count of integers up to $n$ coprime to $n$.

---

## Formula

$\varphi$ is **multiplicative**: $\varphi(mn) = \varphi(m)\varphi(n)$ for coprime $m,n$, and for a prime power:

$$\varphi(p^k) = p^k - p^{k-1} = p^k\left(1 - \frac1p\right)$$

giving, for $n = p_1^{k_1}\cdots p_r^{k_r}$:

$$\varphi(n) = n\prod_{i=1}^r \left(1 - \frac{1}{p_i}\right)$$

**Example**: $\varphi(12) = \varphi(4)\varphi(3) = 2 \cdot 2 = 4$ (namely $1,5,7,11$).

---

## Euler's Theorem

> [!example] Euler's Theorem
> If $\gcd(a,n) = 1$: $$a^{\varphi(n)} \equiv 1 \pmod n$$

This generalizes [[17.1.6 Modular exponentiation & Fermat's little theorem]]'s Fermat's Little Theorem (the case $n$ prime, where $\varphi(p) = p-1$), and is the theoretical basis for RSA: decryption works because $\varphi(n)$ controls the exponent's period.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $\varphi(360)$.
2. Show $\sum_{d \mid n} \varphi(d) = n$ using divisor pairing.
3. Verify Euler's theorem for $a=7$, $n=10$.
4. Explain how RSA key generation uses $\varphi(pq) = (p-1)(q-1)$.
