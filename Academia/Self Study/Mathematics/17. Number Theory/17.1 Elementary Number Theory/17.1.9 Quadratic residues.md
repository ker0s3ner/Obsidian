## Definition

An integer $a$ coprime to an odd prime $p$ is a **quadratic residue** mod $p$ if $x^2 \equiv a \pmod p$ has a solution; otherwise it's a **quadratic non-residue**.

Exactly $(p-1)/2$ of the nonzero residues mod $p$ are QRs, and $(p-1)/2$ are non-residues.

---

## Legendre Symbol

$$\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) = \begin{cases} 1 & a \text{ is a QR mod } p \\ -1 & a \text{ is a non-residue} \\ 0 & p \mid a \end{cases}$$

**Euler's criterion**:

$$\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) \equiv a^{(p-1)/2} \pmod p$$

---

## Quadratic Reciprocity

> [!example] Law of Quadratic Reciprocity
> For distinct odd primes $p, q$:
> $$\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\left(\frac{q}{p}\right) = (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot\frac{q-1}{2}}$$

This lets you decide whether $p$ is a QR mod $q$ by instead checking whether $q$ is a QR mod $p$ — often a much smaller computation.

## Practice Problems

1. List all quadratic residues mod $11$.
2. Use Euler's criterion to determine if $7$ is a QR mod $13$.
3. Use quadratic reciprocity to determine if $3$ is a QR mod $23$.
4. Show $\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right) = 1$ iff $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$.
