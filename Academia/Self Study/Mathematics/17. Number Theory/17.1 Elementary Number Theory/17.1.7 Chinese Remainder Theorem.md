## Statement

> [!example] Chinese Remainder Theorem
> If $n_1, \ldots, n_k$ are pairwise coprime, the system
> $$x \equiv a_1 \pmod{n_1}, \quad \ldots, \quad x \equiv a_k \pmod{n_k}$$
> has a unique solution modulo $N = n_1 n_2 \cdots n_k$.

---

## Constructing the Solution

Let $N_i = N/n_i$. Since $\gcd(N_i, n_i) = 1$, find $y_i$ with $N_i y_i \equiv 1 \pmod{n_i}$ (extended Euclidean algorithm). Then:

$$x \equiv \sum_{i=1}^k a_i N_i y_i \pmod N$$

**Example — solve $x \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, $x \equiv 3 \pmod 5$:**

$N = 15$, $N_1 = 5$, $N_2 = 3$. Inverse of $5 \bmod 3$ is $2$; inverse of $3 \bmod 5$ is $2$.

$$x \equiv 2\cdot5\cdot2 + 3\cdot3\cdot2 = 20 + 18 = 38 \equiv 8 \pmod{15}$$

## Ring-Theoretic Statement

$$\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/n_1\mathbb{Z} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{Z}/n_k\mathbb{Z}$$

as rings, whenever the $n_i$ are pairwise coprime — this generalizes directly to any Dedekind domain via [[17.2.3 Class groups]]'s ideal factorization.

## Practice Problems

1. Solve $x \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, $x \equiv 2 \pmod 9$, $x \equiv 3 \pmod{25}$.
2. Show the CRT map $\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}$ is a ring isomorphism.
3. Use CRT to speed up computing $x^2 \bmod 77$ by working mod 7 and mod 11 separately.
4. Explain why the coprimality condition is essential — give a system with no solution when moduli share a factor.
