## Definition

A **Diophantine equation** is a polynomial equation in two or more unknowns for which only **integer solutions** are sought.

---

## Linear Diophantine Equations

$$ax + by = c, \qquad a, b, c \in \mathbb{Z}$$

> [!example] Solvability Criterion
> $ax + by = c$ has integer solutions if and only if $\gcd(a,b) \mid c$.

If $(x_0, y_0)$ is one solution, the full solution set is:

$$x = x_0 + \frac{b}{d}t, \qquad y = y_0 - \frac{a}{d}t, \qquad t \in \mathbb{Z}, \ d = \gcd(a,b)$$

**Example — solve $6x + 9y = 15$:** $\gcd(6,9) = 3 \mid 15$, so solutions exist. Dividing through: $2x + 3y = 5$, giving $(x_0,y_0)=(1,1)$, and general solution $x = 1 + 3t$, $y = 1 - 2t$.

---

## Pythagorean Triples

Solutions to $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$. Every primitive triple (pairwise coprime) has the form:

$$x = m^2 - n^2, \qquad y = 2mn, \qquad z = m^2 + n^2$$

for coprime integers $m > n > 0$ of opposite parity.

---

## Fermat's Last Theorem

> [!note] Fermat's Last Theorem
> For $n \geq 3$, the equation $x^n + y^n = z^n$ has no positive integer solutions. Conjectured by Fermat (1637), proved by Wiles (1994) using the modularity of elliptic curves — see [[4.13.8 Elliptic curves]].

---

## Pell's Equation

$$x^2 - Dy^2 = 1, \qquad D \text{ a non-square positive integer}$$

Has infinitely many solutions, generated from the smallest ("fundamental") solution via continued fraction expansion of $\sqrt{D}$.

## Practice Problems

1. Find all integer solutions to $12x + 18y = 30$.
2. Find a primitive Pythagorean triple with $m=5, n=2$.
3. Show that $x^2 + y^2 = 3z^2$ has no nontrivial integer solutions (infinite descent).
4. Find the fundamental solution of $x^2 - 2y^2 = 1$.
5. Why does Fermat's Last Theorem reduce to the case where $n$ is prime or $n=4$?
