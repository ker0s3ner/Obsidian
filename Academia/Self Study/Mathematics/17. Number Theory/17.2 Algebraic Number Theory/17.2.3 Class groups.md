## Definition

The **ideal class group** $Cl(K)$ measures the failure of unique factorization in $\mathcal{O}_K$. It is the quotient:

$$Cl(K) = \frac{\text{fractional ideals of } \mathcal{O}_K}{\text{principal fractional ideals}}$$

Two ideals $I, J$ are equivalent if $I = (\alpha) J$ for some $\alpha \in K^\times$.

> [!tip] Reading the class group
> $Cl(K)$ is trivial **iff** $\mathcal{O}_K$ is a principal ideal domain (equivalently, a UFD, since it's already Dedekind). Its size, the **class number** $h_K$, quantifies how badly factorization fails.

---

## Class Number Finiteness

**Theorem**: $Cl(K)$ is always a **finite** abelian group.

**Example**: $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ has $h_K = 2$ — this is exactly why $6 = 2\cdot3 = (1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})$ gives genuinely different factorizations in [[17.2.2 Ring of integers]]: the ideals $(2)$, $(3)$, $(1\pm\sqrt{-5})$ are non-principal but become principal when squared or combined.

---

## Minkowski Bound

Every ideal class contains an ideal of norm at most the **Minkowski bound**:

$$M_K = \left(\frac{4}{\pi}\right)^{r_2} \frac{n!}{n^n}\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}$$

This gives a finite, computable check: to show $Cl(K)$ is trivial, it suffices to verify every prime ideal of norm $\leq M_K$ is principal.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the Minkowski bound for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$ and use it to find $Cl(K)$.
2. Show that $(2, 1+\sqrt{-5})$ is a non-principal ideal in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$.
3. Explain why class number 1 is equivalent to $\mathcal{O}_K$ being a UFD.
