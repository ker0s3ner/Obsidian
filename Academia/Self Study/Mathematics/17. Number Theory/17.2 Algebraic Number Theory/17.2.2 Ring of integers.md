## Definition

The **ring of integers** $\mathcal{O}_K$ of a number field $K$ is the set of elements of $K$ that are roots of a **monic** polynomial with integer coefficients (algebraic integers).

$$\mathcal{O}_K = \{\alpha \in K : \alpha \text{ satisfies } x^n + c_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \cdots + c_0 = 0, \ c_i \in \mathbb{Z}\}$$

$\mathcal{O}_K$ is a ring, and $\mathcal{O}_K \cap \mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{Z}$.

---

## Examples

| Field | Ring of integers |
|-------|-------------------|
| $\mathbb{Q}$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ |
| $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ | $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ (Gaussian integers) |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ | $\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\right]$, **not** $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt5]$ |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$, $d \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ | $\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1+\sqrt d}{2}\right]$ |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$, $d \equiv 2,3 \pmod 4$ | $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt d]$ |

> [!warning] Not always the "obvious" ring
> For quadratic fields with $d \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, the ring of integers is strictly larger than $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt d]$ — it includes half-integers.

---

## Failure of Unique Factorization

Unlike $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathcal{O}_K$ need not be a UFD.

**Classic example** in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$:

$$6 = 2 \cdot 3 = (1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})$$

Both factorizations are irreducible, and they're genuinely different — no unit relates them. This failure motivates [[17.2.3 Class groups]], which measures exactly how far $\mathcal{O}_K$ is from unique factorization.

---

## Dedekind Domains

$\mathcal{O}_K$ is always a **Dedekind domain**: Noetherian, integrally closed, and every nonzero prime ideal is maximal. Although unique factorization of *elements* can fail, unique factorization of **ideals** into prime ideals always holds.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $1 + \sqrt{-5}$ and $2$ are irreducible in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ but not associates.
2. Show $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ is a UFD (Gaussian integers) using the norm map.
3. Determine $\mathcal{O}_K$ for $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$.
4. Explain why "unique factorization of ideals" rescues $\mathcal{O}_K$ even when element factorization fails.
