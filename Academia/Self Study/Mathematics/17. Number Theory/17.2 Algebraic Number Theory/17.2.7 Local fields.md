## Definition

A **local field** is the completion of a number field $K$ at one of its primes (places) $\mathfrak{p}$, denoted $K_\mathfrak{p}$. For $K = \mathbb{Q}$, these are exactly the $p$-adic fields $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of [[17.4.1 p-adic numbers]] (plus $\mathbb{R}$, the completion at the "infinite place").

---

## Local–Global Principle

Many global questions about $K$ can be studied one prime at a time via its completions $K_\mathfrak{p}$ — a **local** problem — then reassembled. This "local-global" philosophy underlies:

- The **Hasse–Minkowski theorem**: a quadratic form has a rational solution iff it has a solution over every completion (including $\mathbb{R}$)
- Ramification theory: $e_i, f_i$ from [[17.2.5 Ramification]] are read off directly from the extension $K_\mathfrak{p}/\mathbb{Q}_p$

> [!tip] Why "local"
> Working in $K_\mathfrak{p}$ isolates the behavior at a single prime, ignoring all others — analogous to how a local ring in algebraic geometry isolates behavior near one point.
