## Definition

The **$n$-th cyclotomic field** is $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, where $\zeta_n = e^{2\pi i/n}$ is a primitive $n$-th root of unity.

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n) : \mathbb{Q}] = \varphi(n)$$

(Euler's totient — see [[17.1.8 Euler's totient function]]).

---

## Galois Group

$$\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$$

via $\sigma_a(\zeta_n) = \zeta_n^a$ for $a$ coprime to $n$ — a purely abelian Galois group, making cyclotomic fields the simplest nontrivial testing ground in [[15.3 Field Theory#Galois theory|Galois theory]].

---

## Ring of Integers

$$\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)} = \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_n]$$

— unlike general number fields (see [[17.2.2 Ring of integers]]), the ring of integers here is exactly the "obvious" one.

---

## Why They Matter

- **Kronecker–Weber theorem**: every abelian extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ sits inside some $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ — cyclotomic fields generate *all* abelian number fields
- **Fermat's Last Theorem**: Kummer's 19th-century attack used arithmetic of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_p]$, discovering failure of unique factorization exactly where [[17.2.3 Class groups]] measures it
