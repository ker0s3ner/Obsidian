## Splitting Types

A rational prime $p$ in a number field $K$ can:

| Behavior | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **Split completely** | $p\mathcal{O}_K = \mathfrak{p}_1 \cdots \mathfrak{p}_n$, all $e_i=f_i=1$ |
| **Inert** | $p\mathcal{O}_K = \mathfrak{p}$ stays prime, $f = n$ |
| **Ramified** | some $e_i > 1$ |

---

## Quadratic Fields Example

For $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt d)$ and odd prime $p \nmid d$, the splitting is governed by the Legendre symbol from [[17.1.9 Quadratic residues]]:

$$\left(\frac{d}{p}\right) = 1 \implies p \text{ splits}, \qquad \left(\frac{d}{p}\right) = -1 \implies p \text{ is inert}$$

**Example**: in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ ($d=-1$), $p$ splits iff $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, is inert iff $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, and $2$ ramifies.

---

## Frobenius Element

For unramified $p$, the decomposition group acts via a canonical **Frobenius element** $\text{Frob}_p$, whose order equals the residue degree $f$ — this becomes the central object of class field theory and the Langlands program.

## Practice Problems

1. Determine the splitting of $13$ and $19$ in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$.
2. Show $p$ splits completely in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt d)$ iff $d$ is a QR mod $p$.
3. Explain why ramified primes are exactly the divisors of the discriminant (link to [[17.2.5 Ramification]]).
4. For $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$, classify how $2, 3, 5, 7$ each split.
