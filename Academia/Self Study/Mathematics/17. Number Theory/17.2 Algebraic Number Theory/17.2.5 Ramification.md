## Definition

For a number field $K$ with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$, a rational prime $p$ factors in $\mathcal{O}_K$ as a product of prime ideals:

$$p\mathcal{O}_K = \mathfrak{p}_1^{e_1} \cdots \mathfrak{p}_g^{e_g}$$

$e_i$ is the **ramification index** of $\mathfrak{p}_i$. If some $e_i > 1$, $p$ is **ramified**.

---

## Ramification and the Discriminant

> [!example] Key Fact
> $p$ ramifies in $K$ if and only if $p \mid \Delta_K$ (the field discriminant). Since $|\Delta_K| > 1$ for $K \neq \mathbb{Q}$ (Minkowski), **every** nontrivial number field has at least one ramified prime.

**Example**: in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, $\Delta_K = -4$, so only $2$ ramifies: $2 = -i(1+i)^2$, giving $e=2$.

---

## Fundamental Identity

$$\sum_{i=1}^g e_i f_i = n = [K:\mathbb{Q}]$$

where $f_i$ is the **residue degree** $[\mathcal{O}_K/\mathfrak{p}_i : \mathbb{F}_p]$.

## Practice Problems

1. Factor $5$ in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and determine $e, f$ for each prime factor.
2. Show $2$ is the only ramified prime in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$.
3. Verify the fundamental identity for $3$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$.
4. Explain why a totally ramified prime ($g=1$, $e=n$) means $p\mathcal{O}_K = \mathfrak{p}^n$.
