## Definition

A **unit** in $\mathcal{O}_K$ is an element with a multiplicative inverse in $\mathcal{O}_K$. The unit group is written $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$.

---

## Dirichlet's Unit Theorem

> [!example] Dirichlet's Unit Theorem
> $$\mathcal{O}_K^\times \cong \mu_K \times \mathbb{Z}^{r_1 + r_2 - 1}$$
> where $\mu_K$ is the finite group of roots of unity in $K$, and $r_1, r_2$ are the number of real and complex embeddings from [[17.2.1 Number fields]].

The quantity $r_1 + r_2 - 1$ is the **unit rank**.

---

## Examples

| Field | $r_1$ | $r_2$ | Unit rank | Units |
|-------|-------|-------|-----------|-------|
| $\mathbb{Q}$ | 1 | 0 | 0 | $\{\pm 1\}$ |
| $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ | 0 | 1 | 0 | $\{\pm1,\pm i\}$ |
| Real quadratic $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt d)$, $d>0$ | 2 | 0 | 1 | $\pm \varepsilon^n$, fundamental unit $\varepsilon$ |
| Imaginary quadratic $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ | 0 | 1 | 0 | roots of unity only |

**Example** — real quadratic fields have infinite unit groups. For $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2)$, the **fundamental unit** is $\varepsilon = 1 + \sqrt 2$ (norm $-1$), generating all units up to sign: $\pm\varepsilon^n$.

---

## Connection to Pell's Equation

Units of norm $1$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt d)$ correspond exactly to solutions of Pell's equation $x^2 - dy^2 = 1$ (see [[17.1.4 Diophantine equations]]) via $\varepsilon = x + y\sqrt d$.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $r_1 + r_2 - 1 = 0$ for every imaginary quadratic field, and explain why their unit groups are finite.
2. Find the fundamental unit of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 3)$.
3. Show $(1+\sqrt2)(1-\sqrt2) = -1$, confirming $1+\sqrt2$ is a unit.
4. Why does Dirichlet's theorem fail to pin down the unit rank for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]2)$ without first computing $r_1, r_2$?
