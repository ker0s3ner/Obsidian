## Definition

A **number field** $K$ is a finite-degree field extension of $\mathbb{Q}$:

$$[K : \mathbb{Q}] = n < \infty$$

Every number field has the form $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$ for some algebraic number $\alpha$ (primitive element theorem).

---

## Examples

| Field | Description | Degree |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ | Gaussian rationals | 2 |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ | Quadratic field | 2 |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ | Cyclotomic field, $\zeta_n = e^{2\pi i/n}$ | $\varphi(n)$ |
| $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$ | Pure cubic field | 3 |

---

## Embeddings

A number field $K$ of degree $n$ has exactly $n$ embeddings into $\mathbb{C}$, splitting into:

- $r_1$ **real embeddings** (image in $\mathbb{R}$)
- $r_2$ pairs of **complex embeddings** (conjugate pairs)

with $r_1 + 2r_2 = n$.

**Example** — $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$ has $r_1 = 1$, $r_2 = 1$: one real cube root, and a complex conjugate pair.

---

## Discriminant

The **discriminant** $\Delta_K$ measures ramification in $K$; a rational prime $p$ ramifies in $K$ iff $p \mid \Delta_K$ (Minkowski's theorem guarantees $|\Delta_K| > 1$ for $K \neq \mathbb{Q}$, so every number field ramifies somewhere).

> [!tip] Why number fields matter
> They generalize $\mathbb{Q}$ the way finite extensions generalize a base field in [[15.3 Field Theory]] — but arithmetic questions (unique factorization, units, primes) become subtle, motivating [[17.2.2 Ring of integers]] and [[17.2.3 Class groups]].

## Practice Problems

1. Show $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3})$ and find its degree over $\mathbb{Q}$.
2. Determine $r_1, r_2$ for $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$.
3. Show that $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ has exactly two embeddings into $\mathbb{C}$, complex conjugates of each other.
4. Explain why every number field is a simple extension $\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)$.
