## The Big Question

Class field theory classifies the **abelian extensions** of a number field $K$ — extensions $L/K$ with abelian Galois group — entirely in terms of data intrinsic to $K$ itself (its ideals and completions), without ever constructing $L$ directly.

---

## Kronecker–Weber as the Model Case

For $K = \mathbb{Q}$, every abelian extension lies in a cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ (see [[17.2.8 Cyclotomic fields]]). Class field theory generalizes this: for general $K$, abelian extensions correspond to subgroups of a suitable **idele class group**, and unramified abelian extensions correspond exactly to quotients of the ideal class group $Cl(K)$ from [[17.2.3 Class groups]].

---

## Artin Reciprocity

> [!note] Artin Reciprocity Law
> There's a canonical isomorphism between the idele class group of $K$ (modulo a norm subgroup) and $\text{Gal}(L/K)$ for the corresponding abelian extension $L$ — generalizing the Frobenius correspondence of [[17.2.6 Splitting of primes]] to compatible families of primes at once.

---

## Hilbert Class Field

The **Hilbert class field** $H$ of $K$ is the maximal *unramified* abelian extension of $K$, and:

$$\text{Gal}(H/K) \cong Cl(K)$$

— the class group is literally the Galois group of this canonical extension, tying together the ideal-theoretic and Galois-theoretic pictures.

## Practice Problems

1. What is the Hilbert class field of $\mathbb{Q}$? (Consider that $Cl(\mathbb{Q})$ is trivial.)
2. Explain in your own words why Kronecker–Weber is a special case of class field theory.
3. What does it mean for an extension to be "unramified everywhere," and why does this restrict the Hilbert class field to have Galois group exactly $Cl(K)$?
