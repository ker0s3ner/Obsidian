## Definition

Let $X, Y$ be topological spaces. A function $f : X \to Y$ is **continuous** if the preimage of every open set is open:

$$U \subseteq Y \text{ open} \implies f^{-1}(U) \subseteq X \text{ open}$$

This generalizes the $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition from analysis without reference to distance.

---

## Equivalent Characterizations

| Condition | Statement |
|-----------|-----------|
| Open sets | $f^{-1}(U)$ open for every open $U \subseteq Y$ |
| Closed sets | $f^{-1}(C)$ closed for every closed $C \subseteq Y$ |
| Closure | $f(\overline{A}) \subseteq \overline{f(A)}$ for all $A \subseteq X$ |
| Local (metric case) | For every $x$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, there is $\delta > 0$ with $f(B(x,\delta)) \subseteq B(f(x), \varepsilon)$ |

> [!tip] Checking continuity on a basis
> It suffices to check $f^{-1}(B)$ is open for every $B$ in a basis of $Y$ — you don't need to check every open set.

---

## Composition and Restriction

- If $f : X \to Y$ and $g : Y \to Z$ are continuous, so is $g \circ f : X \to Z$.
- If $f : X \to Y$ is continuous and $A \subseteq X$ has the subspace topology, then $f|_A : A \to Y$ is continuous.

---

## Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Confusing preimage with image
> Continuity is stated in terms of **preimages** of open sets, not images. The image of an open set under a continuous map need not be open (e.g. constant maps).

## Practice Problems

1. Show directly from the definition that the identity map $\text{id} : X \to X$ is always continuous.
2. Prove that a constant function is continuous regardless of the topologies on $X$ and $Y$.
3. Show that $f : X \to Y$ is continuous iff $f^{-1}(C)$ is closed for every closed $C \subseteq Y$.
4. Give an example of a continuous bijection that is not a homeomorphism.
5. Prove that continuity is a local property: $f$ is continuous iff it is continuous at each point of $X$.
