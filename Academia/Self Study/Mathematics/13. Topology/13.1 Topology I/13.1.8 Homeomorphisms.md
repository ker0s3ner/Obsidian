## Definition

A **homeomorphism** is a bijection $f : X \to Y$ that is continuous with continuous inverse. If one exists, $X$ and $Y$ are **homeomorphic**, written $X \cong Y$.

Homeomorphic spaces are considered "the same" topologically — a homeomorphism preserves exactly the structure topology cares about.

---

## Examples

> [!example] Some classic homeomorphisms
> - Any open interval $(a,b) \cong \mathbb{R}$ (via a suitable rescaled $\tan$ or logistic map)
> - A square and a disk in $\mathbb{R}^2$ (both homeomorphic to the closed unit disk)
> - A coffee mug and a torus (informally — both have "one hole")

> [!warning] Continuous bijection $\neq$ homeomorphism
> $f : [0, 2\pi) \to S^1$, $f(t) = (\cos t, \sin t)$ is a continuous bijection but **not** a homeomorphism — $f^{-1}$ is not continuous at $(1,0)$, since nearby points on the circle can map back to values near both $0$ and $2\pi$.

## Homeomorphism Invariants

To show $X \not\cong Y$, find a property preserved by homeomorphisms that differs between them:

| Invariant | Example use |
|-----------|-------------|
| Compactness | $[0,1] \not\cong (0,1)$ |
| Connectedness | $[0,1] \not\cong [0,1] \cup [2,3]$ |
| Number of "holes" ($\pi_1$, homology) | Circle $\not\cong$ disk |

This is the starting point of [[13.1.9 Topological invariants]] and, more systematically, [[14. Algebraic Topology]].

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $(0,1) \cong \mathbb{R}$ by exhibiting an explicit homeomorphism.
2. Prove that $[0,1]$ is not homeomorphic to $(0,1)$ using compactness.
3. Show that $f : [0,2\pi) \to S^1$ given by $f(t) = (\cos t, \sin t)$ has a discontinuous inverse.
4. Prove that homeomorphism is an equivalence relation on topological spaces.
5. Show that $\mathbb{R}$ is not homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^2$ (hint: removing a point).
