## Definition

A topological space $X$ is **connected** if it cannot be written as the union of two disjoint nonempty open sets. Equivalently, $X$ has no subset other than $\emptyset$ and $X$ that is both open and closed (**clopen**).

$$X \text{ connected} \iff \nexists\, U, V \text{ open}, \ U \cap V = \emptyset, \ U \cup V = X, \ U, V \neq \emptyset$$

---

## Connected Subsets of $\mathbb{R}$

> [!example] Intervals are exactly the connected subsets of $\mathbb{R}$
> A subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is connected if and only if it is an interval (possibly unbounded, open, closed, or half-open).

This is the topological basis of the **Intermediate Value Theorem**: if $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous and $X$ is connected, $f(X)$ is an interval.

---

## Path-Connectedness

$X$ is **path-connected** if for every $x, y \in X$ there is a continuous path $\gamma : [0,1] \to X$ with $\gamma(0) = x$, $\gamma(1) = y$.

$$\text{path-connected} \implies \text{connected} \qquad \text{(converse fails)}$$

> [!warning] The topologist's sine curve
> $\{(x, \sin(1/x)) : x \in (0,1]\} \cup \{(0,0)\}$ is connected but **not** path-connected — there is no continuous path from $(0,0)$ to points on the curve.

---

## Connected Components

Every space decomposes uniquely into maximal connected subsets, its **connected components**. Components are always closed, but not always open (e.g. $\mathbb{Q}$ has singleton components, none of which are open).

---

## Key Facts

- Continuous images of connected spaces are connected.
- The union of connected sets sharing a common point is connected.
- A product of connected spaces is connected (in the product topology).
