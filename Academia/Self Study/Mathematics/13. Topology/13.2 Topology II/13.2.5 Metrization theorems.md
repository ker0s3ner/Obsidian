## The Metrization Question

Not every topological space arises from a metric. **Metrization theorems** give conditions under which a topology is guaranteed to come from *some* metric.

---

## Urysohn Metrization Theorem

> [!example] Statement
> Every second-countable, regular ($T_3$) space is metrizable.

Since regular + $T_1$ implies normal-adjacent behavior sufficient for Urysohn functions, the proof embeds the space into the countable product $[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}$ (the Hilbert cube) using countably many Urysohn functions separating points from closed sets, and the Hilbert cube is metrizable.

---

## Necessary Conditions

Every metric space is automatically:

- Hausdorff
- First countable
- Normal
- Paracompact

So a space failing any of these **cannot** be metrized — a quick way to rule metrizability out.

---

## Nagata–Smirnov Theorem

A more general (iff) characterization: $X$ is metrizable if and only if $X$ is regular and has a **basis that is $\sigma$-locally finite** (a countable union of locally finite families).

## Practice Problems

1. Show that a non-Hausdorff space cannot be metrizable.
2. Verify that $\mathbb{R}^n$ satisfies the hypotheses of the Urysohn Metrization Theorem.
3. Explain why second countability is a genuinely necessary hypothesis (not just sufficient) in many practical cases.
4. Look up the Nagata–Smirnov theorem statement and compare it to Urysohn's version.
