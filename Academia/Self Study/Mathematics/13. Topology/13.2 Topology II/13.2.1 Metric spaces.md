## Definition

A **metric space** is a pair $(X, d)$ where $d : X \times X \to [0, \infty)$ satisfies, for all $x,y,z \in X$:

1. $d(x,y) = 0 \iff x = y$
2. $d(x,y) = d(y,x)$ (symmetry)
3. $d(x,z) \leq d(x,y) + d(y,z)$ (triangle inequality)

Every metric induces a topology: open sets are unions of open balls $B(x,r) = \{y : d(x,y) < r\}$.

---

## Metric Spaces as Topological Spaces

Not every topology comes from a metric — this failure is exactly what [[13.2.5 Metrization theorems]] characterizes. But every metric topology automatically has strong properties:

| Property | Always holds in metric spaces |
|----------|-------------------------------|
| Hausdorff ($T_2$) | Yes |
| First countable | Yes |
| Normal ($T_4$) | Yes |
| Sequential compactness $=$ compactness | Yes |

> [!tip] Sequences suffice
> In a metric space, closure, continuity, and compactness can all be characterized using sequences — this fails in general topological spaces.

---

## Equivalent Metrics

Two metrics $d_1, d_2$ on $X$ are **topologically equivalent** if they generate the same open sets — they need not be numerically close, only produce the same balls-up-to-containment structure.

**Example**: on $\mathbb{R}^n$, the Euclidean, taxicab, and max metrics are all topologically equivalent.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that $d(x,y) = |x - y|$ satisfies the triangle inequality on $\mathbb{R}$.
2. Show that every metric space is Hausdorff.
3. Prove that the discrete metric $d(x,y) = 1$ for $x \neq y$ induces the discrete topology.
4. Show that the Euclidean and taxicab metrics on $\mathbb{R}^2$ are topologically equivalent.
5. Prove that in a metric space, $x_n \to x$ iff $d(x_n, x) \to 0$.
