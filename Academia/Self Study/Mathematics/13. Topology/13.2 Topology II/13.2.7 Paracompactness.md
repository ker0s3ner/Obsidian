## Definition

A space $X$ is **paracompact** if every open cover admits an **open refinement** that is **locally finite** — a refinement $\mathcal{V}$ such that every point has a neighborhood meeting only finitely many members of $\mathcal{V}$.

$$\text{compact} \implies \text{paracompact} \qquad \text{(converse fails)}$$

---

## Why It Matters

Paracompactness is exactly the condition needed to build a **partition of unity** subordinate to any open cover — a family of continuous functions $\{\rho_i\}$ with $\sum_i \rho_i = 1$, each supported in one cover element.

> [!tip] Partitions of unity
> These let you patch together locally defined objects (bump functions, Riemannian metrics, connections) into globally defined ones — indispensable in differential geometry and manifold theory.

---

## Key Facts

- Every metric space is paracompact (Stone's theorem).
- Every manifold (second countable, Hausdorff, locally Euclidean) is paracompact.
- Compact Hausdorff spaces are automatically paracompact.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that a compact space is trivially paracompact.
2. Explain informally why locally finite refinements let you build a partition of unity.
3. Verify that $\mathbb{R}^n$ with the standard open cover by balls of radius 1 has a locally finite refinement.
4. Look up Stone's theorem and summarize why every metric space is paracompact.
