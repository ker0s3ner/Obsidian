## Product Topology

Given spaces $X_\alpha$ for $\alpha \in A$, the **product topology** on $\prod_\alpha X_\alpha$ is generated by the basis of sets:

$$\prod_\alpha U_\alpha, \qquad U_\alpha \subseteq X_\alpha \text{ open}, \ U_\alpha = X_\alpha \text{ for all but finitely many } \alpha$$

This "finitely many restrictions" clause is essential — it's exactly what makes [[13.2.3 Tychonoff theorem]] true for infinite products.

> [!tip] Characterizing property
> $f : Z \to \prod_\alpha X_\alpha$ is continuous iff each composite $\pi_\alpha \circ f : Z \to X_\alpha$ is continuous, where $\pi_\alpha$ is the projection map.

## Quotient Topology

Given a surjection $q : X \to Y$, the **quotient topology** on $Y$ declares $U \subseteq Y$ open iff $q^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$. Equivalently, $Y = X/\sim$ for an equivalence relation, gluing together equivalent points.

**Example — the circle as a quotient**: $S^1 \cong [0,1]/\!\sim$ where $0 \sim 1$ (glue the endpoints of an interval).

**Example — the torus**: $T^2 \cong [0,1]^2 / \!\sim$ gluing opposite edges of a square.

---

## Common Mistakes

> [!warning] Quotients can lose separation properties
> The quotient of a Hausdorff space need not be Hausdorff — e.g. collapsing a non-closed subset to a point often produces a non-$T_1$ quotient.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that the projection map $\pi_\alpha : \prod X_\beta \to X_\alpha$ is always continuous.
2. Prove that $[0,1]/\{0 \sim 1\}$ is homeomorphic to $S^1$.
3. Describe the quotient space obtained by collapsing the boundary of a disk $D^2$ to a point.
4. Show that a quotient map $q : X \to Y$ is continuous by definition, and give an example where it is not open.
5. Construct the Möbius band as a quotient of $[0,1]^2$.
