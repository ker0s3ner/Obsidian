## Statement

**Tychonoff's Theorem**: An arbitrary product of compact topological spaces is compact in the product topology:

$$X_\alpha \text{ compact for all } \alpha \in A \implies \prod_{\alpha \in A} X_\alpha \text{ compact}$$

This holds for **any** index set $A$, including uncountable ones — the surprising part, since finite products of compact spaces being compact is elementary.

---

## Why It's Remarkable

> [!example] Depends on the Axiom of Choice
> Tychonoff's theorem for arbitrary (possibly infinite) products is **equivalent** to the Axiom of Choice. For finite products, no choice is needed.

The proof typically goes through **ultrafilters** or the **finite intersection property** characterization of compactness rather than open covers directly, since managing open covers of an infinite product combinatorially is intractable.

---

## Applications

- **Banach–Alaoglu theorem**: the closed unit ball of the dual of a normed space is compact in the weak-* topology — proved via Tychonoff applied to a product of copies of $[-1,1]$.
- **Stone–Čech compactification**: embeds any space into a product of intervals, using Tychonoff to guarantee compactness of the target.
- **Compactness of $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$**: the Cantor space, viewed as an infinite product of the compact 2-point discrete space.
