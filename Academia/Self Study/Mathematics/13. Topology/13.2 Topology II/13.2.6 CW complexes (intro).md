## Definition

A **CW complex** is built by attaching cells of increasing dimension:

1. Start with a discrete set of points, the $0$-skeleton $X^0$.
2. Inductively form the $n$-skeleton $X^n$ by attaching $n$-cells $D^n$ to $X^{n-1}$ via continuous maps (attaching maps) $\phi : \partial D^n \to X^{n-1}$.
3. $X = \bigcup_n X^n$, with the topology where a set is closed iff its intersection with every closed cell is closed.

The name stands for **C**losure-finite, **W**eak topology — the two technical conditions this construction satisfies automatically.

---

## Examples

> [!example] Building familiar spaces
> - **Circle $S^1$**: one 0-cell and one 1-cell attached at both endpoints to that point.
> - **Torus $T^2$**: one 0-cell, two 1-cells, one 2-cell attached via $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.
> - **Sphere $S^n$**: one 0-cell and one $n$-cell, attaching map sends the entire boundary $\partial D^n$ to the point.

---

## Why CW Complexes Matter

CW complexes are the standard setting for [[14.2.1 Homology]] computations — cellular homology computes $H_n(X)$ directly from the number and attachment of cells, avoiding the more cumbersome simplicial or singular constructions.

> [!tip] Euler characteristic from cells
> $\chi(X) = \sum_n (-1)^n c_n$, where $c_n$ is the number of $n$-cells — no algebraic topology required to compute it.
