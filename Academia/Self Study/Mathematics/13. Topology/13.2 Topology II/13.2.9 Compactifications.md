## Definition

A **compactification** of $X$ is a compact space $\hat{X}$ containing a dense homeomorphic copy of $X$. Compactifications let non-compact spaces "borrow" the good properties of compact ones (e.g. every continuous function is bounded).

---

## One-Point Compactification

For a locally compact Hausdorff space $X$, add a single point $\infty$; open sets around $\infty$ are complements of compact subsets of $X$.

$$X^+ = X \cup \{\infty\}$$

**Example**: the one-point compactification of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the sphere $S^n$.

---

## Stone–Čech Compactification

The **largest** possible compactification $\beta X$: every bounded continuous $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ extends continuously to $\beta X$. Built by embedding $X$ into a product of intervals (one per bounded continuous function) via Tychonoff's theorem, then taking the closure of the image.

> [!warning] Not the same as one-point compactification
> $\beta X$ can be enormous — $\beta\mathbb{N}$ is uncountable, far bigger than $\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$. One-point compactification adds a single point; Stone–Čech adds "enough room" for every bounded continuous function to extend.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that the one-point compactification of $\mathbb{R}$ is homeomorphic to $S^1$.
2. Show that the one-point compactification of $\mathbb{R}^2$ is homeomorphic to $S^2$.
3. Explain why local compactness of $X$ is needed for the one-point compactification to be Hausdorff.
4. Describe informally why $\beta\mathbb{N}$ is much larger than $\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$.
