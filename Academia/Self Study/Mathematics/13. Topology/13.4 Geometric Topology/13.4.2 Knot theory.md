## Definition

A **knot** is an embedding $S^1 \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^3$ (or $S^3$). Two knots are **equivalent** if one can be continuously deformed into the other without passing through itself (ambient isotopy).

A **link** is a disjoint union of knots.

---

## Reidemeister Moves

Two knot diagrams represent the same knot iff one can be transformed into the other by a finite sequence of three local moves (twist, poke, slide). This reduces the geometric equivalence problem to a combinatorial one on diagrams.

---

## Knot Invariants

To distinguish knots, assign invariants unchanged by Reidemeister moves:

| Invariant | Idea |
|-----------|------|
| Crossing number | Minimum crossings in any diagram |
| Tricolorability | Can diagram arcs be 3-colored per the coloring rule? |
| Jones polynomial | Polynomial invariant from a skein relation |
| Knot group $\pi_1(S^3 \setminus K)$ | Fundamental group of the complement |

> [!example] Trefoil vs. unknot
> The trefoil knot is tricolorable (nontrivially) while the unknot is not — a quick way to prove the trefoil is genuinely knotted.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that the unknot admits only the trivial tricoloring.
2. Verify that the trefoil knot has a nontrivial tricoloring.
3. Explain why crossing number is a knot invariant but depends on more than the diagram alone.
4. Look up the skein relation defining the Jones polynomial and compute it for the unknot.
