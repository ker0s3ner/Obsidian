## The Knot Group

For a knot $K \subset S^3$, the **knot group** is $\pi_1(S^3 \setminus K)$. This algebraic invariant is far more powerful than most others: by the **Gordon–Luecke theorem**, the knot complement (hence the knot group together with peripheral data) determines the knot up to mirror image.

---

## Wirtinger Presentation

Given a knot diagram with $n$ crossings, the **Wirtinger presentation** gives an explicit presentation of the knot group with $n$ generators (one per arc) and $n$ relations (one per crossing), read directly off the diagram.

> [!example] Trefoil knot group
> The trefoil has knot group $\langle a, b \mid aba = bab \rangle$ — nonabelian, distinguishing it immediately from the unknot (whose group is trivial $\mathbb{Z}$... actually $\pi_1(S^3\setminus \text{unknot}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$).

## Practice Problems

1. Write the Wirtinger presentation for the unknot's standard diagram (0 crossings) and verify the group is $\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Explain why the knot group is a stronger invariant than the crossing number.
3. Look up what "peripheral structure" adds to the knot group to fully determine the knot (Gordon–Luecke).
