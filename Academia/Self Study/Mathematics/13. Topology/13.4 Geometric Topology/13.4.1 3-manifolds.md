## Overview

A **3-manifold** is a space locally homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^3$. Unlike surfaces (2-manifolds), which are fully classified by genus and orientability, 3-manifolds admit an enormously richer variety of topologies.

---

## Prime Decomposition

Every closed, orientable 3-manifold decomposes as a **connected sum** of finitely many **prime** 3-manifolds (those not expressible as a nontrivial connected sum):

$$M \cong P_1 \# P_2 \# \cdots \# P_k$$

---

## Geometrization Conjecture (Theorem)

> [!example] Thurston's Geometrization, proved by Perelman
> Every prime 3-manifold can be cut along tori into pieces, each admitting one of eight canonical geometric structures (including hyperbolic, spherical, and Euclidean geometry). This includes the **Poincaré Conjecture** as the special case: every simply connected closed 3-manifold is homeomorphic to $S^3$.

---

## Examples

- $S^3$, $\mathbb{R}^3$, $T^3$ (the 3-torus)
- Lens spaces $L(p,q)$, quotients of $S^3$ by cyclic group actions
- Hyperbolic 3-manifolds, built from ideal tetrahedra (e.g. knot complements)

2. Explain what "prime" means for 3-manifolds by analogy with prime numbers under connected sum.
3. Look up the Poincaré conjecture's statement and explain how it's a special case of geometrization.
