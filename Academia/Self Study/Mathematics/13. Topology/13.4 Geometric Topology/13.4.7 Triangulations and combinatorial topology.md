## Triangulations

A **triangulation** of a space $X$ is a homeomorphism from $X$ to (the underlying space of) a simplicial complex — a combinatorial gluing of simplices along faces. Triangulations give a purely combinatorial handle on a manifold's topology, underlying both homology computation and algorithmic topology.

---

## The Hauptvermutung and Its Failure

> [!warning] Not every space is triangulable, and triangulations aren't unique
> The **Hauptvermutung** ("main conjecture") — that any two triangulations of a manifold are combinatorially equivalent — is **false** in general (Milnor). Every smooth manifold is triangulable, but some topological manifolds (in dimension $\geq 5$) are not triangulable at all.

---

## Algorithmic Topology

Triangulations make problems decidable: given a triangulated 3-manifold, algorithms exist (though not always efficient) to test whether it is $S^3$, unknotted, or satisfies other properties — the foundation of computational 3-manifold topology.
