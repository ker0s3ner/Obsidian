## Classification of Surfaces

Every closed, connected surface is homeomorphic to exactly one of:

| Type | Family | Euler characteristic |
|------|--------|-----------------------|
| Orientable | Sphere $S^2$, or connected sum of $g \geq 1$ tori $\Sigma_g$ | $2 - 2g$ |
| Non-orientable | Connected sum of $k \geq 1$ projective planes $N_k$ | $2 - k$ |

$g$ (or $k$) is the **genus**, informally the number of "handles" (or "crosscaps").

---

## Euler Characteristic

For any CW structure on a surface with $V$ vertices, $E$ edges, $F$ faces:

$$\chi = V - E + F$$

This is a topological invariant (see [[13.1.9 Topological invariants]]) and fully determines a surface together with orientability.

> [!example] Genus from Euler characteristic
> A surface with $\chi = -2$ that is orientable must be $\Sigma_2$ (double torus), since $2 - 2g = -2 \implies g = 2$.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $\chi$ for a cube's surface using its CW structure ($V=8, E=12, F=6$) and verify it matches $S^2$.
2. Show that connected sum decreases Euler characteristic by 2: $\chi(\Sigma_1 \# \Sigma_2) = \chi(\Sigma_1) + \chi(\Sigma_2) - 2$.
3. Determine the genus of the orientable surface with $\chi = -6$.
4. Explain why the classification theorem needs both genus and orientability to pin down a surface.
