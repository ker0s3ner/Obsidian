## Definition

The **mapping class group** of a surface $\Sigma$ is the group of homeomorphisms up to isotopy:

$$\text{MCG}(\Sigma) = \text{Homeo}(\Sigma) / \text{Isotopy}$$

It measures the "symmetries" of a surface that are genuinely distinct as mappings, ignoring continuous deformation.

---

## Dehn Twists

The basic generators of $\text{MCG}(\Sigma_g)$ for $g \geq 1$ are **Dehn twists**: cut along a simple closed curve, twist one side by $2\pi$, and reglue.

> [!example] Torus case
> $\text{MCG}(T^2) \cong \text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$ — every mapping class corresponds to an integer matrix acting on $H_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$.

---

## Why It Matters

Mapping class groups classify the different ways a surface can be "twisted," and are central to Teichmüller theory (moduli of geometric structures on a surface) and 3-manifold topology (via mapping tori).

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that $\text{MCG}(S^2)$ is trivial (up to orientation-reversal).
2. Show that a Dehn twist along a curve bounding a disk is isotopic to the identity.
3. Explain the correspondence between $\text{MCG}(T^2)$ and $\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$ via action on homology.
