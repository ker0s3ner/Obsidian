## Definition

For a smooth map $f : M \to N$ between compact, connected, oriented manifolds of the same dimension, and $q \in N$ a regular value, the **degree** is:

$$\deg(f) = \sum_{p \in f^{-1}(q)} \text{sign}(\det df_p)$$

a signed count of preimages, weighted by whether $f$ preserves or reverses orientation at each point.

---

## Well-Definedness

> [!tip] Independent of the regular value chosen
> Although the formula uses a specific regular value $q$, $\deg(f)$ is the **same** for every regular value — and by Sard's theorem, regular values exist and are generic (see [[13.3.5 Sard's theorem]]).

The degree is also a **homotopy invariant**: homotopic maps have equal degree.

---

## Examples

> [!example] Degree of standard maps
> - $f : S^1 \to S^1$, $f(z) = z^n$ has $\deg(f) = n$
> - The identity map has degree 1
> - A constant map has degree 0
> - The antipodal map on $S^n$ has degree $(-1)^{n+1}$

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the degree of $f(z) = z^2$ on $S^1$ by directly counting preimages of a regular value.
2. Explain why a map of degree $\neq 0$ cannot be homotopic to a constant map.
3. Use the antipodal map's degree to give a topological proof sketch of the fact that $S^{2n}$ has no fixed-point-free involution preserving orientation trivially (hint: relate to the hairy ball theorem).
