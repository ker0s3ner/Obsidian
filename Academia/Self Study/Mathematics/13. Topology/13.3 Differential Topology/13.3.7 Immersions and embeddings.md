## Definitions

Let $f : M \to N$ be smooth.

| Term | Condition |
|------|-----------|
| **Immersion** | $df_p$ is injective for every $p$ |
| **Embedding** | Immersion that is also a homeomorphism onto its image (with subspace topology) |
| **Submersion** | $df_p$ is surjective for every $p$ |

> [!warning] Immersion $\neq$ embedding
> The figure-eight curve is an immersion of $S^1$ into $\mathbb{R}^2$ (injective derivative everywhere) but not injective as a map, and hence not an embedding.

---

## Whitney Embedding Theorem

Every smooth $n$-manifold embeds in $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$. The proof uses Sard's theorem (see [[13.3.5 Sard's theorem]]): start with an embedding into some high-dimensional $\mathbb{R}^N$, then generically project down one dimension at a time, since almost every projection direction avoids creating self-intersections or losing injectivity.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that the standard parametrization of the figure-eight is an immersion but not an embedding.
2. Verify that the inclusion $S^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ is an embedding.
3. Explain informally why generic projections avoid self-intersections, using Sard's theorem.
4. Give an example of an injective immersion that is still not an embedding (hint: consider a spiral approaching a limit circle).
