## Definition

Two closed $n$-manifolds $M_0, M_1$ are **cobordant** if there is a compact $(n+1)$-manifold $W$ with boundary $\partial W = M_0 \sqcup M_1$.

$$M_0 \sim M_1 \iff \exists\, W, \ \partial W = M_0 \sqcup M_1$$

This is an equivalence relation, and its classes form the **cobordism group** $\Omega_n$ under disjoint union.

---

## Examples

> [!example] Basic cobordisms
> - Any manifold $M$ is cobordant to itself, via $W = M \times [0,1]$.
> - Every closed 1-manifold (disjoint union of circles) is cobordant to the empty set, via $W$ = disjoint union of disks.
> - $\mathbb{R}P^2$ is not cobordant to the empty set — it generates a nontrivial class in $\Omega_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.

---

## Connection to Morse Theory

A cobordism $W$ between $M_0$ and $M_1$ can always be built from a Morse function on $W$ with $M_0, M_1$ as level sets — passing critical points corresponds to elementary "surgery" operations that change one manifold into the other. This is the starting point of surgery theory and the classification of manifolds.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that cobordism is an equivalence relation.
2. Verify that $S^n$ is cobordant to the empty set via the ball $D^{n+1}$.
3. Explain, informally, how a Morse function on $W$ decomposes a cobordism into elementary pieces.
