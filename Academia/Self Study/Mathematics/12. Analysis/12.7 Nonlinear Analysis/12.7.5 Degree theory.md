## Topological Degree

For a continuous map $f: \bar\Omega \to \mathbb{R}^n$ ($\Omega$ bounded open) and $y \notin f(\partial\Omega)$, the **degree** $\deg(f,\Omega,y)$ is an integer counting (with sign/multiplicity) how many times $f$ "wraps around" $y$.

---

## Key Properties

| Property | Statement |
|----------|-----------|
| Existence | $\deg(f,\Omega,y) \neq 0 \implies f(x) = y$ has a solution in $\Omega$ |
| Homotopy invariance | Degree is unchanged under homotopies of $f$ avoiding $y$ |
| Additivity | $\deg(f,\Omega,y) = \deg(f,\Omega_1,y)+\deg(f,\Omega_2,y)$ for disjoint $\Omega_1,\Omega_2$ covering the relevant preimages |

> [!example] Proving Brouwer via degree theory
> $\deg(\text{id}, B, 0) = 1$, and homotopy invariance shows any fixed-point-free continuous $f: \bar B \to \bar B$ would force a contradiction — this is one of the cleanest proofs of the [[12.7.1 Fixed point theorems|Brouwer fixed point theorem]].

---

## Leray–Schauder Degree

Extends topological degree to infinite-dimensional Banach spaces for maps of the form $f = I - K$ with $K$ compact — the tool underlying most nonlinear PDE existence results that go beyond variational methods.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $\deg(f,\Omega,0)$ for $f(z) = z^2$ on the unit disk in $\mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}^2$.
2. Use homotopy invariance to argue why a map homotopic to the identity (rel. boundary) must be surjective onto $\Omega$.
3. Explain, at a high level, why Leray–Schauder degree requires $K$ compact rather than just continuous.
