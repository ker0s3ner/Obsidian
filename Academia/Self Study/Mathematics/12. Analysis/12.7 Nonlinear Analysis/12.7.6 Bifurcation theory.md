## The Setup

Consider $F(x,\lambda) = 0$ where $\lambda$ is a parameter. A **bifurcation point** $\lambda_0$ is where the number or stability of solutions changes as $\lambda$ crosses $\lambda_0$.

---

## Implicit Function Theorem — When Bifurcation Cannot Happen

If $D_xF(x_0,\lambda_0)$ is invertible, the implicit function theorem gives a **unique** locally continuous branch of solutions near $(x_0,\lambda_0)$ — bifurcation requires this derivative to be **singular**.

---

## Crandall–Rabinowitz Theorem

> [!example] Simple bifurcation
> If $D_xF(x_0,\lambda_0)$ has a simple (one-dimensional) kernel, satisfying a transversality condition, then $\lambda_0$ is a bifurcation point from which exactly one nontrivial branch of solutions emerges, alongside the trivial branch.

---

## Example: Buckling of a Beam

$-u'' = \lambda \sin u$ with boundary conditions models beam buckling: for $\lambda$ below a critical value only $u=0$ solves the equation; above it, nontrivial buckled solutions bifurcate off.
