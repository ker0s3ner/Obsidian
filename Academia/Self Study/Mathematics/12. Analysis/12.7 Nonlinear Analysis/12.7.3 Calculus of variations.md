## The Euler–Lagrange Equation

To minimize $J(u) = \int_a^b F(x, u(x), u'(x))\,dx$ over functions with fixed endpoints, a minimizer must satisfy:

$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial u} - \frac{d}{dx}\frac{\partial F}{\partial u'} = 0$$

**Derivation**: set $\frac{d}{d\epsilon}J(u+\epsilon\phi)\big|_{\epsilon=0} = 0$ for every admissible variation $\phi$ (vanishing at endpoints), then integrate by parts.

---

## Classic Example: The Brachistochrone

Minimize descent time along a curve between two points under gravity — the Euler–Lagrange equation reduces to the cycloid, historically the problem that launched the calculus of variations (Bernoulli, 1696).

---

## Second-Order Conditions

> [!note] Legendre condition
> A necessary condition for a **minimum** (not just a critical point) is $\frac{\partial^2 F}{\partial (u')^2} \geq 0$ along the extremal — the variational analogue of the second-derivative test.
