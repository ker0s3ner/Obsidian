## Beyond Minimization

The direct method ([[12.7.2 Variational methods]]) finds minimizers, but many functionals arising in PDEs (e.g. semilinear elliptic equations with superlinear growth) are **unbounded below** — there's no minimizer to find, but there are still critical points, found via a "mountain pass" geometry.

---

## Mountain Pass Theorem

If $J$ has $J(0) = 0$, $J(u) \geq \alpha > 0$ on a sphere $\|u\| = r$, and $J(v) < \alpha$ for some $v$ with $\|v\| > r$, then $J$ has a critical point at level:

$$c = \inf_{\gamma \in \Gamma} \max_{t\in[0,1]} J(\gamma(t)) \geq \alpha$$

where $\Gamma$ is the set of paths from $0$ to $v$ — geometrically, $c$ is the height of the lowest "pass" between the two valleys.

---

## The Palais–Smale Condition

> [!tip] The compactness substitute
> Since minimax sequences aren't automatically compact, the **Palais–Smale condition** (bounded sequences with $J(u_n)$ bounded and $J'(u_n)\to0$ have a convergent subsequence) plays the role that compactness of $K$ played in Schauder's theorem — it's the standard hypothesis that makes minimax arguments actually produce a critical point.
