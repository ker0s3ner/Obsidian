## The Variational Idea

Many nonlinear PDEs are the Euler–Lagrange equation of some **energy functional** $J: X \to \mathbb{R}$ on a function space $X$. Instead of solving the PDE directly, find a **critical point** of $J$ — often its minimizer.

**Example**: $-\Delta u = f$ is the Euler–Lagrange equation of $J(u) = \frac12\int|\nabla u|^2 - \int fu$; minimizing $J$ over $H^1_0(\Omega)$ produces a weak solution.

---

## Direct Method of the Calculus of Variations

1. Show $J$ is bounded below and take a minimizing sequence $u_n$
2. Show $(u_n)$ has a subsequence converging (typically weakly) to some $u$
3. Show $J$ is **weakly lower semicontinuous**: $J(u) \leq \liminf J(u_n)$
4. Conclude $u$ is a minimizer

> [!tip] Why weak convergence, not strong
> Minimizing sequences are usually only bounded, not compact in norm ([[12.4 Functional Analysis/12.4.1 Normed spaces/12.4.1.3 Finite-dimensional normed spaces]]) — but bounded sequences in reflexive spaces *are* weakly sequentially compact, which is exactly enough for step 2.

## Practice Problems

1. Derive the Euler–Lagrange equation for $J(u) = \frac12\int_0^1 (u')^2\,dx$.
2. Explain why weak lower semicontinuity, not weak continuity, is the right property needed for the direct method.
3. Give an example of a functional that is bounded below but has no minimizer (non-coercive case).
