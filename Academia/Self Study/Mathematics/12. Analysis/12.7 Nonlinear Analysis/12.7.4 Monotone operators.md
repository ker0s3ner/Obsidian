## Definition

An operator $A: H \to H$ on a Hilbert space is **monotone** if:

$$\langle Ax - Ay, x-y\rangle \geq 0 \quad \text{for all } x,y \in H$$

It is **strictly monotone** if strict inequality holds for $x\neq y$, and **strongly monotone** if $\langle Ax-Ay,x-y\rangle \geq c\|x-y\|^2$ for some $c>0$.

---

## Why This Generalizes Convexity

If $J$ is a convex differentiable functional, its gradient $\nabla J$ is monotone — monotone operator theory is exactly the right nonlinear generalization of "derivative of a convex function," playing the role that positive-semidefinite matrices play in linear theory.

---

## Existence via Monotonicity

> [!example] Browder–Minty theorem
> If $A: H \to H$ is continuous, monotone, and **coercive** ($\langle Ax,x\rangle/\|x\| \to \infty$ as $\|x\|\to\infty$), then $A$ is surjective — the equation $Ax = f$ has a solution for every $f \in H$.

This is a nonlinear generalization of the Lax–Milgram theorem, and underlies existence theory for nonlinear elliptic PDEs.
