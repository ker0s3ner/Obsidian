## The Abstract Cauchy Problem

$$\frac{du}{dt} + Au = 0, \qquad u(0) = u_0$$

for a (possibly nonlinear) operator $A$ on a Banach space. When $A$ is linear and bounded, the solution is $u(t) = e^{-tA}u_0$; nonlinear semigroup theory extends this to unbounded, nonlinear $A$.

---

## Nonlinear Semigroups

A family $\{S(t)\}_{t\geq0}$ of maps is a **nonlinear contraction semigroup** if $S(0) = I$, $S(t+s) = S(t)S(s)$, and $\|S(t)x - S(t)y\| \leq \|x-y\|$. The **Crandall–Liggett theorem** constructs such a semigroup for $-A$ **m-accretive** (the Banach-space generalization of monotone, from [[12.7.4 Monotone operators]]) via an implicit-Euler limit:

$$S(t)u_0 = \lim_{n\to\infty} \left(I + \frac{t}{n}A\right)^{-n} u_0$$

> [!example] Why this matters
> This machinery gives well-posedness for nonlinear PDEs like the porous medium equation and certain conservation laws, where classical ODE theory in Banach space doesn't directly apply because $A$ is unbounded and nonlinear.

2. Explain the analogy between $(I + \frac{t}{n}A)^{-n}$ here and $(1+\frac{t}{n}a)^{-n} \to e^{-ta}$ for scalars.
