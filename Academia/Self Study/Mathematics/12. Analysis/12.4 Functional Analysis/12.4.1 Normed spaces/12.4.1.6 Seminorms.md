## Definition

A **seminorm** $p: X \to [0,\infty)$ satisfies homogeneity and the triangle inequality but **not** positivity — $p(x) = 0$ is allowed for $x \neq 0$:

$$p(\lambda x) = |\lambda| p(x), \qquad p(x+y) \leq p(x) + p(y)$$

---

## From Seminorm to Norm

Given a seminorm $p$, let $N = \{x : p(x) = 0\}$. Then $N$ is a subspace, and $p$ descends to a genuine norm on the quotient $X/N$:

$$\|x + N\| := p(x)$$

**Example**: on $L^p(\mu)$ before quotienting by a.e.-equality, $p(f) = \left(\int |f|^p\right)^{1/p}$ is only a seminorm — functions equal a.e. but not everywhere have $p(f-g) = 0$. Quotienting gives the true $L^p$ norm.

---

## Role in the Hahn-Banach Theorem

Seminorms are the natural "control function" in [[12.4.6 Hahn-Banach theorem]]: a linear functional dominated by a seminorm on a subspace can always be extended, dominated by the same seminorm, to the whole space.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $p(f) = |f(0)|$ is a seminorm but not a norm on $C[0,1]$.
2. Verify that $N = \{x : p(x) = 0\}$ is a subspace for any seminorm $p$.
3. Show the quotient construction gives a well-defined norm on $X/N$.
