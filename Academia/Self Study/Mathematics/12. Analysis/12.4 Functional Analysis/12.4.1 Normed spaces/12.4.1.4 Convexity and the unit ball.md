## The Unit Ball Is Convex

For any normed space $X$, the closed unit ball $B = \{x : \|x\| \leq 1\}$ is **convex**: for $x, y \in B$ and $t \in [0,1]$,

$$\|tx + (1-t)y\| \leq t\|x\| + (1-t)\|y\| \leq 1$$

directly from the triangle inequality and homogeneity.

---

## Strict Convexity

A norm is **strictly convex** if the unit sphere contains no line segments — equivalently, $\|x\| = \|y\| = 1$ and $x \neq y$ implies $\|\tfrac{x+y}{2}\| < 1$.

| Norm | Strictly convex? |
|------|-------------------|
| $\|\cdot\|_2$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ | Yes |
| $\|\cdot\|_1$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ | No — the unit ball has flat faces |
| $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ | No |

> [!tip] Why it matters
> Strict convexity gives **uniqueness of best approximation**: in a strictly convex space, the closest point in a convex set to a given point is unique — the key fact behind [[12.4.3.3 Projection theorem]] in Hilbert spaces.

## Practice Problems

1. Draw the unit balls of $\|\cdot\|_1$, $\|\cdot\|_2$, $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and identify which are strictly convex.
2. Prove that Hilbert space norms (from an inner product) are always strictly convex.
3. Show that in a strictly convex normed space, if $\|x\| = \|y\| = \|\frac{x+y}{2}\|$ then $x = y$.
