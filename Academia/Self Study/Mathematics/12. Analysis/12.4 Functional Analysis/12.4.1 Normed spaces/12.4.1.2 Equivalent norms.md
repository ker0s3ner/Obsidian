## Definition

Two norms $\|\cdot\|_a$ and $\|\cdot\|_b$ on $X$ are **equivalent** if there exist constants $0 < c \leq C$ such that:

$$c\|x\|_a \leq \|x\|_b \leq C\|x\|_a \quad \text{for all } x \in X$$

Equivalent norms induce the same topology — the same open sets, same convergent sequences, same continuous functions.

---

## Finite Dimensions: All Norms Are Equivalent

> [!example] Key theorem
> On a **finite-dimensional** vector space, all norms are equivalent.

This is why in $\mathbb{R}^n$ it rarely matters which norm you use for convergence or continuity arguments — $\|\cdot\|_1$, $\|\cdot\|_2$, $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ all agree on which sequences converge.

**Proof sketch**: compare any norm to $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ using a basis; compactness of the unit sphere in finite dimensions gives the bounds $c, C$.

---

## Infinite Dimensions: Norms Can Differ

In infinite-dimensional spaces, inequivalent norms are common and consequential.

**Example** — on $C[0,1]$, compare $\|f\|_\infty = \sup|f|$ and $\|f\|_1 = \int_0^1 |f|$. We have $\|f\|_1 \leq \|f\|_\infty$, but there's no constant $C$ with $\|f\|_\infty \leq C\|f\|_1$ — take $f_n$ spiking to height $n$ on a shrinking interval: $\|f_n\|_1 \to 0$ while $\|f_n\|_\infty = n \to \infty$.

> [!warning] Consequence
> A sequence can converge in one norm and diverge in another. Always specify which norm "convergence" refers to on infinite-dimensional spaces.

## Practice Problems

1. Prove that $\|x\|_1$ and $\|x\|_\infty$ are equivalent on $\mathbb{R}^n$, finding explicit constants.
2. Construct a sequence in $C[0,1]$ that converges in $\|\cdot\|_1$ but not in $\|\cdot\|_\infty$.
3. Show that equivalent norms have the same Cauchy sequences.
4. Why does the finite-dimensional equivalence theorem fail to extend to $\ell^p$ spaces?
