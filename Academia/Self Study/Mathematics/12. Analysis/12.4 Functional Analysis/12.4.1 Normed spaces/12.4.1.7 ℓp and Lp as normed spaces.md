## The ℓp Family

For $1 \leq p < \infty$:

$$\ell^p = \left\{ (x_n)_{n=1}^\infty : \sum_{n=1}^\infty |x_n|^p < \infty \right\}, \qquad \|x\|_p = \left(\sum_n |x_n|^p\right)^{1/p}$$

and $\ell^\infty$ is bounded sequences with $\|x\|_\infty = \sup_n |x_n|$.

| Relation | Statement |
|----------|-----------|
| Inclusion | $\ell^p \subseteq \ell^q$ for $p \leq q$ (opposite of $L^p[0,1]$ inclusions) |
| Density | Finitely-supported sequences are dense in $\ell^p$ for $p < \infty$, not in $\ell^\infty$ |

> [!note] Why the inclusion direction flips vs $L^p[0,1]$
> On $[0,1]$ (finite measure), $L^q \subseteq L^p$ for $q \geq p$ — integrability gets easier as $p$ shrinks. On $\mathbb{N}$ with counting measure, summability gets easier as $p$ *grows*, since large exponents suppress the many small terms.

## Norm Inequalities

**Hölder's inequality**: for conjugate exponents $\frac1p + \frac1q = 1$,

$$\sum_n |x_n y_n| \leq \|x\|_p \|y\|_q$$

**Minkowski's inequality** (the triangle inequality for $\|\cdot\|_p$): $\|x+y\|_p \leq \|x\|_p + \|y\|_p$.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $\ell^1 \subseteq \ell^2 \subseteq \ell^\infty$, with strict inclusions.
2. Verify Hölder's inequality for $x = (1, 1/2, 1/4, \ldots)$, $p = q = 2$.
3. Show that the sequence $x_n = 1/n$ lies in $\ell^p$ for $p > 1$ but not $\ell^1$.
