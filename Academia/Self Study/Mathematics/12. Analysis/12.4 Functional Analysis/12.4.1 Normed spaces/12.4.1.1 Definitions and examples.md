## Definition

A **norm** on a vector space $X$ over $\mathbb{K}$ ($\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$) is a function $\|\cdot\| : X \to [0,\infty)$ satisfying:

| Axiom | Statement |
|-------|-----------|
| Positivity | $\|x\| \geq 0$, and $\|x\| = 0 \iff x = 0$ |
| Homogeneity | $\|\lambda x\| = |\lambda| \, \|x\|$ |
| Triangle inequality | $\|x + y\| \leq \|x\| + \|y\|$ |

A **normed space** is a pair $(X, \|\cdot\|)$. Every norm induces a metric $d(x,y) = \|x-y\|$, so every normed space is a metric space.

---

## Standard Examples

| Space | Norm |
|-------|------|
| $\mathbb{R}^n$ | $\|x\|_p = \left(\sum_i |x_i|^p\right)^{1/p}$, $\|x\|_\infty = \max_i |x_i|$ |
| $\ell^p$ | $\|x\|_p = \left(\sum_{i=1}^\infty |x_i|^p\right)^{1/p}$, $1 \leq p < \infty$ |
| $C[a,b]$ | $\|f\|_\infty = \sup_{t \in [a,b]} |f(t)|$ |
| $L^p(\mu)$ | $\|f\|_p = \left(\int |f|^p \, d\mu\right)^{1/p}$ |

> [!example] Why the triangle inequality is the hard axiom
> For $\ell^p$ and $L^p$ norms, the triangle inequality is exactly **Minkowski's inequality** — it's not automatic and requires $p \geq 1$.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the three norm axioms for $\|x\|_\infty = \max_i |x_i|$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$.
2. Show that $\|f\|_1 = \int_0^1 |f(t)| \, dt$ defines a norm on $C[0,1]$.
3. Prove that every norm satisfies the reverse triangle inequality $|\|x\| - \|y\|| \leq \|x - y\|$.
4. Show that $d(x,y) = \|x - y\|$ satisfies the metric axioms whenever $\|\cdot\|$ is a norm.
