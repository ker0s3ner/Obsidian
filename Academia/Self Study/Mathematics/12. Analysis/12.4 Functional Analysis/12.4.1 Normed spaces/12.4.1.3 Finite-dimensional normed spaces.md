## Key Facts

Every finite-dimensional normed space $X$ over $\mathbb{K}$ shares three properties, none of which extend to infinite dimensions automatically:

1. **All norms are equivalent** (see [[12.4.1.2 Equivalent norms]])
2. $X$ is **complete** — hence automatically a Banach space
3. The closed unit ball is **compact**

---

## Compactness Characterizes Finite Dimension

> [!example] Riesz's Lemma / Compactness Theorem
> The closed unit ball of a normed space $X$ is compact **if and only if** $X$ is finite-dimensional.

This is the deepest of the three facts — it's the reason infinite-dimensional analysis needs entirely different tools (weak topologies, compact operators) instead of relying on sequential compactness.

---

## Consequence: Linear Maps Are Automatically Bounded

If $X$ is finite-dimensional, **every** linear map $T: X \to Y$ is bounded/continuous, regardless of the norm chosen on $X$. This fails in infinite dimensions — see [[12.4.4.1 Operator norm]] for unbounded operators.

## Practice Problems

1. Prove that a finite-dimensional normed space is complete, using equivalence of norms to $\|\cdot\|_\infty$.
2. Show directly that every linear functional on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is continuous.
3. Use Riesz's Lemma to show $\ell^2$ is infinite-dimensional by exhibiting a bounded sequence with no convergent subsequence.
