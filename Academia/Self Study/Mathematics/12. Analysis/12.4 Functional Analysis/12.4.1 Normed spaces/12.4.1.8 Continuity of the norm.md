## The Norm Is a Continuous Function

For any normed space $X$, the map $x \mapsto \|x\|$ is continuous — in fact **Lipschitz** with constant 1, by the reverse triangle inequality:

$$\big|\|x\| - \|y\|\big| \leq \|x - y\|$$

Consequently, if $x_n \to x$, then $\|x_n\| \to \|x\|$.

---

## Continuity of Vector Space Operations

Addition $+: X \times X \to X$ and scalar multiplication $\cdot: \mathbb{K} \times X \to X$ are jointly continuous with respect to the norm topology:

$$x_n \to x,\ y_n \to y \implies x_n + y_n \to x + y$$
$$\lambda_n \to \lambda,\ x_n \to x \implies \lambda_n x_n \to \lambda x$$

> [!example] Why this matters
> Together, these facts make every normed space a **topological vector space** — limits interact correctly with the algebraic structure, which is what allows calculus-style arguments (limits of sums, scalar multiples) to carry over from $\mathbb{R}^n$.

## Practice Problems

1. Prove the reverse triangle inequality $|\|x\|-\|y\|| \leq \|x-y\|$ from the norm axioms.
2. Show that $x_n \to x$ and $y_n \to y$ implies $x_n + y_n \to x+y$, directly from the definition of convergence.
3. Give an example showing $\|x_n\| \to \|x\|$ does **not** imply $x_n \to x$.
