## Statement (Banach–Steinhaus)

If $X$ is Banach, $Y$ normed, and $\{T_i\} \subseteq \mathcal{B}(X,Y)$ is pointwise bounded ($\sup_i \|T_ix\| < \infty$ for each $x$), then $\{T_i\}$ is uniformly bounded: $\sup_i \|T_i\| < \infty$.

See the full proof and Baire category context at [[12.4.2.8 Uniform Boundedness Principle]] — this file focuses on its use as an **operator-theoretic tool**.

---

## Operator-Theoretic Applications

**Testing strong convergence of operators**: if $T_n \to T$ strongly (i.e. $T_nx \to Tx$ for every $x$), then $(T_n)$ is automatically bounded in operator norm — a pointwise-convergent sequence of bounded operators cannot have $\|T_n\| \to \infty$.

**Banach–Steinhaus continuity test**: a pointwise limit of bounded operators, if it exists everywhere on a Banach space, is itself a bounded linear operator.

> [!warning] Strong convergence ≠ norm convergence
> $T_n \to T$ pointwise does not imply $\|T_n - T\| \to 0$. Uniform boundedness only bounds the operators; it doesn't make the convergence uniform.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that if $T_n \to T$ pointwise on a Banach space, $T$ is linear and bounded.
2. Construct operators $T_n$ on $\ell^2$ with $T_n \to 0$ pointwise but $\|T_n\| = 1$ for all $n$.
3. Explain why completeness of $X$ (not $Y$) is the hypothesis needed here.
