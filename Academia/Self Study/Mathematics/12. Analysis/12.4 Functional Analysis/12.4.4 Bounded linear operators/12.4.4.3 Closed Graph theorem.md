## Statement

Let $X, Y$ be Banach spaces and $T: X \to Y$ linear. If the **graph** of $T$,

$$\Gamma(T) = \{(x, Tx) : x \in X\} \subseteq X \times Y$$

is **closed**, then $T$ is bounded.

---

## Why This Is Useful

Directly checking boundedness ($\|Tx\| \leq M\|x\|$) can be hard. Checking closedness of the graph only requires: if $x_n \to x$ **and** $Tx_n \to y$, then $y = Tx$. This "separates" the two convergence assumptions, which is often much easier to verify — especially for differential operators.

> [!example] Differential operators
> $T = \frac{d}{dx}$ on a suitable domain in $L^2$ is typically **unbounded** but still **closed** — the Closed Graph Theorem doesn't apply because $T$ isn't defined on all of $X$, which is exactly why unbounded closed operators need their own theory (see [[16. Specialized Topics/Spectral Theory]]).

---

## Relation to Open Mapping Theorem

The Closed Graph Theorem is proved as a corollary of the [[12.4.4.2 Open Mapping theorem]] applied to the projection $X \times Y \to X$ restricted to $\Gamma(T)$ (itself Banach, since it's closed in the Banach space $X\times Y$).

## Practice Problems

1. Show that if $T$ is continuous, its graph is automatically closed.
2. Verify that $\frac{d}{dx}: \{f \in C^1[0,1]\} \subseteq C[0,1] \to C[0,1]$ has a closed graph but is unbounded with respect to $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on its domain.
3. Prove the Closed Graph Theorem from the Open Mapping Theorem.
