## Definition

$T \in \mathcal{B}(X,Y)$ is **compact** if $T(B_X)$ has compact closure in $Y$, where $B_X$ is the closed unit ball of $X$. Equivalently, every bounded sequence $(x_n)$ has $(Tx_n)$ with a convergent subsequence.

---

## General Banach Space Facts

- Compact operators form a **closed** two-sided ideal in $\mathcal{B}(X)$: sums and compositions (with any bounded operator) of compact operators are compact
- A compact operator on an infinite-dimensional space is **never surjective** onto the whole space (else the identity would be compact, contradicting non-compactness of the unit ball, [[12.4.1.3 Finite-dimensional normed spaces]])
- $I - T$ for $T$ compact satisfies the **Fredholm alternative**: either $I - T$ is injective (hence bijective with bounded inverse), or $\ker(I-T)$ is finite-dimensional and $I-T$ has closed, finite-codimensional range

---

## Fredholm Alternative

> [!example] Practical use
> This is exactly how existence/uniqueness for many integral equations $f = g + Tf$ (with $T$ compact) is settled: either the homogeneous equation $f = Tf$ has only $f=0$ (then $f = g+Tf$ always has a unique solution), or the solution space is finite-dimensional and solvability depends on a finite-dimensional condition on $g$.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that if $T$ is compact and $S$ is bounded, both $ST$ and $TS$ are compact.
2. Prove that a compact operator on an infinite-dimensional Banach space cannot be invertible.
3. Explain how the Fredholm alternative resolves solvability of $f(x) - \int_0^1 k(x,y)f(y)\,dy = g(x)$.
