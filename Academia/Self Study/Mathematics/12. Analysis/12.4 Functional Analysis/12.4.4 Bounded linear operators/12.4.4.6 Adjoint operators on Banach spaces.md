## Definition

For $T \in \mathcal{B}(X,Y)$ between Banach spaces, the **Banach-space adjoint** $T^*: Y^* \to X^*$ is defined by:

$$(T^*\varphi)(x) = \varphi(Tx), \qquad \varphi \in Y^*, \ x \in X$$

Unlike the Hilbert-space adjoint of [[12.4.3.5 Adjoint and self-adjoint operators]] (which maps $H \to H$ via the inner product), $T^*$ here maps between the **dual spaces**, since general Banach spaces have no inner product to identify $X$ with $X^*$.

---

## Key Properties

$$\|T^*\| = \|T\|, \qquad (ST)^* = T^*S^*, \qquad (T^*)^* \text{ acts on } X^{**} \supseteq X$$

---

## Relation to the Hilbert Case

When $X = Y = H$ is a Hilbert space, the Riesz representation theorem identifies $H^* \cong H$, and under this identification the Banach-space adjoint becomes exactly the Hilbert-space adjoint.

> [!tip] Duality as a bridge
> This is the general pattern in functional analysis: Hilbert space results are often special cases of Banach space results, made concrete because $H \cong H^*$.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\|T^*\| = \|T\|$ directly from the definition.
2. Compute $T^*$ for the shift operator $T(x_1,x_2,\ldots) = (0,x_1,x_2,\ldots)$ on $\ell^1$ (whose dual is $\ell^\infty$).
3. Explain why $(ST)^* = T^*S^*$ (note the order reversal).
