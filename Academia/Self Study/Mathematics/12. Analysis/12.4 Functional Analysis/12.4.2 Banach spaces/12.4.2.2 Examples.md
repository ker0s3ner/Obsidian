## Key Banach Spaces

| Space | Norm | Complete? |
|-------|------|-----------|
| $\mathbb{R}^n, \mathbb{C}^n$ | any norm | Yes (finite-dim.) |
| $\ell^p$, $1 \leq p \leq \infty$ | $\|x\|_p$ | Yes |
| $C[a,b]$ | $\|f\|_\infty$ | Yes |
| $C[a,b]$ | $\|f\|_1$ | No |
| $L^p(\mu)$, $1 \leq p \leq \infty$ | $\|f\|_p$ | Yes (Riesz–Fischer) |
| $c_0$ (sequences $\to 0$) | $\|x\|_\infty$ | Yes |

---

## Riesz–Fischer Theorem

$L^p(\mu)$ is complete for every $1 \leq p \leq \infty$ and every measure $\mu$ — this is the theorem that makes $L^p$ spaces the natural setting for functional analysis rather than raw $C[a,b]$.

---

## Not Every Banach Space Is a Hilbert Space

$\ell^p$ for $p \neq 2$ and $C[a,b]$ are Banach but admit **no** inner product inducing their norm — they fail the parallelogram law (see [[12.4.3.1 Inner product spaces]]).

> [!tip] Quick test
> A norm comes from an inner product iff $\|x+y\|^2 + \|x-y\|^2 = 2\|x\|^2 + 2\|y\|^2$ for all $x,y$. Check $\|\cdot\|_1$ or $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ and this fails immediately.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the parallelogram law fails for $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, showing it's not Hilbert.
2. Show $c_0$ is a closed subspace of $\ell^\infty$, hence Banach.
3. Explain why completeness of $L^p$ is nontrivial compared to completeness of $\ell^p$.
