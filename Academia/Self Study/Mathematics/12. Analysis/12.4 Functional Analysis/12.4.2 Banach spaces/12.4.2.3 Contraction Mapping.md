## Banach Fixed Point Theorem

Let $X$ be a complete metric space and $T: X \to X$ a **contraction**: there exists $k \in [0,1)$ with

$$d(Tx, Ty) \leq k\, d(x,y) \quad \text{for all } x, y \in X$$

Then $T$ has a **unique** fixed point $x^* = Tx^*$, and for any $x_0 \in X$, the iterates $x_{n+1} = Tx_n$ converge to $x^*$ with

$$d(x_n, x^*) \leq \frac{k^n}{1-k} d(x_1, x_0)$$

---

## Why Completeness Is Essential

The proof shows $(x_n)$ is Cauchy (via the geometric bound on consecutive distances) — **completeness** is exactly what guarantees this Cauchy sequence converges. On an incomplete space, a contraction can have no fixed point at all.

---

## Applications

- **Picard–Lindelöf theorem**: existence/uniqueness of solutions to $y' = f(x,y)$, $y(x_0)=y_0$, via the contraction $Ty(x) = y_0 + \int_{x_0}^x f(t,y(t))\,dt$ on $C[a,b]$
- **Newton's method** convergence analysis
- Numerical solution of linear systems via iterative methods

> [!example] Worked example
> Solve $x = \cos x$ by iterating $x_{n+1} = \cos x_n$. Since $|\cos'(x)| = |\sin x| \leq 1$ with strict inequality on any closed interval avoiding $0$, $T$ is a contraction near the fixed point, and iteration converges rapidly.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $T(x) = \frac{1}{2}(x + \frac{2}{x})$ on $[1,2]$ is a contraction, and identify its fixed point.
2. Use the contraction mapping theorem to prove existence/uniqueness for $y' = -y$, $y(0) = 1$ on a small interval.
3. Give an example of a non-complete space where a contraction has no fixed point.
4. Estimate how many iterations of $x_{n+1} = \cos x_n$ are needed to get within $10^{-6}$ of the fixed point, given $k \approx 0.85$.
