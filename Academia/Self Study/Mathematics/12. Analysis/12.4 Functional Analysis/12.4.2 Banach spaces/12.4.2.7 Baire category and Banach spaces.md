## Baire Category Theorem

In a complete metric space, a countable intersection of dense open sets is dense. Equivalently: a complete metric space cannot be written as a countable union of closed sets with empty interior.

---

## Why It Underpins Functional Analysis

Baire category is the hidden engine behind the "big three" Banach space theorems:

| Theorem | Baire category role |
|---------|----------------------|
| Uniform Boundedness Principle | pointwise bounds force a uniform bound on some ball via a Baire category argument |
| Open Mapping Theorem | surjectivity + completeness + Baire ⟹ openness |
| Closed Graph Theorem | corollary of Open Mapping |

> [!example] A concrete consequence
> No infinite-dimensional Banach space has a **countable** Hamel (vector-space) basis — otherwise it would be a countable union of finite-dimensional (closed, empty-interior) subspaces, contradicting Baire.

## Practice Problems

1. State the contrapositive form of the Baire Category Theorem in terms of unions of closed sets.
2. Show that $\mathbb{Q}$, as a countable union of singletons, does not contradict Baire category (why doesn't it need to be complete... or does it fail to be a counterexample?).
3. Prove that an infinite-dimensional Banach space cannot have a countable Hamel basis.
