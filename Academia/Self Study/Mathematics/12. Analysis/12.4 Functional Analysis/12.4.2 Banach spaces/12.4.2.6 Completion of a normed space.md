## Completion Theorem

Every normed space $X$ has a **completion** $\hat{X}$: a Banach space containing (an isometric copy of) $X$ as a dense subspace, unique up to isometric isomorphism.

**Construction**: $\hat X$ is built from equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences in $X$, exactly as $\mathbb{R}$ is built from Cauchy sequences of rationals.

---

## Examples

| Incomplete space | Completion |
|-------------------|------------|
| $\mathbb{Q}$ | $\mathbb{R}$ |
| $C[0,1]$ with $\|\cdot\|_1$ | $L^1[0,1]$ |
| Simple/step functions with $\|\cdot\|_p$ | $L^p[0,1]$ |
| Polynomials with $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on $[a,b]$ | $C[a,b]$ (Weierstrass approximation) |

> [!tip] Why $L^p$ is defined the way it is
> $L^p$ spaces are most naturally understood as the completion of continuous (or simple) functions under $\|\cdot\|_p$ — this is why every $L^p$ function is a limit of nicer functions.

## Practice Problems

1. Describe the completion of the rationals with $|\cdot|$ and verify it satisfies the completion theorem's properties.
2. Explain why completing polynomials under $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on $[a,b]$ gives all of $C[a,b]$.
3. Show that the completion of a normed space is unique up to isometric isomorphism.
