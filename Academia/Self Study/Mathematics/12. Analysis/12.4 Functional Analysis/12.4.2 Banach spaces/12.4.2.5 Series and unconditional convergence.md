## Absolute vs Unconditional Convergence

A series $\sum x_n$ in a Banach space is:

- **Absolutely convergent** if $\sum \|x_n\| < \infty$
- **Unconditionally convergent** if $\sum x_{\sigma(n)}$ converges to the same limit for every permutation $\sigma$

In finite dimensions these coincide (Riemann rearrangement-type results), but in infinite dimensions absolute convergence always implies unconditional convergence, while the converse can fail.

---

## Dvoretzky–Rogers Theorem

> [!note] Sharp contrast with finite dimensions
> In every infinite-dimensional Banach space, there exists an unconditionally convergent series that is **not** absolutely convergent — unlike $\mathbb{R}^n$, where the two notions always agree.

## Practice Problems

1. Show absolute convergence implies unconditional convergence in any Banach space.
2. In $\ell^2$, show $\sum_n \frac{1}{n} e_n$ converges unconditionally but not absolutely (where $e_n$ is the standard basis vector).
3. Explain why finite-dimensionality is what forces the two notions to coincide.
