## Definition

$T \in \mathcal{B}(H)$ is **compact** if it maps bounded sets to sets with compact closure — equivalently, $T$ maps every bounded sequence to a sequence with a norm-convergent subsequence.

Compact operators are the infinite-dimensional analogue of finite-rank/finite-dimensional maps, and are approximable: $T$ is compact iff it is a norm-limit of finite-rank operators (true on Hilbert space).

---

## Spectral Theorem for Compact Self-Adjoint Operators

> [!example] Statement
> If $T$ is compact and self-adjoint, $H$ has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of $T$, with eigenvalues $\lambda_n \to 0$ (if infinitely many are nonzero).

This is the direct generalization of diagonalizing a symmetric matrix, and it underlies the existence of eigenfunction expansions used throughout PDE theory (e.g. the Laplacian's Green's function is compact and self-adjoint).

---

## Example

The integral operator $(Tf)(x) = \int_0^1 k(x,y) f(y)\,dy$ with $k \in L^2([0,1]^2)$ is compact on $L^2[0,1]$ — a **Hilbert–Schmidt operator**.

## Practice Problems

1. Show every finite-rank operator is compact.
2. Prove that a compact operator maps weakly convergent sequences to strongly convergent ones.
3. Verify the identity operator on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space is not compact.
4. Explain why compact self-adjoint operators cannot be invertible on an infinite-dimensional space (unless finite rank plus a nonzero "tail").
