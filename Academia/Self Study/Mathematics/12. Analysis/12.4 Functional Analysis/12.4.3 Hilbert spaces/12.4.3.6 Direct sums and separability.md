## Orthogonal Direct Sums

If $H_1, H_2$ are closed subspaces of $H$ with $H_1 \perp H_2$ and $H = H_1 + H_2$, write $H = H_1 \oplus H_2$. Every $x$ splits uniquely as $x = x_1 + x_2$ with $\|x\|^2 = \|x_1\|^2 + \|x_2\|^2$ (Pythagorean identity).

This extends to countable orthogonal families: $H = \overline{\bigoplus_n H_n}$ when the $H_n$ are pairwise orthogonal and their union spans a dense subspace.

---

## Separability

$H$ is **separable** if it has a countable dense subset — equivalently, a countable orthonormal basis.

| Space | Separable? |
|-------|------------|
| $\ell^2$, $L^2[0,1]$ | Yes |
| $\ell^2(I)$, $I$ uncountable | No |

> [!note] Practical consequence
> Nearly every Hilbert space that arises in applications (quantum mechanics, PDEs, signal processing) is separable, which is why "Hilbert space" in applied contexts almost always means "isomorphic to $\ell^2$."

---

## All Separable Infinite-Dimensional Hilbert Spaces Are Isomorphic

Any two separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces over the same field are isometrically isomorphic — both are isomorphic to $\ell^2$, via mapping an orthonormal basis to the standard basis.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $L^2[0,1]$ is separable by exhibiting a countable dense set (e.g. polynomials with rational coefficients).
2. Prove the Pythagorean identity $\|x_1+x_2\|^2 = \|x_1\|^2+\|x_2\|^2$ for orthogonal $x_1, x_2$.
3. Explain why the isomorphism-to-$\ell^2$ result requires separability.
