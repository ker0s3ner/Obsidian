## Definition

A sequence $x_n \in H$ converges **weakly** to $x$, written $x_n \rightharpoonup x$, if:

$$\langle x_n, y\rangle \to \langle x, y\rangle \quad \text{for every } y \in H$$

Weak convergence is strictly weaker than norm ("strong") convergence: $x_n \to x$ implies $x_n \rightharpoonup x$, but not conversely.

---

## Example: Orthonormal Sequences Converge Weakly to Zero

Let $\{e_n\}$ be an orthonormal sequence. By Bessel's inequality, $\sum_n |\langle y,e_n\rangle|^2 \leq \|y\|^2 < \infty$, so $\langle y, e_n\rangle \to 0$ for every $y$ — hence $e_n \rightharpoonup 0$. But $\|e_n\| = 1$ for all $n$, so $e_n \not\to 0$ strongly.

---

## Bounded Weak Compactness

> [!example] Key theorem
> Every bounded sequence in a Hilbert space has a weakly convergent subsequence.

This is the Hilbert-space analogue of Bolzano–Weierstrass, and it's what makes weak convergence indispensable: norm-bounded sets aren't compact in infinite dimensions ([[12.4.1.3 Finite-dimensional normed spaces]]), but they are **weakly** sequentially compact.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that weak limits are unique.
2. Prove that norm convergence implies weak convergence.
3. Show that if $x_n \rightharpoonup x$ and $\|x_n\| \to \|x\|$, then $x_n \to x$ strongly.
4. Verify the orthonormal-sequence example directly for $e_n = (0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots) \in \ell^2$.
