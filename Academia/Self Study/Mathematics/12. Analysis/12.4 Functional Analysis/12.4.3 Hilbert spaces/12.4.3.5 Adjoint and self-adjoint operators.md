## The Adjoint Operator

For $T \in \mathcal{B}(H)$, the **adjoint** $T^*$ is the unique bounded operator satisfying:

$$\langle Tx, y\rangle = \langle x, T^*y\rangle \quad \text{for all } x,y \in H$$

Existence follows from Riesz representation applied to $y \mapsto \langle Tx,y\rangle$ for fixed $x$. Key properties: $\|T^*\| = \|T\|$, $(ST)^* = T^*S^*$, $(T^*)^* = T$.

---

## Self-Adjoint Operators

$T$ is **self-adjoint** if $T = T^*$, i.e. $\langle Tx,y\rangle = \langle x,Ty\rangle$ for all $x,y$.

| Type | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Self-adjoint | $T = T^*$ |
| Normal | $TT^* = T^*T$ |
| Unitary | $T^*T = TT^* = I$ |

> [!example] Analogy with matrices
> Self-adjoint operators generalize real symmetric / complex Hermitian matrices; unitary operators generalize orthogonal/unitary matrices. Self-adjoint operators always have **real** spectrum, exactly as Hermitian matrices have real eigenvalues.

---

## Quadratic Form Characterization

If $T$ is self-adjoint, then $\langle Tx, x\rangle \in \mathbb{R}$ for all $x$, and:

$$\|T\| = \sup_{\|x\|=1} |\langle Tx, x\rangle|$$

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $\langle Tx,x\rangle$ is real for every $x$ when $T$ is self-adjoint.
2. Prove $\|T^*\| = \|T\|$ for any bounded operator $T$.
3. Verify that the multiplication operator $(Tf)(x) = xf(x)$ on $L^2[0,1]$ is self-adjoint.
4. Show that a unitary operator preserves inner products: $\langle Ux, Uy\rangle = \langle x,y\rangle$.
