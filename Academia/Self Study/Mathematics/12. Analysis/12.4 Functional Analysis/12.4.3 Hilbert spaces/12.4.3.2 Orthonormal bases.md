## Orthonormal Sets

A set $\{e_i\}_{i \in I}$ is **orthonormal** if $\langle e_i, e_j\rangle = \delta_{ij}$. It is an **orthonormal basis** if additionally $\overline{\text{span}}\{e_i\} = H$ (its closed span is the whole space).

---

## Bessel's Inequality and Parseval's Identity

For any orthonormal set $\{e_i\}$ and $x \in H$:

$$\sum_i |\langle x, e_i\rangle|^2 \leq \|x\|^2 \qquad \text{(Bessel)}$$

Equality holds for **all** $x$ iff $\{e_i\}$ is a complete orthonormal basis:

$$x = \sum_i \langle x,e_i\rangle e_i, \qquad \|x\|^2 = \sum_i |\langle x,e_i\rangle|^2 \qquad \text{(Parseval)}$$

> [!example] Fourier series as an orthonormal basis
> $\{e^{inx}/\sqrt{2\pi}\}_{n \in \mathbb{Z}}$ is an orthonormal basis of $L^2[-\pi,\pi]$ — Parseval's identity here is exactly the statement that the Fourier coefficients capture all of $f$'s $L^2$ energy. See [[12.5.1 Fourier series]].

---

## Gram–Schmidt Process

Any countable linearly independent set $\{x_n\}$ can be turned into an orthonormal set $\{e_n\}$ with the same span, inductively:

$$u_n = x_n - \sum_{k<n} \langle x_n, e_k\rangle e_k, \qquad e_n = \frac{u_n}{\|u_n\|}$$

---

## Every Hilbert Space Has an Orthonormal Basis

Every Hilbert space admits an orthonormal basis (via Zorn's lemma in the general case). A Hilbert space is **separable** iff it has a countable orthonormal basis — the case relevant to $\ell^2$ and $L^2$ on standard measure spaces.

## Practice Problems

1. Apply Gram–Schmidt to $\{1, x, x^2\}$ on $L^2[-1,1]$ to obtain the first three (normalized) Legendre polynomials.
2. Prove Bessel's inequality directly from $\|x - \sum_{i} \langle x,e_i\rangle e_i\|^2 \geq 0$.
3. Show that $\ell^2$'s standard basis $\{e_n\}$ is a complete orthonormal basis.
4. Explain why Parseval's identity fails if the orthonormal set is not complete.
