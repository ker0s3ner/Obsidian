## Definition

An **inner product** on a vector space $X$ over $\mathbb{K}$ is a map $\langle \cdot,\cdot\rangle: X \times X \to \mathbb{K}$ satisfying:

| Axiom | Statement |
|-------|-----------|
| Conjugate symmetry | $\langle x,y\rangle = \overline{\langle y,x\rangle}$ |
| Linearity in first argument | $\langle ax+by, z\rangle = a\langle x,z\rangle + b\langle y,z\rangle$ |
| Positive-definiteness | $\langle x,x\rangle \geq 0$, and $= 0$ iff $x=0$ |

Every inner product induces a norm $\|x\| = \sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}$, and a Hilbert space is an inner product space complete under this norm.

---

## Cauchy–Schwarz Inequality

$$|\langle x,y\rangle| \leq \|x\|\,\|y\|$$

with equality iff $x, y$ are linearly dependent.

---

## Parallelogram Law

$$\|x+y\|^2 + \|x-y\|^2 = 2\|x\|^2 + 2\|y\|^2$$

> [!example] Jordan–von Neumann theorem
> A norm arises from an inner product **if and only if** it satisfies the parallelogram law — this is the definitive test for "is this Banach space secretly a Hilbert space?"

## Standard Examples

| Space | Inner product |
|-------|----------------|
| $\mathbb{R}^n$ | $\langle x,y\rangle = \sum x_iy_i$ |
| $\mathbb{C}^n$ | $\langle x,y\rangle = \sum x_i\overline{y_i}$ |
| $\ell^2$ | $\langle x,y\rangle = \sum_n x_n\overline{y_n}$ |
| $L^2(\mu)$ | $\langle f,g\rangle = \int f\overline{g}\,d\mu$ |

## Practice Problems

1. Prove Cauchy–Schwarz using the fact that $\langle x - ty, x-ty\rangle \geq 0$ for all real $t$.
2. Verify the parallelogram law holds in $\ell^2$.
3. Show $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$ fails the parallelogram law, hence isn't a Hilbert norm.
4. Derive the polarization identity expressing $\langle x,y\rangle$ in terms of norms alone.
