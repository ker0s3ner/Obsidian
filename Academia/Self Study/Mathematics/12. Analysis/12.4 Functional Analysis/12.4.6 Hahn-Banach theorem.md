## Statement (Extension Form)

Let $X$ be a vector space, $p: X \to \mathbb{R}$ a seminorm, $M \subseteq X$ a subspace, and $\varphi_0: M \to \mathbb{K}$ linear with $|\varphi_0(x)| \leq p(x)$ for $x \in M$. Then there exists a linear extension $\varphi: X \to \mathbb{K}$ with:

$$\varphi|_M = \varphi_0, \qquad |\varphi(x)| \leq p(x) \quad \text{for all } x \in X$$

**Normed-space corollary**: every bounded functional on a subspace extends to a bounded functional on the whole space, with the same norm.

---

## Consequences

- $X^*$ **separates points**: for $x \neq 0$ there exists $\varphi \in X^*$ with $\varphi(x) \neq 0$, and in fact $\|\varphi\|=1$, $\varphi(x) = \|x\|$
- Every normed space embeds isometrically into $X^{**}$ (foundation of reflexivity)
- Existence of the dual is nontrivial: without Hahn–Banach, $X^*$ could conceivably contain only $\varphi = 0$

---

## Geometric (Separation) Form

> [!example] Separating hyperplane theorem
> If $A, B$ are disjoint convex sets in a normed space with $A$ open, there exists a bounded linear functional $\varphi$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ separating them: $\varphi(a) < \alpha \leq \varphi(b)$ for all $a\in A$, $b \in B$.

This geometric form is what makes Hahn–Banach the foundation of convex optimization and separation arguments in economics and optimization theory.
