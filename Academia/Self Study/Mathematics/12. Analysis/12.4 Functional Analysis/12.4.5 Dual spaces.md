## Definition

The **dual space** $X^*$ of a normed space $X$ is the space of all bounded linear functionals $\varphi: X \to \mathbb{K}$, normed by:

$$\|\varphi\| = \sup_{\|x\|\leq 1} |\varphi(x)|$$

$X^*$ is always a Banach space, regardless of whether $X$ itself is complete.

---

## Common Duals

| Space | Dual |
|-------|------|
| $\ell^p$, $1 < p < \infty$ | $\ell^q$, $\frac1p+\frac1q=1$ |
| $\ell^1$ | $\ell^\infty$ |
| $c_0$ | $\ell^1$ |
| $L^p(\mu)$, $1 < p < \infty$ | $L^q(\mu)$ |
| $H$ (Hilbert space) | $H$ itself (Riesz representation) |

> [!warning] $\ell^\infty$ is not the dual of anything nice
> $(\ell^1)^* = \ell^\infty$, but $(\ell^\infty)^* \neq \ell^1$ — it's a much larger, more exotic space. Duality is not symmetric outside the reflexive cases.

---

## Reflexivity

$X$ is **reflexive** if the canonical embedding $X \hookrightarrow X^{**}$ (via $x \mapsto (\varphi \mapsto \varphi(x))$) is onto. Hilbert spaces and $L^p$, $1<p<\infty$, are reflexive; $\ell^1$, $c_0$, and $C[a,b]$ are not.

---

## Weak-* Topology

The **weak-\* topology** on $X^*$ is the topology of pointwise convergence on $X$: $\varphi_n \xrightarrow{w^*} \varphi$ iff $\varphi_n(x) \to \varphi(x)$ for every $x \in X$.

> [!example] Banach–Alaoglu theorem
> The closed unit ball of $X^*$ is compact in the weak-\* topology — a genuine compactness result in infinite dimensions, unlike the failure of norm-compactness in [[12.4.1.3 Finite-dimensional normed spaces]].

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $(\ell^1)^* \cong \ell^\infty$ via the pairing $\varphi(x) = \sum_n x_n y_n$.
2. Explain why every Hilbert space is reflexive, using Riesz representation twice.
3. Verify that $C[0,1]$ is not reflexive (its dual involves signed measures via Riesz representation for $C(K)^*$).
4. State the Banach–Alaoglu theorem and explain why it needs the weak-\* rather than norm topology.
