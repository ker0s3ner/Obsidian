## The General Framework

Classical Fourier analysis on $\mathbb{T}$ (series) and $\mathbb{R}$ (transform) are the two simplest cases of harmonic analysis on a **locally compact abelian group** $G$: every such group carries a **Haar measure**, and its **Pontryagin dual** $\hat G$ (the group of characters $G \to \mathbb{T}$) plays the role of "frequency space."

| $G$ | $\hat G$ | Theory |
|-----|----------|--------|
| $\mathbb{T}$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | Fourier series |
| $\mathbb{R}$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | Fourier transform |
| $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ | $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ | Discrete Fourier transform |
| $\mathbb{Z}$ | $\mathbb{T}$ | Fourier series in reverse |

---

## Pontryagin Duality

> [!example] Statement
> For a locally compact abelian group $G$, $\hat{\hat G} \cong G$ naturally — dualizing twice returns the original group.

This explains, for example, why the Discrete Fourier Transform is its own kind of inverse-transform pair, and why Fourier series (on $\mathbb{T}$, dual $\mathbb{Z}$) and Fourier transforms on $\mathbb{Z}$ (dual $\mathbb{T}$) are mirror images of each other.

---

## Non-Abelian Extension

For non-abelian compact groups, characters are replaced by **irreducible unitary representations**, and Fourier analysis becomes representation theory — see [[15. Abstract Algebra/15.5 Representation Theory/15.5 Representation Theory]].
