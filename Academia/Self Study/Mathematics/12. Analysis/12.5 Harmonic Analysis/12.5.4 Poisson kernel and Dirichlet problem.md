## The Dirichlet Problem

Given boundary values $f$ on $\partial\Omega$, find $u$ harmonic on $\Omega$ with $u|_{\partial\Omega} = f$.

---

## Poisson Kernel on the Disk

For the unit disk, the solution is given by convolving $f$ with the **Poisson kernel**:

$$P_r(\theta) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} r^{|n|} e^{in\theta} = \frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^2}, \qquad u(re^{i\theta}) = (P_r * f)(\theta)$$

As $r \to 1^-$, $u(re^{i\theta}) \to f(\theta)$ for a.e. $\theta$ — this is a strong (a.e./radial) form of Fourier series convergence, better-behaved than pointwise convergence of partial sums.

---

## Kernel Properties

$P_r \geq 0$, $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int P_r = 1$, and $P_r$ concentrates near $\theta = 0$ as $r \to 1$ — making $\{P_r\}$ an **approximate identity**, the same mechanism used in [[12.6 Fourier Analysis/12.6.2 Convergence of Fourier series]] to fix convergence issues with raw partial sums.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi P_r(\theta)\,d\theta = 1$ for all $r < 1$.
2. Show that $P_r * f$ is harmonic in the disk for any continuous $f$ on the circle.
3. Explain why Abel summation ($r\to1^-$ limits) of a Fourier series can succeed where ordinary partial-sum convergence fails.
