## Definition

For $f \in L^2[-\pi,\pi]$, the **Fourier series** expresses $f$ in the orthonormal basis $\{e^{inx}/\sqrt{2\pi}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}$ of [[12.4.3.2 Orthonormal bases]]:

$$f(x) \sim \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n e^{inx}, \qquad c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x) e^{-inx}\,dx$$

---

## Harmonic-Analysis Viewpoint

Harmonic analysis studies Fourier series as **decomposition into characters** of the circle group $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}$: each $e^{inx}$ is a group homomorphism $\mathbb{T}\to \mathbb{C}^\times$, and Fourier analysis is exactly harmonic analysis on this particular compact abelian group.

> [!tip] Why "harmonic"
> The $e^{inx}$ are literally the harmonics (overtones) of a fundamental frequency — the language comes directly from acoustics, and generalizes to representation theory on arbitrary groups.

---

## Convergence in $L^2$

By Parseval (see [[12.4.3.2 Orthonormal bases]]), the partial sums converge in $L^2$ norm to $f$ for **every** $f \in L^2$, regardless of smoothness — a much stronger and easier statement than pointwise convergence.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the Fourier coefficients of $f(x) = x$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$.
2. Show that $c_{-n} = \overline{c_n}$ when $f$ is real-valued.
3. Use Parseval's identity to evaluate $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2}$ from the Fourier series of $f(x)=x$.
