## Definition

The **Hardy space** $H^p(\mathbb{D})$, $0 < p \leq \infty$, consists of holomorphic functions $f$ on the unit disk with:

$$\|f\|_{H^p} = \sup_{0 \leq r < 1} \left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} |f(re^{i\theta})|^p \, d\theta\right)^{1/p} < \infty$$

---

## Boundary Values

Every $f \in H^p$ ($p \geq 1$) has **radial limits** $f^*(\theta) = \lim_{r\to1^-} f(re^{i\theta})$ existing for a.e. $\theta$, and $f^* \in L^p(\mathbb{T})$ with $\|f\|_{H^p} = \|f^*\|_{L^p}$ — realizing $H^p$ as a closed subspace of $L^p(\mathbb{T})$: exactly those boundary functions whose negative Fourier coefficients vanish.

> [!note] Connection to harmonic functions
> $\text{Re}(f)$ for $f \in H^p$ is a harmonic function recoverable from its boundary values by the Poisson kernel of [[12.5.4 Poisson kernel and Dirichlet problem]] — Hardy spaces are the holomorphic refinement of that theory.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that a bounded holomorphic function on $\mathbb{D}$ lies in $H^\infty$.
2. Explain why $H^2$ boundary functions are characterized by vanishing negative Fourier coefficients.
3. Verify that $f(z) = \frac{1}{1-z}$ fails to lie in $H^1$.
