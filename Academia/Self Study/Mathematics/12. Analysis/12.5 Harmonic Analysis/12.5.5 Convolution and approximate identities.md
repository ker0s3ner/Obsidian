## Convolution

$$(f * g)(x) = \int f(y) g(x-y)\, dy$$

Convolution is commutative, associative, and turns the Fourier transform into pointwise multiplication: $\widehat{f*g} = \hat f \hat g$.

---

## Approximate Identities

A family $\{\phi_\epsilon\}$ is an **approximate identity** if $\int \phi_\epsilon = 1$, $\phi_\epsilon \geq 0$ (typically), and $\phi_\epsilon$ concentrates near $0$ as $\epsilon \to 0$. Then:

$$f * \phi_\epsilon \to f \quad \text{as } \epsilon \to 0$$

in $L^p$ (for $f \in L^p$, $1\leq p<\infty$) and uniformly if $f$ is continuous with compact support.

| Example | Where it appears |
|---------|-------------------|
| Poisson kernel $P_r$ | Dirichlet problem on the disk |
| Fejér kernel | Fixing Fourier partial-sum convergence |
| Gaussian $\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}e^{-x^2/4t}$ | Heat kernel, heat equation |

> [!tip] The unifying idea
> Nearly every "smoothing" or "regularization" trick in analysis is convolution against an approximate identity — mollifiers, heat flow, and Fourier summation methods are all instances of the same mechanism.

## Practice Problems

1. Prove $\widehat{f*g} = \hat f \cdot \hat g$ for $f, g \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$.
2. Verify the Gaussian family $\phi_t(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}e^{-x^2/4t}$ is an approximate identity as $t \to 0$.
3. Show that $f * \phi_\epsilon \to f$ uniformly for continuous compactly-supported $f$, given uniform continuity of $f$.
