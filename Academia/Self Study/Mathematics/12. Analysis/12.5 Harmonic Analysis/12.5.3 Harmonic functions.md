## Definition

$u: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ on an open $\Omega \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is **harmonic** if it satisfies **Laplace's equation**:

$$\Delta u = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x_i^2} = 0$$

---

## Mean Value Property

> [!example] Key characterization
> $u$ is harmonic iff for every ball $B(x_0, r) \subseteq \Omega$, $u(x_0)$ equals the average of $u$ over the sphere (and over the ball):
> $$u(x_0) = \frac{1}{|\partial B_r|}\int_{\partial B(x_0,r)} u \, dS$$

This gives the **maximum principle** immediately: a harmonic function attains its max/min on the boundary of any region, never strictly inside.

---

## Relation to Fourier Analysis

On the disk, harmonic functions with prescribed boundary values are recovered via the **Poisson kernel**, whose Fourier coefficients are $r^{|n|}$ — connecting harmonic function theory directly to Fourier series in [[12.5.1 Fourier series]].

---

## Relation to Complex Analysis

In two dimensions, $u$ is harmonic iff it is locally the real part of a holomorphic function — this is why harmonic function theory and complex analysis are so intertwined (see [[12. Analysis/12.5 Complex Analysis]]).

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $u(x,y) = x^2 - y^2$ is harmonic and find a harmonic conjugate.
2. Use the mean value property to prove the maximum principle.
3. Show that a bounded harmonic function on all of $\mathbb{R}^n$ must be constant (Liouville-type theorem).
