## The Hardy–Littlewood Maximal Function

$$Mf(x) = \sup_{r>0} \frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)} |f(y)|\, dy$$

$M$ is **not** bounded $L^1 \to L^1$, but satisfies the crucial **weak-type $(1,1)$ estimate**:

$$|\{x : Mf(x) > \lambda\}| \leq \frac{C}{\lambda}\|f\|_1$$

and is bounded $L^p \to L^p$ for $1 < p \leq \infty$.

---

## Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem

> [!example] Main application
> $\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)} f \, dy \to f(x)$ for a.e. $x$, as $r \to 0$ — average values of an $L^1$ function converge back to the function itself almost everywhere. The maximal function's weak-type bound is exactly what controls this.

## Calderón–Zygmund Operators

Singular integral operators (like the Hilbert transform) that are bounded on $L^2$ but whose kernels are too singular for naive $L^1$ estimates. Calderón–Zygmund theory extends their $L^2$ boundedness to weak-$(1,1)$ and $L^p$, $1<p<\infty$, using maximal-function techniques — the foundation of modern harmonic analysis and PDE regularity theory.
