## Product Measures

Given measure spaces $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ and $(Y, \mathcal{N}, \nu)$, the **product $\sigma$-algebra** $\mathcal{M} \otimes \mathcal{N}$ is generated by rectangles $A \times B$ with $A \in \mathcal{M}$, $B \in \mathcal{N}$. There is a unique **product measure** $\mu \times \nu$ satisfying:

$$(\mu \times \nu)(A \times B) = \mu(A)\,\nu(B)$$

---

## Tonelli's Theorem

For $f \geq 0$ measurable on $X \times Y$:

$$\int_{X \times Y} f \, d(\mu \times \nu) = \int_X \left(\int_Y f(x,y) \, d\nu(y)\right) d\mu(x) = \int_Y \left(\int_X f(x,y) \, d\mu(x)\right) d\nu(y)$$

This holds **unconditionally** for non-negative functions — no integrability assumption needed.

---

## Fubini's Theorem

If $f \in L^1(X \times Y, \mu \times \nu)$ (i.e. $\int |f| \, d(\mu\times\nu) < \infty$), the same iterated-integral equality holds, and both inner integrals exist for a.e. $x$ (resp. $y$).

> [!warning] Fubini requires integrability
> Without $\int |f| < \infty$, the two iterated integrals can disagree. Classic counterexample: $f(x,y) = \frac{x^2 - y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}$ on $[0,1]^2$ gives opposite-sign iterated integrals.

**Strategy**: to swap integration order, first check $\int\int |f|$ via Tonelli (always valid), then invoke Fubini once finiteness is confirmed.

## Practice Problems

1. Use Tonelli to compute $\int_0^\infty \int_0^\infty e^{-xy}\sin x \, dx\, dy$ by choosing a convenient order.
2. Show the counterexample $f(x,y) = \frac{x^2-y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}$ has iterated integrals $+\pi/4$ and $-\pi/4$.
3. Prove that $\int |f| \, d(\mu \times \nu) < \infty$ is exactly the hypothesis needed for Fubini.
4. Use Fubini to justify differentiating under the integral sign for $F(t) = \int_0^1 x^t \, dx$.
