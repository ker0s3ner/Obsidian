## Signed Measures

A **signed measure** $\nu$ allows negative values: $\nu: \mathcal{M} \to [-\infty, \infty]$, countably additive, $\nu(\emptyset) = 0$.

### Hahn Decomposition

Every signed measure splits the space into a positive set $P$ and negative set $N$ with $X = P \cup N$, $P \cap N = \emptyset$, such that $\nu \geq 0$ on $P$ and $\nu \leq 0$ on $N$.

### Jordan Decomposition

$$\nu = \nu^+ - \nu^-$$

where $\nu^+, \nu^-$ are (non-negative) measures concentrated on $P$ and $N$ respectively, and $\nu^+ \perp \nu^-$ (**mutually singular** — disjoint supports).

## Absolute Continuity

$\nu$ is **absolutely continuous** with respect to $\mu$, written $\nu \ll \mu$, if:

$$\mu(E) = 0 \implies \nu(E) = 0$$

---

## Radon–Nikodym Theorem

If $\nu \ll \mu$ and both are $\sigma$-finite, there exists a measurable function $f \geq 0$, unique up to a.e. equality, such that:

$$\nu(E) = \int_E f \, d\mu \quad \text{for all } E \in \mathcal{M}$$

$f$ is the **Radon–Nikodym derivative**, written $f = \dfrac{d\nu}{d\mu}$.

> [!example] Density functions
> This is exactly how a probability density is defined: if $\nu$ is a probability measure absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, its density $f = d\nu/d\mu$ is what's integrated in $\int f\,dx$.

---

## Lebesgue Decomposition

Any $\sigma$-finite $\nu$ splits uniquely as:

$$\nu = \nu_{ac} + \nu_{s}$$

where $\nu_{ac} \ll \mu$ and $\nu_s \perp \mu$ (singular part — e.g. a measure concentrated on a Lebesgue-null set).

## Practice Problems

1. Find the Hahn decomposition of $\nu(E) = \int_E x \, dx$ on $[-1, 1]$.
2. Show that if $\nu \ll \mu$ and $\mu \ll \nu$, the Radon–Nikodym derivatives are reciprocal a.e.
3. Give an example of a measure that is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure but not zero.
4. Prove the chain rule $\dfrac{d\nu}{d\mu} = \dfrac{d\nu}{d\lambda}\cdot\dfrac{d\lambda}{d\mu}$ for $\nu \ll \lambda \ll \mu$.
