## The Problem With the Plain Fourier Transform

$\hat f(\xi)$ tells you *what* frequencies are present in $f$, but not *when* — all time-localization is lost. For signals whose frequency content changes over time (speech, music), this is a serious limitation.

---

## Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)

Multiply by a window $w$ centered at $t$ before transforming:

$$\text{STFT}(t,\xi) = \int f(x) w(x-t) e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\, dx$$

giving a **time–frequency** representation — but the [[12.6.7 Uncertainty principle]] forces a trade-off: a narrow window gives good time resolution but poor frequency resolution, and vice versa.

---

## Spectrograms

$|\text{STFT}(t,\xi)|^2$, plotted as an image over $(t,\xi)$, is a **spectrogram** — the standard visualization for audio analysis, seismology, and radar.

> [!tip] Connection to wavelets
> Wavelet transforms replace the fixed-width window with a scalable one, achieving good time resolution at high frequencies and good frequency resolution at low frequencies simultaneously — a partial escape from the STFT's fixed trade-off.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain qualitatively why narrowing the window $w$ improves time resolution but worsens frequency resolution.
2. Compute the STFT of a pure tone $f(x) = \sin(2\pi f_0 x)$ using a Gaussian window.
3. Describe how a spectrogram of a chirp signal (frequency increasing linearly in time) would look.
