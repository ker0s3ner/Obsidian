## Definition and Inversion

$$\hat f(\xi) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\xi}\,dx, \qquad f(x) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \hat f(\xi) e^{2\pi i x\xi}\,d\xi$$

The inversion formula holds pointwise when $f, \hat f \in L^1$, and in the $L^2$ sense (Plancherel) more generally — see [[12.5 Harmonic Analysis/12.5.2 Fourier transform]] for the Hilbert-space treatment.

## Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT)

For a finite sequence $x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}$:

$$X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n\, e^{-2\pi i kn/N}, \qquad k = 0,\ldots,N-1$$

The DFT is the finite-group analogue: $\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$ is its own dual, so the transform maps $N$ numbers to $N$ numbers exactly.

## Sampling and Aliasing

> [!warning] Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem
> A signal band-limited to frequencies $|\xi| \leq B$ is fully recoverable from samples taken at rate $\geq 2B$. Sampling below this rate causes **aliasing** — high frequencies fold back and masquerade as lower ones.
