## Definition

For a $2\pi$-periodic function $f$, the Fourier series is:

$$f(x) \sim \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(a_n\cos nx + b_n \sin nx\right)$$

$$a_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi f(x)\cos nx\,dx, \qquad b_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi f(x)\sin nx\,dx$$

equivalent to the complex form $\sum c_n e^{inx}$ from [[12.5 Harmonic Analysis/12.5.1 Fourier series]] via $c_n = \frac{a_n - ib_n}{2}$ ($n>0$).

---

## Even and Odd Functions

| $f$ | Series contains |
|-----|------------------|
| Even | Only cosines ($b_n = 0$) |
| Odd | Only sines ($a_n = 0$) |

---

## Worked Example

**Square wave** $f(x) = \text{sgn}(\sin x)$: since $f$ is odd,

$$f(x) \sim \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{\sin((2k+1)x)}{2k+1}$$

> [!example] Gibbs phenomenon
> Near the jump discontinuities of the square wave, partial sums overshoot by a fixed amount (~9% of the jump) no matter how many terms are added — a persistent artifact of approximating discontinuities with smooth functions.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the Fourier series of $f(x) = x^2$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$.
2. Show that the Fourier series of an odd function contains no cosine terms.
3. Use the square wave's Fourier series (evaluated at $x=\pi/2$) to derive $\frac{\pi}{4} = 1 - \frac13+\frac15-\cdots$.
