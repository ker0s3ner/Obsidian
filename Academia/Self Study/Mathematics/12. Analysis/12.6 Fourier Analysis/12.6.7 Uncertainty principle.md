## Heisenberg–Weyl Uncertainty Inequality

For $f \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$ with $\|f\|_2 = 1$:

$$\left(\int x^2 |f(x)|^2 \, dx\right)\left(\int \xi^2 |\hat f(\xi)|^2 \, d\xi\right) \geq \frac{1}{16\pi^2}$$

A function and its Fourier transform cannot both be arbitrarily concentrated — sharpening one spreads the other.

---

## Equality Case: Gaussians

Equality holds exactly for Gaussians $f(x) = Ce^{-\pi\alpha x^2}$, which are (up to scaling) their own Fourier transforms — the unique functions achieving the tightest possible joint localization.

> [!example] Physical meaning
> This is the mathematical content of Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: position and momentum wavefunctions are Fourier transforms of each other, so the inequality above is literally $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \geq \hbar/2$ after rescaling.

---

## Proof Idea

Uses Cauchy–Schwarz applied to $\int x f(x)\overline{f'(x)}\,dx$, combined with integration by parts and the differentiation property $\widehat{f'} = 2\pi i\xi\hat f$.
