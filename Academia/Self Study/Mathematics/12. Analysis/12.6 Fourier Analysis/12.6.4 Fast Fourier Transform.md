## The Problem

Computing the DFT of $N$ points directly from the formula $X_k = \sum_n x_n e^{-2\pi ikn/N}$ takes $O(N^2)$ operations.

## Cooley–Tukey FFT

When $N = N_1N_2$, the DFT decomposes into smaller DFTs of size $N_1$ and $N_2$ combined with $O(N)$ extra work (twiddle factors). Recursively applied when $N = 2^m$:

$$X_k = \sum_{n \text{ even}} x_n e^{-2\pi ikn/N} + e^{-2\pi ik/N}\sum_{n \text{ odd}} x_n e^{-2\pi ikn/N}$$

each sum being a DFT of size $N/2$, giving total cost:

$$O(N\log N)$$

> [!example] Why this matters practically
> The FFT is arguably the most-used numerical algorithm in existence — it underlies audio/image compression, PDE solvers via spectral methods, convolution algorithms, and polynomial multiplication.

---

## Fast Convolution via FFT

Since $\widehat{f*g} = \hat f\hat g$, convolving two length-$N$ sequences directly costs $O(N^2)$, but via FFT: transform ($O(N\log N)$), multiply pointwise ($O(N)$), inverse transform ($O(N\log N)$) — total $O(N\log N)$.
