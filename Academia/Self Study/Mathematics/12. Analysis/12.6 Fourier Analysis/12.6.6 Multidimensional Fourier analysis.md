## Definition in $\mathbb{R}^n$

$$\hat f(\xi) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x) e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\, dx, \qquad x, \xi \in \mathbb{R}^n$$

All 1D properties (linearity, differentiation $\widehat{\partial_j f} = 2\pi i\xi_j\hat f$, convolution theorem, Plancherel) carry over directly.

---

## Radial Functions and Bessel Functions

If $f(x) = f_0(|x|)$ is **radial**, so is $\hat f$, and it's expressible via a **Hankel transform** involving Bessel functions:

$$\hat f(\xi) = 2\pi|\xi|^{1-n/2}\int_0^\infty f_0(r) J_{n/2-1}(2\pi r|\xi|) r^{n/2}\, dr$$

This is why Bessel functions appear throughout physics wherever radial symmetry meets Fourier methods (diffraction patterns, quantum mechanics in spherical coordinates).

---

## Separable Transforms

If $f(x_1,\ldots,x_n) = f_1(x_1)\cdots f_n(x_n)$, then $\hat f(\xi) = \hat f_1(\xi_1)\cdots \hat f_n(\xi_n)$ — multidimensional transforms of separable functions (like multivariate Gaussians) reduce to products of 1D transforms.

2. Verify the differentiation identity $\widehat{\partial_j f} = 2\pi i \xi_j \hat f$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
3. Explain why the Fourier transform of a radially symmetric function is itself radially symmetric.
