## Modes of Convergence

| Result | Statement |
|--------|-----------|
| $L^2$ convergence | Partial sums $\to f$ in $L^2$ for every $f \in L^2$ (Parseval) |
| Pointwise (Dirichlet) | Converges to $\frac{f(x^+)+f(x^-)}{2}$ if $f$ is piecewise $C^1$ near $x$ |
| Pointwise failure | There exist **continuous** $f$ whose Fourier series diverges at a point |
| Uniform | Converges uniformly if $f$ is $C^1$ (or Hölder continuous) and periodic |

---

## Why Pointwise Convergence Can Fail

> [!warning] Du Bois-Reymond's example
> The failure of pointwise convergence for general continuous functions follows from the Uniform Boundedness Principle: the partial-sum operators $S_N: C(\mathbb{T}) \to \mathbb{R}$, $S_Nf \mapsto (S_Nf)(0)$, are **not** uniformly bounded, so Banach–Steinhaus guarantees some continuous $f$ makes $S_Nf(0)$ unbounded.

---

## Fejér's Theorem — Fixing Convergence with Averaging

Cesàro-averaging the partial sums, $\sigma_N f = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n<N} S_nf$, **does** converge uniformly to $f$ for every continuous periodic $f$ — this is convolution with the **Fejér kernel**, a genuine approximate identity (unlike the Dirichlet kernel behind raw partial sums).
