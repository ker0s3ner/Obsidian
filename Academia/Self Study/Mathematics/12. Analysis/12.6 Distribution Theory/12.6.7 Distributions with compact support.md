## The Space $\mathcal{E}'$

Distributions with **compact support** form the space $\mathcal{E}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$, dual to $\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{R}^n) = C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$ (all smooth functions, not just compactly supported ones).

**Key fact**: a compactly-supported distribution can be paired against *any* smooth function, not just test functions — its pairing $\langle T,\phi\rangle$ makes sense for $\phi \in C^\infty$, because $T$'s own compact support "cuts off" the interaction.

---

## Structure Theorem

Every $T \in \mathcal{E}'$ is a **finite sum of derivatives of continuous functions**, all supported in any neighborhood of $\text{supp}(T)$ — compactly supported distributions have finite "order," unlike general distributions.

---

## Chain: $\mathcal{D} \subset \mathcal{S} \subset \mathcal{E}$, dually $\mathcal{E}' \subset \mathcal{S}' \subset \mathcal{D}'$

$$\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^n) \subset \mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^n) \subset \mathcal{E}(\mathbb{R}^n)$$

Dualizing reverses inclusions:

$$\mathcal{E}'(\mathbb{R}^n) \subset \mathcal{S}'(\mathbb{R}^n) \subset \mathcal{D}'(\mathbb{R}^n)$$

> [!note] Where $\delta_0$ sits
> $\delta_0 \in \mathcal{E}'$ — compactly supported — hence automatically tempered, hence automatically a distribution. This chain is why $\delta_0$ has a well-defined Fourier transform without any extra argument.
