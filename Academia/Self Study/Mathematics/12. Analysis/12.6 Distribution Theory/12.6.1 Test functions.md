## The Space of Test Functions

$\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^n) = C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^n)$: smooth functions with **compact support**. This is the space distributions act on.

**Topology**: $\phi_k \to \phi$ in $\mathcal{D}$ if all $\phi_k$ are supported in a common compact set and $\partial^\alpha \phi_k \to \partial^\alpha \phi$ uniformly, for every multi-index $\alpha$.

---

## Bump Functions

The standard construction:

$$\phi(x) = \begin{cases} e^{-1/(1-|x|^2)} & |x| < 1 \\ 0 & |x| \geq 1 \end{cases}$$

is smooth, compactly supported, and nonzero — the basic building block for mollifiers and partitions of unity.

> [!example] Why compact support and smoothness together
> $C_c^\infty$ functions are dense enough (via mollification) to test *any* locally integrable function against, yet controlled enough (smooth, compactly supported) that every derivative of a distribution can be defined by shifting the derivative onto the test function.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\phi(x) = e^{-1/(1-x^2)}\mathbf{1}_{|x|<1}$ is smooth at $x = \pm 1$ (all derivatives vanish there).
2. Show $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is dense in $L^p(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for $1\leq p<\infty$.
3. Construct a smooth partition of unity subordinate to a finite open cover using bump functions.
