## Convolution with a Test Function

For $T \in \mathcal{D}'$ and $\phi \in \mathcal{D}$, $T * \phi$ is a **smooth function**:

$$(T*\phi)(x) = \langle T, \phi(x-\cdot)\rangle$$

This is the key mechanism of **mollification**: convolving any distribution with a smooth bump function produces a genuinely smooth function approximating it.

---

## Distributions as Limits of Smooth Functions

Every distribution $T$ is the limit (in $\mathcal{D}'$) of smooth functions $T * \phi_\epsilon$ for an approximate identity $\phi_\epsilon$, as in [[12.5.5 Convolution and approximate identities]]. This is why distribution theory is sometimes summarized as "every generalized function is a limit of nice functions."

---

## Fundamental Solutions

$T$ is a **fundamental solution** of a differential operator $P(\partial)$ if $P(\partial) T = \delta_0$. Convolving with a fundamental solution solves $P(\partial)u = f$:

$$u = T * f$$

> [!example] Example
> For the Laplacian in $\mathbb{R}^3$, $T(x) = -\frac{1}{4\pi|x|}$ satisfies $\Delta T = \delta_0$ — the Newtonian potential is a fundamental solution, immediately giving the solution to Poisson's equation by convolution.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $\delta_0 * \phi = \phi$ for any test function $\phi$.
2. Verify $T*\phi$ is smooth by differentiating under the pairing.
3. Use the fundamental solution of the Laplacian to write the solution of $\Delta u = f$ for compactly supported $f$.
