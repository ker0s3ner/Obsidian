## Definition

A **distribution** is a continuous linear functional $T: \mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^n) \to \mathbb{C}$ (continuous with respect to the topology of [[12.6.1 Test functions]]). Write $\langle T, \phi\rangle$ for the pairing.

---

## Functions as Distributions

Every locally integrable $f$ defines a distribution:

$$\langle T_f, \phi\rangle = \int f(x)\phi(x)\,dx$$

so distributions **generalize** functions — but include objects with no pointwise meaning.

---

## The Dirac Delta

$$\langle \delta_a, \phi\rangle = \phi(a)$$

$\delta_a$ is not given by any function via the integral formula above, yet it's a perfectly well-defined distribution.

> [!example] Why distributions were invented
> Physics needed to make rigorous sense of objects like "a point mass" or "an instantaneous impulse" ($\delta$), and of differentiating non-differentiable functions (like $|x|$ or the Heaviside step). Distributions provide exactly this — every distribution is infinitely differentiable *as a distribution*.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $\delta_a$ is a continuous linear functional on $\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})$.
2. Verify that $T_f$ for $f \in L^1_{loc}$ is well-defined and linear.
3. Show two locally integrable functions equal a.e. define the same distribution.
