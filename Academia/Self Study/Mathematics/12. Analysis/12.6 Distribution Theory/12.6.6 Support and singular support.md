## Support of a Distribution

$T$ **vanishes** on an open set $U$ if $\langle T,\phi\rangle = 0$ for every $\phi$ supported in $U$. The **support** of $T$ is the complement of the largest open set on which $T$ vanishes.

**Example**: $\text{supp}(\delta_a) = \{a\}$.

---

## Singular Support

The **singular support** is the smallest closed set outside which $T$ agrees with a smooth function — it isolates exactly where $T$ fails to be smooth.

| Distribution | Support | Singular support |
|--------------|---------|-------------------|
| $\delta_0$ | $\{0\}$ | $\{0\}$ |
| $H(x)$ (Heaviside) | $[0,\infty)$ | $\{0\}$ |
| Smooth $f$ | $\overline{\{f\neq0\}}$ | $\emptyset$ |

> [!tip] Why the distinction matters
> Singular support pinpoints where "bad behavior" (non-smoothness) actually occurs, even for distributions with unbounded support like $H(x)$ — it's the natural language for stating elliptic regularity theorems in PDE theory.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the singular support of $|x|$.
2. Show that if $\text{supp}(T)$ is empty, $T = 0$.
3. Explain why $\text{sing supp}(T) \subseteq \text{supp}(T)$ always.
