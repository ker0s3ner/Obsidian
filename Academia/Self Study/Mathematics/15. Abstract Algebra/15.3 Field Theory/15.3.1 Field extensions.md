## Definition

A **field extension** $K/F$ is a field $K$ containing $F$ as a subfield. The **degree** $[K:F]$ is $\dim_F K$ (viewing $K$ as an $F$-vector space).

An extension is **finite** if $[K:F] < \infty$, **algebraic** if every element of $K$ is algebraic over $F$.

---

## Tower Law

For $F \subseteq E \subseteq K$:

$$[K:F] = [K:E]\cdot[E:F]$$

**Example**: $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)]\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2):\mathbb{Q}] = 2 \cdot 2 = 4$.

> [!tip] Finite extensions are always algebraic
> If $[K:F]<\infty$, every $\alpha \in K$ satisfies a polynomial over $F$ (its powers $1,\alpha,\alpha^2,\ldots$ can't stay linearly independent forever) — so finite $\implies$ algebraic, though not conversely for infinite extensions.

---

## Algebraic Closure

$F$ is **algebraically closed** if every polynomial in $F[x]$ has a root in $F$. Every field has an algebraic closure $\overline F$, unique up to isomorphism.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb{Q}]$ using the tower law and a basis.
2. Show $[K:F]=1 \iff K=F$.
3. Prove that a finite extension is algebraic.
4. Is $\mathbb{Q}(\pi)/\mathbb{Q}$ finite? Algebraic?
5. Show $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed but $\mathbb{R}$ is not (by example).
