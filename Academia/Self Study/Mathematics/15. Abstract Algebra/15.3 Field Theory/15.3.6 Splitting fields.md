## Definition

A **splitting field** of $f \in F[x]$ is the smallest extension $K/F$ over which $f$ factors completely into linear factors.

---

## Existence and Uniqueness

Every polynomial has a splitting field, constructed by repeatedly adjoining roots; it's unique up to isomorphism.

**Example**: the splitting field of $x^3-2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]2,\omega)$ where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$ (need the cube root **and** a primitive cube root of unity to get all 3 roots) — degree 6, not 3.

> [!warning] Splitting fields are usually bigger than $F(\alpha)$ for one root $\alpha$
> Adjoining a single root often isn't enough — the other roots may not lie in $F(\alpha)$ unless the extension is normal.

---

## Normal Extensions

$K/F$ is **normal** if $K$ is the splitting field of some polynomial over $F$ — equivalently, every irreducible polynomial in $F[x]$ with one root in $K$ has **all** its roots in $K$.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Find the splitting field of $x^3-2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and its degree.
2. Show $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]2)/\mathbb{Q}$ is not normal.
3. Find the splitting field of $x^4-1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
4. Prove splitting fields are unique up to isomorphism.
5. Show every splitting field extension is normal.
