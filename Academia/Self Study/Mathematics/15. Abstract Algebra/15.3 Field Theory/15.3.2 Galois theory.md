## Galois Extensions

$K/F$ is **Galois** if it's normal (splitting field of some polynomial) and **separable** (minimal polynomials have distinct roots). The **Galois group** $\text{Gal}(K/F)$ is the group of field automorphisms of $K$ fixing $F$ pointwise.

$$|\text{Gal}(K/F)| = [K:F] \quad \text{when } K/F \text{ is Galois}$$

---

## The Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory

There's an inclusion-reversing bijection between:

- subfields $F \subseteq E \subseteq K$, and
- subgroups $H \leq \text{Gal}(K/F)$

given by $E \mapsto \text{Gal}(K/E)$ and $H \mapsto K^H$ (fixed field). Moreover $E/F$ is Galois iff the corresponding $H$ is **normal** in $\text{Gal}(K/F)$.

> [!tip] Subgroups ↔ subfields, normal ↔ normal
> This dictionary translates every question about intermediate fields into a question about the (finite, computable) Galois group — the payoff of the whole theory.

---

## Example

For $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)/\mathbb{Q}$: $\text{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, with 3 nontrivial subgroups corresponding to the 3 intermediate fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2)$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt6)$.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)/\mathbb{Q})$ and match its subgroups to intermediate fields.
2. Show $|\text{Gal}(K/F)| = [K:F]$ when $K/F$ is Galois.
3. Find $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q})$ — is this extension Galois?
4. State why normality of the subgroup corresponds to the intermediate field being Galois over $F$.
5. Compute the Galois group of the splitting field of $x^4-2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
