## Classification

Every finite field has $p^n$ elements for some prime $p$ and $n \geq 1$, and up to isomorphism there is **exactly one** finite field of each order $p^n$, denoted $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ or $\text{GF}(p^n)$.

$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is constructed as $\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(f(x))$ for any irreducible $f$ of degree $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

---

## Multiplicative Group

$\mathbb{F}_{p^n}^\times$ (nonzero elements under multiplication) is **cyclic** of order $p^n-1$. A generator is called a **primitive element**.

> [!example] $\mathbb{F}_4$
> $\mathbb{F}_4 = \mathbb{F}_2[x]/(x^2+x+1)$, with elements $0,1,\alpha,\alpha+1$ where $\alpha^2=\alpha+1$; $\mathbb{F}_4^\times = \{1,\alpha,\alpha+1\}$ is cyclic of order 3.

---

## Frobenius Automorphism

$\phi: x \mapsto x^p$ generates $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, since $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p$ is always Galois.

## Practice Problems

1. Construct $\mathbb{F}_9$ explicitly as $\mathbb{F}_3[x]/(f)$ for a suitable irreducible $f$.
2. Show $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}^\times$ is cyclic.
3. Find a primitive element of $\mathbb{F}_5$.
4. Show $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is generated by the Frobenius map.
5. Why is there no field with exactly 6 elements?
