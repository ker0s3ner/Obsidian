## Radical Extensions

$K/F$ is a **radical extension** if it's built by successively adjoining $n$-th roots: $K = F(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)$ with each $\alpha_i^{n_i} \in F(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{i-1})$.

A polynomial is **solvable by radicals** if its splitting field lies in some radical extension of $F$.

---

## Galois's Criterion

$f$ is solvable by radicals over $F$ (char 0) iff $\text{Gal}(K/F)$, for $K$ the splitting field, is a **solvable group** — one whose composition factors are all abelian (see [[15.1 Group Theory/15.1.6 Composition series]]).

> [!tip] Why the quintic has no formula
> $S_5$ is not solvable (its composition series has the simple non-abelian factor $A_5$), so a generic degree-5 polynomial's Galois group is $S_5$, which is not solvable — hence **no radical formula** can exist for the general quintic (Abel–Ruffini theorem).
