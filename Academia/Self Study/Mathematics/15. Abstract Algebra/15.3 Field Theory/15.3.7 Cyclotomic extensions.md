## Definition

The $n$-th **cyclotomic field** $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ is the splitting field of $x^n-1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, where $\zeta_n = e^{2\pi i/n}$.

$$[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n):\mathbb{Q}] = \varphi(n) \quad \text{(Euler's totient)}$$

---

## Galois Group

$$\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$$

via $\sigma_a(\zeta_n) = \zeta_n^a$ for $a$ coprime to $n$ — an **abelian** Galois group, always.

> [!example] Constructing regular polygons
> A regular $n$-gon is constructible with straightedge and compass iff $\varphi(n)$ is a power of 2 — directly tied to $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n):\mathbb{Q}]$ being a power of 2 (Gauss–Wantzel theorem).

---

## The Cyclotomic Polynomial

$\Phi_n(x)$, the minimal polynomial of $\zeta_n$, has degree $\varphi(n)$ and $x^n - 1 = \prod_{d\mid n} \Phi_d(x)$.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $\Phi_{12}(x)$ using $x^{12}-1 = \prod_{d\mid 12}\Phi_d(x)$.
2. Verify $\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.
3. Show a regular 7-gon is not constructible.
4. Show a regular 17-gon IS constructible (Gauss's famous result) — what is $\varphi(17)$?
5. Prove $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n):\mathbb{Q}] = \varphi(n)$ using irreducibility of $\Phi_n$.
