## Semisimplicity

$\mathfrak g$ is **simple** if it has no nontrivial ideals and isn't abelian; **semisimple** if it's a direct sum of simple Lie algebras — the Lie-algebra analogue of semisimple modules.

$\mathfrak g$ is semisimple iff its **Killing form** $\kappa(x,y) = \operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}x \circ \operatorname{ad}y)$ is nondegenerate (Cartan's criterion).

---

## The Classification (Killing–Cartan)

Every finite-dimensional simple complex Lie algebra falls into one of:

$$A_n, B_n, C_n, D_n \quad \text{(classical: } \mathfrak{sl}, \mathfrak{so}, \mathfrak{sp}\text{)}, \qquad G_2, F_4, E_6, E_7, E_8 \quad \text{(exceptional)}$$

> [!tip] Same data, drawn as a diagram
> This full classification is encoded in **Dynkin diagrams** — a picture with nodes and edges reproducing the entire root system, one of the most compressed classification results in mathematics.

## Practice Problems

1. Identify $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ in the $A_n$ family — what is $n$?
2. State Cartan's criterion for semisimplicity.
3. Show $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ is NOT semisimple (its center is an abelian ideal).
4. Draw the Dynkin diagram for $A_2$ (i.e. $\mathfrak{sl}_3$).
5. What distinguishes the exceptional Lie algebras from the classical ones?
