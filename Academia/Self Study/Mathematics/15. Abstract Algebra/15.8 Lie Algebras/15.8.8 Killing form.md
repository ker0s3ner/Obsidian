## Definition

The **Killing form** on $\mathfrak g$ is the symmetric bilinear form:

$$\kappa(x,y) = \operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}(x)\circ\operatorname{ad}(y))$$

where $\operatorname{ad}(x): \mathfrak g \to \mathfrak g$, $\operatorname{ad}(x)(z) = [x,z]$.

---

## Cartan's Criterion

$\mathfrak g$ is **semisimple** iff $\kappa$ is **nondegenerate**. $\mathfrak g$ is **solvable** iff $\kappa(x,y) = 0$ for all $x,y \in [\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]$.

> [!example] Killing form on $\mathfrak{sl}_2$
> For $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with basis $e,h,f$, $\kappa$ is nondegenerate (matching semisimplicity), and explicitly $\kappa(h,h) = 8$ in the standard normalization.

---

## Invariance

$\kappa$ is **invariant**: $\kappa([x,y],z) = \kappa(x,[y,z])$ — this invariance is what makes it useful for constructing the Casimir element and setting up an inner product on the space of roots.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the Killing form of the abelian (trivial bracket) 2-dimensional Lie algebra — is it degenerate?
2. Verify $\kappa$ is symmetric directly from the trace definition.
3. Show $\mathfrak{gl}_n$'s Killing form is degenerate (consistent with it not being semisimple).
4. State Cartan's criterion for semisimplicity precisely.
5. Explain how the Killing form induces an inner product on the roots of a semisimple Lie algebra.
