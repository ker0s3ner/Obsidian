## Definition

A representation of $\mathfrak g$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism $\rho: \mathfrak g \to \mathfrak{gl}(V)$, i.e. $\rho([x,y]) = \rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x)$.

---

## Weight Decomposition

For a representation of a semisimple $\mathfrak g$, restricting to a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ decomposes $V$ into **weight spaces** $V_\lambda$, $\lambda \in \mathfrak h^*$ — the representation-theoretic echo of the root space decomposition of $\mathfrak g$ itself.

---

## Highest Weight Theory

Every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of a semisimple Lie algebra has a unique **highest weight**, and irreducibles are classified exactly by **dominant integral weights** — a complete classification, paralleling the classification of the algebra itself.

> [!example] $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ representations
> Irreducibles of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ are indexed by $n = 0,1,2,\ldots$ (dimension $n+1$), matching the "spin-$j$" representations of $SU(2)$ from [[15.5 Representation Theory/15.5.8 Representations of Lie groups]] with $n=2j$.

## Practice Problems

1. Find the weights of the standard representation of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$.
2. Explain the correspondence between $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ irreducibles of dimension $n+1$ and spin-$j$ with $j=n/2$.
3. Verify $\rho([x,y])=\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x)$ for the adjoint representation $\rho = \operatorname{ad}$.
4. State the highest weight theorem informally.
5. Find the highest weight of the standard representation of $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ on $\mathbb{C}^3$.
