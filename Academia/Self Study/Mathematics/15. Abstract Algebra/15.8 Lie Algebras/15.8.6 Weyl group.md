## Definition

The **Weyl group** $W$ of a root system $\Phi$ is the finite group generated by reflections $s_\alpha$ across the hyperplane orthogonal to each root $\alpha$.

$$s_\alpha(\lambda) = \lambda - \frac{2(\lambda,\alpha)}{(\alpha,\alpha)}\alpha$$

---

## Examples

| Lie algebra | Weyl group |
|-------------|------------|
| $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ ($A_1$) | $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ |
| $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ ($A_2$) | $S_3$ (dihedral of order 6) |
| $\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}$ ($A_n$) | $S_{n+1}$ |

> [!example] $A_2$'s Weyl group is $D_3 \cong S_3$
> The hexagonal root system of $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ has symmetry group exactly the dihedral group of order 6, matching the permutation group $S_3$ acting on the three coordinate axes.

---

## Role in Representation Theory

The Weyl group acts on weights, and the **Weyl character formula** expresses every irreducible character as an alternating sum over $W$ — the single most important computational tool for semisimple representation theory.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the Weyl group of $A_1$ has order 2.
2. Show the Weyl group of $A_2$ acts on the hexagonal root system as its full symmetry group.
3. Verify $s_\alpha(\alpha) = -\alpha$ from the reflection formula.
4. Explain why the Weyl group of $A_n$ is $S_{n+1}$.
5. State the Weyl character formula informally.
