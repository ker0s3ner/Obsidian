## Definition

A **Lie algebra** $\mathfrak{g}$ is a vector space with a bilinear bracket $[\cdot,\cdot]$ satisfying:

1. **Antisymmetry**: $[x,y] = -[y,x]$
2. **Jacobi identity**: $[x,[y,z]] + [y,[z,x]] + [z,[x,y]] = 0$

---

## Examples

| Lie algebra | Underlying space | Bracket |
|-------------|--------------------|---------|
| $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ | $n\times n$ matrices | $[X,Y]=XY-YX$ |
| $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ | trace-0 matrices | $[X,Y]=XY-YX$ |
| $\mathbb{R}^3$ | vectors | $[x,y] = x\times y$ (cross product) |

> [!example] Cross product as a Lie bracket
> $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the cross product satisfies the Jacobi identity — this is $\mathfrak{so}(3)$, the Lie algebra of rotations.

---

## From Lie Groups

Every Lie group $G$ has a Lie algebra $\mathfrak g = T_eG$ (tangent space at identity), with bracket defined via the commutator of vector fields — the "linearization" of the group near the identity.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the Jacobi identity for $[X,Y]=XY-YX$ using associativity of matrix multiplication.
2. Show $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ is closed under the bracket (trace-0 stays trace-0).
3. Verify $\mathbb{R}^3$ with cross product satisfies antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity.
4. Compute $[E_{12}, E_{21}]$ for elementary matrices in $\mathfrak{gl}_2$.
5. Explain informally why $\mathfrak g = T_eG$ captures "infinitesimal" group structure.
