## Cartan Subalgebra and Roots

A **Cartan subalgebra** $\mathfrak h \subset \mathfrak g$ is a maximal abelian subalgebra acting diagonalizably. Decomposing $\mathfrak g$ under the adjoint action of $\mathfrak h$ gives:

$$\mathfrak g = \mathfrak h \oplus \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi} \mathfrak g_\alpha$$

where $\Phi \subset \mathfrak h^*$ is the **root system** — the nonzero weights of this decomposition.

---

## Root System Axioms

$\Phi$ is a finite spanning set of vectors closed under reflections through hyperplanes orthogonal to each root, with integrality conditions on the angles between roots — these axioms alone force $\Phi$ into one of finitely many possible shapes, giving the classification.

> [!example] $A_2 = \mathfrak{sl}_3$ root system
> Six roots forming a regular hexagon in a 2-dimensional plane — the simplest rank-2 example beyond $A_1\times A_1$.

---

## Simple Roots and Dynkin Diagrams

A choice of **simple roots** (a minimal generating subset) encodes $\Phi$ entirely; the Dynkin diagram records angles between simple roots as edges.

## Practice Problems

1. List the root system of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ (rank 1) — how many roots are there?
2. Describe the hexagonal root system of $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ geometrically.
3. Explain what "closed under reflection" means for a root system.
4. What is a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{sl}_n$ (as matrices)?
5. Show that a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ is 1-dimensional.
