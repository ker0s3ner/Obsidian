## Derived Series and Solvability

The **derived series** $\mathfrak g \supseteq [\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g] \supseteq [[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g],[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]] \supseteq \cdots$ terminates at 0 iff $\mathfrak g$ is **solvable** — the Lie-algebra analogue of the derived/commutator series for groups.

## Lower Central Series and Nilpotency

$\mathfrak g \supseteq [\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g] \supseteq [\mathfrak g,[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]] \supseteq \cdots$ terminates at 0 iff $\mathfrak g$ is **nilpotent**. Nilpotent $\implies$ solvable, not conversely.

---

## Engel's Theorem and Lie's Theorem

- **Engel's theorem**: $\mathfrak g$ is nilpotent iff every element acts nilpotently (as an operator) on any representation.
- **Lie's theorem**: over $\mathbb{C}$, every representation of a solvable Lie algebra has a common eigenvector — solvable Lie algebras act "upper-triangularizably."

> [!tip] Levi decomposition
> Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra splits as $\mathfrak g = \mathfrak r \oplus \mathfrak s$ (semidirect), with $\mathfrak r$ the solvable radical and $\mathfrak s$ semisimple — reducing general Lie algebra theory to the solvable and semisimple cases separately.
