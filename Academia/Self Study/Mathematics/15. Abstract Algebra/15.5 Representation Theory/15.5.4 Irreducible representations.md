## Definition

A representation is **irreducible** if it has no proper nonzero subrepresentation. Over $\mathbb{C}$, every representation of a finite group decomposes as a direct sum of irreducibles (Maschke's theorem).

---

## Counting Irreducibles

The number of irreducible representations (up to isomorphism) of a finite group equals the number of **conjugacy classes**.

$$\sum_i (\dim V_i)^2 = |G|$$

**Example**: $S_3$ has 3 conjugacy classes, so 3 irreducibles, with dimensions $1,1,2$: $1^2+1^2+2^2 = 6 = |S_3|$. ✓

> [!tip] Abelian groups have only 1-dimensional irreducibles
> If $G$ is abelian, every conjugacy class is a singleton, so there are $|G|$ irreducibles, forcing each to have dimension 1 by the sum-of-squares formula.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $1^2+1^2+2^2=6$ for $S_3$ matches $|S_3|$.
2. Show every irreducible representation of an abelian group is 1-dimensional.
3. Find all irreducible representations of $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.
4. How many irreducible representations does $S_4$ have? What are their dimensions (research or compute)?
5. Explain why $\sum(\dim V_i)^2 = |G|$ (cite decomposition of the regular representation).
