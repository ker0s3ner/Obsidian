## Definition

A **representation** of a group $G$ on a vector space $V$ (over a field, usually $\mathbb{C}$) is a homomorphism $\rho: G \to GL(V)$. Equivalently, $V$ is a $\mathbb{C}[G]$-module.

---

## Examples

- **Trivial representation**: $\rho(g) = 1$ for all $g$, on $V = \mathbb{C}$.
- **Regular representation**: $V = \mathbb{C}[G]$ with basis $\{e_g\}$, $\rho(g)$ permutes basis vectors by left multiplication.
- **Permutation representation**: any action of $G$ on a set $X$ gives $\rho(g)$ permuting basis vectors indexed by $X$.

> [!tip] Representations linearize group actions
> Every abstract group action on a set becomes a permutation matrix representation — turning combinatorial symmetry into linear algebra, where eigenvalues and traces become available tools.

---

## Subrepresentations and Irreducibility

$W \subseteq V$ is a **subrepresentation** if it's invariant under every $\rho(g)$. A representation with no proper nonzero subrepresentations is **irreducible**.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Describe the regular representation of $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ explicitly as matrices.
2. Show the trivial representation is always irreducible.
3. Find a subrepresentation of the permutation representation of $S_3$ on $\mathbb{C}^3$.
4. Show that a 1-dimensional representation is automatically irreducible.
5. Verify $\rho(g)$ is invertible for every $g$, directly from the homomorphism property.
