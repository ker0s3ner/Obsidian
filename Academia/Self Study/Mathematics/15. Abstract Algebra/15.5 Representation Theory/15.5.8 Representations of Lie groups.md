## Continuous Groups Need Continuous Tools

Lie groups (like $SO(3)$ or $SU(2)$) are infinite, so "sum over $G$" (as in Maschke's proof or orthogonality relations) is replaced by **integration over $G$** using the Haar measure.

---

## Compact Groups Behave Like Finite Ones

For **compact** Lie groups, every representation still decomposes into irreducibles (Peter–Weyl theorem), and character orthogonality still holds with integrals replacing sums:

$$\int_G \chi_i(g)\overline{\chi_j(g)}\, dg = \delta_{ij}$$

---

## Lie Algebra Representations

Because a Lie group is built from its Lie algebra near the identity, representations of a (connected, simply connected) Lie group correspond exactly to representations of its **Lie algebra** — turning a continuous group problem into a linear algebra problem on $\mathfrak{g}$.

> [!example] $SU(2)$ and spin
> Irreducible representations of $SU(2)$ are indexed by half-integers $j = 0, \tfrac12, 1, \tfrac32,\ldots$ (dimension $2j+1$) — the mathematical origin of "spin" in quantum mechanics.
