## Statement

If $G$ is finite and $\text{char}(F) \nmid |G|$ (in particular, always over $\mathbb{C}$ or $\mathbb{R}$), then every representation of $G$ over $F$ decomposes as a **direct sum of irreducible representations**.

---

## Proof Idea

Given any inner product on $V$, average it over $G$:

$$\langle v,w\rangle_{new} = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G} \langle \rho(g)v, \rho(g)w\rangle$$

This new inner product is **$G$-invariant**, so the orthogonal complement of any subrepresentation is also a subrepresentation — giving a direct sum decomposition by induction on dimension.

> [!warning] Fails in "bad" characteristic
> If $\text{char}(F) \mid |G|$ (modular representation theory), Maschke's theorem fails — representations may not decompose, which is why that setting requires entirely different tools.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain why averaging the inner product requires $|G|$ to be invertible in $F$.
2. Show the averaged inner product is $G$-invariant.
3. Find a representation of $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ that does NOT decompose into irreducibles.
4. Use Maschke's theorem to justify that every representation's character is a sum of irreducible characters.
5. Why does Maschke's theorem require finiteness of $G$?
