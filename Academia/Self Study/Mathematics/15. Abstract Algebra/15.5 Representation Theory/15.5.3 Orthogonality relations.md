## Inner Product on Characters

For a finite group $G$, define:

$$\langle \chi, \psi \rangle = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G} \chi(g)\overline{\psi(g)}$$

---

## First Orthogonality Relation

For irreducible characters $\chi_i, \chi_j$:

$$\langle \chi_i, \chi_j\rangle = \begin{cases} 1 & i=j \\ 0 & i\neq j\end{cases}$$

Irreducible characters form an **orthonormal set** — a powerful tool for decomposing arbitrary representations.

---

## Decomposing Representations

Any character $\chi$ decomposes as $\chi = \sum_i m_i \chi_i$ with $m_i = \langle \chi, \chi_i\rangle \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ — the multiplicity of each irreducible piece.

> [!tip] Irreducibility test
> $\rho$ is irreducible iff $\langle \chi_\rho,\chi_\rho\rangle = 1$ — a single computation checks irreducibility without finding subrepresentations directly.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the first orthogonality relation for the two irreducible characters of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Decompose the permutation character of $S_3$ on $\{1,2,3\}$ into irreducibles.
3. Use $\langle\chi,\chi\rangle=1$ to verify a given 2-dimensional representation of $S_3$ is irreducible.
4. Show the number of irreducible representations equals the number of conjugacy classes (state as fact, cite second orthogonality relation).
5. Compute $\langle \chi_{reg}, \chi_i\rangle$ for the regular representation and any irreducible $\chi_i$ — what does it tell you about multiplicities?
