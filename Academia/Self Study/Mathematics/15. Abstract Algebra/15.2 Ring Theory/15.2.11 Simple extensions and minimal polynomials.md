## Simple Extensions

$K/F$ is a **simple extension** if $K = F(\alpha)$ for a single element $\alpha$. If $\alpha$ satisfies some polynomial over $F$, it's **algebraic**; otherwise **transcendental**.

---

## Minimal Polynomial

For $\alpha$ algebraic over $F$, the **minimal polynomial** $m_\alpha(x)$ is the monic irreducible polynomial in $F[x]$ of least degree with $m_\alpha(\alpha) = 0$. It's unique, and:

$$F(\alpha) \cong F[x]/(m_\alpha(x)), \qquad [F(\alpha):F] = \deg m_\alpha$$

**Example**: $m_{\sqrt2}(x) = x^2 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, so $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2):\mathbb{Q}] = 2$.

> [!tip] Adjoining a root builds a field, not just a ring
> $F[x]/(m_\alpha(x))$ is a field precisely because $m_\alpha$ is irreducible, so the quotient is by a maximal ideal (see [[15.2.2 Ideals/15.2.2.1 Prime and maximal ideals]]).

## Practice Problems

1. Find the minimal polynomial of $\sqrt[3]{2}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and compute $[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]2):\mathbb{Q}]$.
2. Show $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2) \cong \mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2-2)$ explicitly.
3. Is $\pi$ algebraic or transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$? What does this say about $\mathbb{Q}(\pi)$?
4. Show that every simple algebraic extension is finite.
5. Find the minimal polynomial of $i$ over $\mathbb{R}$ and over $\mathbb{Q}$.
