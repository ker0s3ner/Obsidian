## Division Algorithm

For a field $F$, given $f, g \in F[x]$ with $g \neq 0$, there exist unique $q, r \in F[x]$ with:

$$f = qg + r, \qquad \deg r < \deg g$$

This makes $F[x]$ a **Euclidean domain**, hence a PID (see [[15.2.4 UFDs and PIDs]]).

---

## Factor Theorem

$\alpha$ is a root of $f$ iff $(x - \alpha) \mid f(x)$. Iterating: $f$ of degree $n$ has **at most $n$ roots** in any field (counted with multiplicity).

**Example**: $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6$ has root $x=1$, so $f(x) = (x-1)(x^2-5x+6) = (x-1)(x-2)(x-3)$.

> [!tip] Synthetic division for speed
> Once a root is found, synthetic division extracts the quotient $q(x)$ in the factor theorem quickly, without full polynomial long division.

---

## Multiple Roots and Derivatives

$\alpha$ is a **repeated root** of $f$ iff $f(\alpha) = 0$ and $f'(\alpha) = 0$ — checked via $\gcd(f, f')$.

## Practice Problems

1. Divide $x^4 - 1$ by $x - 1$ using the division algorithm.
2. Use the factor theorem to fully factor $x^3 - 2x^2 - x + 2$.
3. Show a nonzero polynomial of degree $n$ over a field has at most $n$ roots.
4. Find the repeated roots of $x^3 - 3x + 2$ using $\gcd(f, f')$.
5. Explain why the division algorithm fails over $\mathbb{Z}$ (a ring, not a field) in general.
