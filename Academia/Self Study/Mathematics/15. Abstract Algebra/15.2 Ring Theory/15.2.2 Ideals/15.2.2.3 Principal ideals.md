## Definition

An ideal generated by a single element, $(a) = \{ra : r \in R\}$, is a **principal ideal**.

**Example**: in $\mathbb{Z}$, every ideal is principal — $(n) = n\mathbb{Z}$.

---

## Principal Ideal Domains

A domain where **every** ideal is principal is a **PID** — $\mathbb{Z}$ and $k[x]$ (for a field $k$) are the standard examples; full theory in [[15.2.4 UFDs and PIDs]].

> [!warning] Not all rings are PIDs
> $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is not a PID: $(2, x)$ (elements with even constant term) is not generated by a single polynomial.

---

## Divisibility and Principal Ideals

$$a \mid b \iff (b) \subseteq (a)$$

so principal ideal containment exactly encodes divisibility — a useful translation between ring theory and elementary number theory.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Show $(2,x)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is not principal.
2. Verify $a \mid b \iff (b) \subseteq (a)$.
3. Find generators for $(4) \cap (6)$ and $(4)+(6)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.
4. Show every ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ is principal, using the division algorithm.
5. Are units characterized by $(u) = R$? Prove it.
