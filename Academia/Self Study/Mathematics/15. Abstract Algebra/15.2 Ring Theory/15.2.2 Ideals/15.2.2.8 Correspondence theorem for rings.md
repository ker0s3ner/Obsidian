## Statement

For an ideal $I \trianglelefteq R$, there is a bijection between:

- ideals $J$ with $I \subseteq J \subseteq R$, and
- ideals of $R/I$

given by $J \mapsto J/I$. This bijection preserves inclusion, and $J$ is prime (resp. maximal) in $R$ iff $J/I$ is prime (resp. maximal) in $R/I$.

---

## Consequence

Maximal ideals of $R/I$ correspond exactly to maximal ideals of $R$ containing $I$ — this is how one locates maximal ideals of a quotient without redoing the analysis from scratch.

> [!example] $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$
> Ideals of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ correspond to ideals of $\mathbb{Z}$ containing $(12)$, i.e. $(d)$ for $d \mid 12$ — matching the divisor lattice, exactly parallel to the group-theoretic version in [[15.1 Group Theory/15.1.3 Normal subgroups/15.1.3.6 Correspondence theorem]].

## Practice Problems

1. List all ideals of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ using the correspondence theorem.
2. Prove $J$ is prime in $R$ iff $J/I$ is prime in $R/I$ (for $I \subseteq J$).
3. Use the theorem to find all maximal ideals of $k[x]/(x^2-1)$ for a field $k$ where $-1$ isn't a square issue (e.g. $k = \mathbb{Q}$).
4. Explain the analogy with the group-theoretic correspondence theorem.
5. Show the bijection preserves ideal containment.
