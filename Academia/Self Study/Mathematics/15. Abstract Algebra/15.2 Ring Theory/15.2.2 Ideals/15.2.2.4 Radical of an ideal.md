## Definition

The **radical** of an ideal $I$ is:

$$\sqrt{I} = \{r \in R : r^n \in I \text{ for some } n \geq 1\}$$

Always $I \subseteq \sqrt I$; $I$ is **radical** if $I = \sqrt I$.

---

## Key Facts

- $\sqrt I$ is always an ideal.
- Every prime ideal is radical (immediate from the prime definition).
- $\sqrt{I \cap J} = \sqrt I \cap \sqrt J$.

**Example**: in $\mathbb{Z}$, $\sqrt{(12)} = (6)$, since $12 = 2^2\cdot 3$ and the radical strips repeated prime factors.

> [!tip] Geometric meaning
> Over $\mathbb{C}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, the Nullstellensatz says $\sqrt I$ is exactly the set of polynomials vanishing on the zero set of $I$ — radical ideals correspond precisely to varieties.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $\sqrt{(12)}$ and $\sqrt{(8)}$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Show every prime ideal is radical.
3. Prove $\sqrt I$ is always an ideal.
4. Find $\sqrt{(x^2)}$ in $k[x]$.
5. Verify $\sqrt{I\cap J} = \sqrt I \cap \sqrt J$ on an example.
