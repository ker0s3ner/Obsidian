## Definition

For $S \subseteq R$, the ideal **generated** by $S$ is the smallest ideal containing $S$:

$$(S) = \bigcap_{S \subseteq I \trianglelefteq R} I$$

For commutative $R$ with unity, $(S) = \left\{ \sum_{i} r_i s_i : r_i \in R, s_i \in S, \text{finite sum} \right\}$.

---

## Finitely Generated Ideals

$(a_1, \ldots, a_n) = Ra_1 + \cdots + Ra_n$ — the sum of principal ideals. A ring where every ideal is finitely generated is **Noetherian** (see [[15.7 Commutative Algebra/15.7.1 Noetherian rings]]).

**Example**: $(2, x) \subset \mathbb{Z}[x]$ consists of all $2f(x) + xg(x)$.

> [!tip] Generated ideal ↔ generated subgroup
> This is the exact ring-theoretic analogue of a generated subgroup — "smallest structure containing a set."

## Practice Problems

1. Describe $(2,x)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ explicitly and show $1 \notin (2,x)$.
2. Show $(S)$ as defined is indeed the smallest ideal containing $S$.
3. Find $(4,6)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ and simplify to a principal ideal.
4. Is every finitely generated ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ principal? Give a counterexample if not.
5. Show $(a_1,\ldots,a_n) = Ra_1+\cdots+Ra_n$ for commutative $R$.
