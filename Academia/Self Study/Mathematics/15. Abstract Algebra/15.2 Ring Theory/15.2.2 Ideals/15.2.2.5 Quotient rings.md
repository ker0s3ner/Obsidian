## Definition

For an ideal $I \trianglelefteq R$, the **quotient ring** $R/I$ consists of cosets $r+I$, with:

$$(r+I)+(s+I) = (r+s)+I, \qquad (r+I)(s+I) = rs+I$$

Well-defined precisely because $I$ absorbs multiplication by $R$.

---

## Examples

- $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$: the prototypical quotient ring.
- $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2+1) \cong \mathbb{C}$: adjoining a root of $x^2+1$ builds the complex numbers algebraically.

> [!example] Why $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb{C}$
> In the quotient, $x^2 = -1$, so $x$ behaves exactly like $i$ — every element is $a+bx$ with $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$, matching $a+bi$.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2+1)\cong\mathbb{C}$ by exhibiting the isomorphism explicitly.
2. Show that multiplication in $R/I$ is well-defined only because $I$ is an ideal (not just a subring).
3. Describe $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x, 5)$.
4. Show $R/I$ is a domain iff $I$ is prime.
5. Compute $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(2)$ and determine its size.
