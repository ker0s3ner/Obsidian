## Definition

The **nilradical** of $R$ is $\mathfrak{N}(R) = \sqrt{(0)}$ — the set of all **nilpotent** elements ($x^n = 0$ for some $n$).

---

## Key Fact

$$\mathfrak{N}(R) = \bigcap_{P \text{ prime}} P$$

the intersection of **all** prime ideals of $R$ — a purely algebraic set (nilpotents) coincides with an intersection over all primes.

> [!example] Nilpotents in $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$
> $6^2 = 36 \equiv 0 \pmod{12}$, so $6$ is nilpotent, while $\mathfrak N(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}) = \{0, 6\}$.

---

## Reduced Rings

$R$ is **reduced** if $\mathfrak N(R) = 0$ (no nonzero nilpotents). $R/\mathfrak N(R)$ is always reduced, for any $R$.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Find $\mathfrak N(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})$ directly by testing elements.
2. Show $\mathfrak N(R) = \bigcap_{\text{prime } P} P$ (statement — proof uses prime avoidance, may just cite it).
3. Prove $R/\mathfrak N(R)$ is reduced.
4. Is $\mathbb{Z}$ reduced? Is $k[x]/(x^2)$?
5. Show nilpotents form an ideal (i.e. $\mathfrak N(R)$ is closed under $+$ and absorbs multiplication).
