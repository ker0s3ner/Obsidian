## Ideals

A subset $I \subseteq R$ is an **ideal** if it's an additive subgroup closed under multiplication by all of $R$: $r \in R, x \in I \implies rx \in I$.

Ideals are exactly the kernels of ring homomorphisms, and $R/I$ is a ring precisely when $I$ is an ideal — the ring-theoretic analogue of normal subgroups.

---

## Prime Ideals

$P \subsetneq R$ is **prime** if $ab \in P \implies a \in P$ or $b \in P$. Equivalently, $R/P$ is an **integral domain**.

**Example**: in $\mathbb{Z}$, $(p) = p\mathbb{Z}$ is prime iff $p$ is a prime number.

---

## Maximal Ideals

$M \subsetneq R$ is **maximal** if no ideal lies strictly between $M$ and $R$. Equivalently, $R/M$ is a **field**.

> [!tip] Maximal $\implies$ prime
> Every maximal ideal is prime, since fields are integral domains. The converse fails: $(0) \subset \mathbb{Z}$ is prime but not maximal, since $\mathbb{Z}$ is a domain but not a field.

---

## Comparison Table

| Ideal type | Quotient $R/I$ is | Example in $\mathbb{Z}$ |
|------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Prime | Integral domain | $(p)$ for prime $p$, or $(0)$ |
| Maximal | Field | $(p)$ for prime $p$ |

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $R/I$ is a field iff $I$ is maximal.
2. Verify $(x) \subset \mathbb{R}[x]$ is prime but not maximal.
3. Show $(x, y) \subset \mathbb{R}[x,y]$ is maximal.
4. Prove every maximal ideal is prime.
5. Find all prime ideals of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$.
