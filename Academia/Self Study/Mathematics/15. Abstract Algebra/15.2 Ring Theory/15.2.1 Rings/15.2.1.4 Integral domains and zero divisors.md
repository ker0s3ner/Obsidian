## Zero Divisors

$a \neq 0$ is a **zero divisor** if $ab = 0$ for some $b \neq 0$. A commutative ring with unity and no zero divisors is an **integral domain**.

**Example**: $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ has zero divisors ($2\cdot3=0$); $\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$ does not, since 7 is prime.

---

## Cancellation

In an integral domain, $ab = ac$ and $a \neq 0$ imply $b = c$ — cancellation holds exactly when there are no zero divisors.

## Finite Domains Are Fields

> [!tip] A finiteness shortcut
> Any finite integral domain is automatically a field: multiplication by a fixed nonzero $a$ is injective (no zero divisors), hence surjective on a finite set, so some product equals $1$.

## Practice Problems

1. Find all zero divisors in $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Prove cancellation holds iff there are no zero divisors.
3. Show $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is an integral domain iff $n$ is prime.
4. Prove every finite integral domain is a field.
5. Is $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ an integral domain? Justify.
