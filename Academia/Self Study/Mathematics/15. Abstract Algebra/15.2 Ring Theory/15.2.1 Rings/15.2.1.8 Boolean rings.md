## Definition

A **Boolean ring** is a ring in which every element is idempotent: $x^2 = x$ for all $x \in R$.

**Example**: the power set $\mathcal{P}(X)$ of a set $X$, with $+ = $ symmetric difference and $\cdot = $ intersection, is a Boolean ring.

---

## Basic Consequences

- $\text{char}(R) = 2$: from $x^2=x$ applied to $x = 1+1$, one derives $2x=0$ for all $x$.
- $R$ is automatically **commutative**: expand $(x+y)^2 = x+y$ to get $xy+yx=0$, and combined with $2x=0$ this forces $xy=yx$.

> [!example] Boolean algebra ↔ Boolean ring
> Every Boolean algebra (with $\vee,\wedge,\neg$) becomes a Boolean ring via $x+y = (x\wedge\neg y)\vee(\neg x\wedge y)$ (symmetric difference) and $xy = x \wedge y$ — the algebraic and lattice-theoretic pictures of logic coincide.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\mathcal{P}(X)$ with symmetric difference and intersection satisfies $x^2=x$.
2. Show every Boolean ring has characteristic 2.
3. Prove every Boolean ring is commutative.
4. Show that in a Boolean ring, every prime ideal is maximal.
5. Is $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ a Boolean ring? Is $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$?
