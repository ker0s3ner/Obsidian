## Definition

A **ring homomorphism** $\varphi: R \to S$ satisfies $\varphi(a+b) = \varphi(a)+\varphi(b)$ and $\varphi(ab) = \varphi(a)\varphi(b)$ (and $\varphi(1_R) = 1_S$ if both rings have unity and this is required).

---

## Kernel and Image

$\ker\varphi = \{r \in R : \varphi(r) = 0\}$ is always an **ideal** of $R$ (the ring analogue of a normal subgroup); $\operatorname{im}\varphi$ is a subring of $S$.

## First Isomorphism Theorem for Rings

$$R/\ker\varphi \;\cong\; \operatorname{im}\varphi$$

**Example**: evaluation map $\varphi: \mathbb{R}[x] \to \mathbb{R}$, $\varphi(f) = f(0)$, has kernel $(x)$, so $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x) \cong \mathbb{R}$.

> [!tip] Same shape as groups
> Ring homomorphism theory mirrors group homomorphism theory exactly, with "ideal" replacing "normal subgroup."

## Practice Problems

1. Show $\ker\varphi$ is always an ideal.
2. Verify $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x) \cong \mathbb{R}$ via the evaluation map.
3. Find the kernel of the reduction map $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.
4. Show that a ring homomorphism sends units to units when both rings have unity and $\varphi(1)=1$.
5. Give an example of an injective ring homomorphism that is not surjective.
