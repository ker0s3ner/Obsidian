## Definition

A **ring** $(R, +, \cdot)$ is a set with two operations such that:

1. $(R, +)$ is an abelian group with identity $0$
2. $\cdot$ is associative
3. Distributivity: $a(b+c) = ab+ac$ and $(a+b)c = ac+bc$

A ring is **commutative** if $ab = ba$; it has **unity** if there's a multiplicative identity $1$.

---

## Examples

| Ring | Commutative? | Has unity? |
|------|---------------|------------|
| $\mathbb{Z}$ | Yes | Yes |
| $M_n(\mathbb{R})$ (matrices) | No (for $n \geq 2$) | Yes |
| $2\mathbb{Z}$ | Yes | No |
| $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ | Yes | Yes |
| Polynomials $R[x]$ | inherits from $R$ | inherits from $R$ |

> [!example] Zero divisors
> In $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$, $2 \cdot 3 = 0$ even though neither factor is zero — $2$ and $3$ are **zero divisors**. A ring with none (other than trivially via $0$) is an **integral domain**.

---

## Units

$u \in R$ is a **unit** if it has a multiplicative inverse. The units of $R$ form a group $R^\times$ under multiplication.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ satisfies all ring axioms but is not commutative.
2. Find all zero divisors in $\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}$.
3. Find $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$ for $n = 8$.
4. Show that a finite integral domain is automatically a field.
5. Verify distributivity for $2\mathbb{Z}$ and explain why it lacks unity.
