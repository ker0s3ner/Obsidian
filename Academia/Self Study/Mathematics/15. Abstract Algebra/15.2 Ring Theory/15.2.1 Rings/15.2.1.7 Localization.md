## Definition

**Localization** generalizes the field-of-fractions construction: for a commutative ring $R$ and a multiplicative subset $T \subseteq R$ (closed under products, containing 1, no zero), the localization $T^{-1}R$ consists of fractions $r/t$ with $r \in R$, $t \in T$.

**Example**: localizing $\mathbb{Z}$ at $T = \{1,2,4,8,\ldots\}$ gives $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12]$, integers with denominators a power of 2.

---

## Localizing at a Prime Ideal

For a prime ideal $P$, take $T = R \setminus P$. The result $R_P$ is a **local ring** — it has a unique maximal ideal, $PR_P$.

> [!tip] "Local" means one maximal ideal
> This is where the term "local ring" comes from: localizing at $P$ produces a ring where $P$ is the *only* maximal ideal, letting you study $R$'s behavior "near" $P$ in isolation.

---

## Relation to Fraction Fields

If $R$ is a domain and $T = R \setminus \{0\}$, localization recovers exactly $\text{Frac}(R)$ from [[15.2.7 Fraction fields]] — the fraction field is the "maximal" localization.

## Practice Problems

1. Describe $\mathbb{Z}$ localized at $T = \{1,3,9,27,\ldots\}$.
2. Show $R_P$ has $PR_P$ as its unique maximal ideal.
3. Verify localizing a domain at $R\setminus\{0\}$ gives $\text{Frac}(R)$.
4. What goes wrong if $T$ contains a zero divisor of $R$?
5. Localize $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ at $T = \{1,2,4,8,\ldots\} \pmod{12}$ and describe the result.
