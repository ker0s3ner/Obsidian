## Definition

The **characteristic** of a ring $R$ with unity is the smallest $n > 0$ such that $n \cdot 1_R = 0$ (i.e. $1_R$ added to itself $n$ times); if no such $n$ exists, $\text{char}(R) = 0$.

**Examples**: $\text{char}(\mathbb{Z}) = 0$; $\text{char}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}) = n$; $\text{char}(\mathbb{F}_p) = p$.

---

## Characteristic of a Domain Is 0 or Prime

If $R$ is an integral domain, $\text{char}(R)$ is either $0$ or a **prime** $p$ — if $n = ab$ with $1 < a,b<n$, then $(a\cdot 1)(b \cdot 1) = 0$ would force a zero divisor unless $n$ is prime.

> [!example] The Frobenius endomorphism
> In characteristic $p$, the map $x \mapsto x^p$ is a ring homomorphism (using $(x+y)^p = x^p+y^p$, since all other binomial coefficients are divisible by $p$) — a key tool in positive-characteristic algebra.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $\text{char}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}) = n$.
2. Prove that the characteristic of an integral domain is $0$ or prime.
3. Verify $(x+y)^p \equiv x^p + y^p \pmod p$ for small $p$.
4. Find the characteristic of $\mathbb{F}_2 \times \mathbb{F}_3$ — is it even well-defined as a single prime?
5. Show that a field of characteristic $0$ contains a copy of $\mathbb{Q}$.
