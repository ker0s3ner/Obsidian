## Definition

$S \subseteq R$ is a **subring** if it's closed under $+, -, \times$ and contains $0$ (and $1$, if requiring unital subrings).

**Examples**: $\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Q} \subseteq \mathbb{R} \subseteq \mathbb{C}$; $\mathbb{Z}[i] = \{a+bi : a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\} \subseteq \mathbb{C}$.

---

## Subring vs. Ideal

Every ideal is a subring (additively closed, and absorbs multiplication), but not every subring is an ideal — $\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$ is a subring but not an ideal (it doesn't absorb multiplication by all of $\mathbb{Q}$).

> [!warning] Don't conflate the two
> Ideals are the "kernels" that matter for quotients; subrings need not interact with quotients at all.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ is a subring of $\mathbb{C}$.
2. Show $\mathbb{Z}$ is a subring but not an ideal of $\mathbb{Q}$.
3. Find all subrings of $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$.
4. Show the intersection of two subrings is a subring.
5. Give an example of a subring with a different multiplicative identity than the ambient ring.
