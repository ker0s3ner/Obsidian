## Statement

Let $I_1, \ldots, I_k$ be ideals of a commutative ring $R$ with unity, pairwise **comaximal** ($I_i + I_j = R$ for $i \neq j$). Then:

$$R \big/ \left(\bigcap_i I_i\right) \;\cong\; \prod_i R/I_i$$

---

## Classical Case

For $R = \mathbb{Z}$ and pairwise coprime $n_1, \ldots, n_k$:

$$\mathbb{Z}/(n_1\cdots n_k)\mathbb{Z} \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}/n_1\mathbb{Z} \times \cdots \times \mathbb{Z}/n_k\mathbb{Z}$$

recovering the familiar Chinese Remainder Theorem for simultaneous congruences.

> [!example] Solving congruences
> $x \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, $x \equiv 3 \pmod 5$ has a unique solution mod 15, since $\gcd(3,5)=1$: $x \equiv 8 \pmod{15}$.

---

## Why Comaximality Matters

$I_i + I_j = R$ ensures the natural map $R \to \prod R/I_i$ is **surjective** — without it, not every combination of residues is achievable.

## Practice Problems

1. Solve $x \equiv 1 \pmod 4$, $x \equiv 2 \pmod 9$ using CRT.
2. Verify $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ via the ring isomorphism.
3. Show the map $R \to \prod R/I_i$ has kernel $\bigcap_i I_i$.
4. Explain why CRT fails for $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (i.e. why $(4)$ and $(6)$ aren't comaximal in $\mathbb{Z}$).
5. State CRT for polynomial rings $k[x]/(f) $ with $f = f_1 f_2$, $\gcd(f_1,f_2)=1$.
