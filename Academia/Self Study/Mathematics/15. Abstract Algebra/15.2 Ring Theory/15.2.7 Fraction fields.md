## Definition

For an integral domain $R$, the **field of fractions** $\text{Frac}(R)$ is the smallest field containing $R$, built from formal fractions:

$$\text{Frac}(R) = \left\{ \frac{a}{b} : a, b \in R, \ b \neq 0 \right\} / \sim, \qquad \frac{a}{b} \sim \frac{c}{d} \iff ad = bc$$

**Example**: $\text{Frac}(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Q}$.

---

## Universal Property

$\text{Frac}(R)$ is the smallest field containing $R$ as a subring: any injective ring homomorphism $R \hookrightarrow F$ into a field $F$ extends uniquely to $\text{Frac}(R) \hookrightarrow F$.

> [!example] Rational functions
> $\text{Frac}(k[x]) = k(x)$, the field of rational functions — this is exactly how "dividing polynomials" is made rigorous.

---

## Why Integral Domain Is Required

The construction needs $b \neq 0 \implies ab \neq 0$ for all $a\neq 0$ to make $\sim$ an equivalence relation avoiding contradictions — this fails if $R$ has zero divisors.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\sim$ is an equivalence relation, using that $R$ has no zero divisors.
2. Show $\text{Frac}(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Q}$.
3. Describe $\text{Frac}(k[x])$ and give an example of an element not in $k[x]$ itself.
4. Explain why $\text{Frac}(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})$ is not defined the usual way.
5. State and verify the universal property for $R = \mathbb{Z}$, $F = \mathbb{R}$.
