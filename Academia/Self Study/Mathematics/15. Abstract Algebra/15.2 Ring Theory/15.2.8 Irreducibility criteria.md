## Irreducible Polynomials

$f \in R[x]$ is **irreducible** if it cannot be written as a product of two lower-degree polynomials in $R[x]$ — the polynomial analogue of a prime number.

---

## Eisenstein's Criterion

For $f(x) = a_nx^n + \cdots + a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, if there's a prime $p$ with:

$$p \mid a_0, \ldots, a_{n-1}, \quad p \nmid a_n, \quad p^2 \nmid a_0$$

then $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Example**: $x^4 + 2$ is irreducible by Eisenstein with $p=2$.

---

## Rational Root Theorem

Any rational root $p/q$ (in lowest terms) of $f(x) = a_nx^n + \cdots + a_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$ satisfies $p \mid a_0$ and $q \mid a_n$ — useful for ruling out linear factors.

> [!tip] Reduction mod $p$
> If $f \bmod p$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_p$ for some prime $p$ not dividing the leading coefficient, then $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ — often easier than Eisenstein when it applies.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $x^3 - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ using Eisenstein.
2. Use the rational root theorem to test $x^3 - x - 1$ for rational roots.
3. Show $x^2 + x + 1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$ by reduction, and conclude about a related integer polynomial.
4. Give an example where Eisenstein's criterion fails to apply but the polynomial is still irreducible.
5. Prove the rational root theorem.
