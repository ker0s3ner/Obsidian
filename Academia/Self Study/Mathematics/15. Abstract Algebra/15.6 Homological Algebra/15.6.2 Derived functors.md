## The Problem

A functor like $\text{Hom}(-,N)$ or $M\otimes-$ is often **not exact** — applying it to a short exact sequence can break exactness at one end. Derived functors systematically measure this failure.

---

## Construction via Resolutions

To compute the derived functors of $F$ at $M$: take a **projective resolution** $\cdots \to P_1 \to P_0 \to M \to 0$, apply $F$ to the $P_i$ (dropping $M$), and take homology of the resulting complex.

$$L_iF(M) = H_i(F(P_\bullet))$$

The result is independent of the resolution chosen — a key technical theorem.

> [!tip] Why "derived"
> $L_0F(M) = F(M)$ recovers the original functor; the higher $L_iF$ are new invariants "derived" from it, vanishing exactly when $F$ was already exact.

## Practice Problems

1. Explain why a projective resolution is needed rather than just any resolution.
2. Show $L_0F(M) \cong F(M)$ for right-exact $F$.
3. Compute a projective resolution of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module.
4. Explain informally why the result doesn't depend on the choice of resolution.
5. What does it mean for $F$ to be exact in terms of vanishing derived functors?
