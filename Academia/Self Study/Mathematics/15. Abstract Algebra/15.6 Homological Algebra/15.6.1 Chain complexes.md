## Definition

A **chain complex** is a sequence of modules with maps $\partial_i: C_i \to C_{i-1}$ satisfying $\partial_{i-1}\circ\partial_i = 0$:

$$\cdots \to C_2 \xrightarrow{\partial_2} C_1 \xrightarrow{\partial_1} C_0 \to 0$$

$\partial^2 = 0$ means $\operatorname{im}\partial_{i+1} \subseteq \ker\partial_i$ — the complex is "exact up to a defect."

---

## Homology

$$H_i(C_\bullet) = \ker\partial_i \big/ \operatorname{im}\partial_{i+1}$$

$H_i = 0$ for all $i$ iff the complex is exact — homology exactly measures **failure of exactness**.

> [!tip] Cochain complexes
> A **cochain complex** has maps $d^i: C^i \to C^{i+1}$ raising the index, with $H^i = \ker d^i/\operatorname{im} d^{i-1}$ — the same idea, arrows reversed, used for cohomology.

---

## Chain Maps and Quasi-Isomorphisms

A **chain map** $f: C_\bullet \to D_\bullet$ commutes with the boundary maps, inducing maps $H_i(C) \to H_i(D)$. A **quasi-isomorphism** is a chain map inducing isomorphisms on all homology.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\partial^2=0$ for the standard simplicial boundary map on a triangle.
2. Compute $H_0, H_1$ of the complex $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{2} \mathbb{Z} \to 0$.
3. Show a chain map induces well-defined maps on homology.
4. Give an example of a chain map that is not a quasi-isomorphism.
5. Show that an exact complex has zero homology everywhere.
