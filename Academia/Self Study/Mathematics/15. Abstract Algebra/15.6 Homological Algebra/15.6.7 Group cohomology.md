## Definition

For a group $G$ and a $G$-module $M$ (an abelian group with $G$ acting), **group cohomology** $H^i(G,M)$ is defined via $\text{Ext}^i_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$ — measuring how far $M$ is from having a nice $G$-action structure.

$$H^0(G,M) = M^G \quad \text{(the $G$-invariants)}$$

---

## $H^1$ and Extensions

$H^1(G,M)$ classifies **crossed homomorphisms** modulo "trivial" ones, and controls the classification of group extensions of $G$ by $M$ (short exact sequences $0 \to M \to E \to G \to 1$).

> [!example] Where this appears
> Group cohomology of Galois groups ($H^i(\text{Gal}(K/F), M)$) is central to modern algebraic number theory — e.g. Hilbert's Theorem 90 identifies $H^1(\text{Gal}(K/F), K^\times) = 0$.
