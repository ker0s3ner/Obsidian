## Projective Resolutions

A **projective resolution** of $M$ is an exact sequence:

$$\cdots \to P_2 \to P_1 \to P_0 \to M \to 0$$

with every $P_i$ projective. Every module has one, since free modules (always projective) can always be used: surject a free module onto $M$, then onto the kernel, and so on.

## Injective Resolutions

Dually, an **injective resolution** $0 \to M \to Q^0 \to Q^1 \to \cdots$ with every $Q^i$ injective, computing right-derived functors like $\text{Ext}$ from the other side.

> [!tip] Two routes to the same Ext
> $\text{Ext}^i(M,N)$ can be computed either from a projective resolution of $M$ or an injective resolution of $N$ — both give the same answer, a nontrivial and useful fact ("balancing Ext").

## Practice Problems

1. Construct a projective resolution of $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module.
2. Explain why free modules being projective guarantees resolutions always exist.
3. State the injective resolution analogue for $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ (may cite existence of injective hulls).
4. Show that if $M$ is already projective, the resolution $0 \to M \to M \to 0$ works.
5. Explain informally why $\text{Ext}$ computed from either side agrees.
