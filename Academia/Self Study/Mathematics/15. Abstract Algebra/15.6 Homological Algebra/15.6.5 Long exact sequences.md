## The Snake Lemma / Zig-Zag Construction

A short exact sequence of chain complexes $0 \to A_\bullet \to B_\bullet \to C_\bullet \to 0$ induces a **long exact sequence** in homology:

$$\cdots \to H_i(A) \to H_i(B) \to H_i(C) \xrightarrow{\partial} H_{i-1}(A) \to H_{i-1}(B) \to \cdots$$

The **connecting homomorphism** $\partial$ is constructed by a "zig-zag" chase through the diagram (the snake lemma).

---

## Why This Is the Central Tool

Long exact sequences let you compute an unknown homology group from two known ones, by exploiting exactness (each term is determined up to the image/kernel of its neighbors).

> [!tip] Mayer–Vietoris as an instance
> The Mayer–Vietoris sequence in topology is exactly this construction applied to a decomposition of a space into two overlapping pieces — algebra doing the topological work.

## Practice Problems

1. State the snake lemma precisely.
2. Given $H_0(A)=H_0(C)=0$ and the long exact sequence, what can you conclude about $H_0(B)$?
3. Construct the long exact sequence for $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{n} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ viewed as complexes concentrated in one degree.
4. Explain informally why the connecting map "shifts degree by one."
5. Use a long exact sequence to compute $H_1$ of a space given $H_0,H_2$ of pieces via Mayer–Vietoris (schematic answer is fine).
