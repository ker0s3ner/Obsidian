## Motivation

A long exact sequence handles one filtration; a **spectral sequence** handles a doubly-indexed filtration — organizing an infinite sequence of approximations that converge to the object you actually want to compute.

---

## The Basic Shape

A spectral sequence consists of pages $E_r^{p,q}$ with differentials $d_r$, each page's homology producing the next page: $E_{r+1} = H(E_r, d_r)$. Under convergence, $E_\infty^{p,q}$ assembles (via associated graded pieces) into the target object.

> [!tip] Reading a spectral sequence
> Most first encounters just need: "if the spectral sequence **collapses** (most differentials vanish), the answer is read directly off one page" — full generality is rarely needed for a first pass.

---

## Where They Appear

Grothendieck spectral sequences (composing derived functors), the Leray–Serre spectral sequence (fibrations, see [[14. Algebraic Topology/14.2 Algebraic Topology II/14.2.3 Spectral sequences (intro)]]), and the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre sequence (group extensions in group cohomology) are the standard entry points.
