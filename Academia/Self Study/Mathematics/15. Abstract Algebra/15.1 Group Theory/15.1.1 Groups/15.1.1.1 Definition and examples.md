## Definition

A **group** is a set $G$ with a binary operation $\cdot : G \times G \to G$ satisfying:

1. **Associativity**: $(ab)c = a(bc)$ for all $a,b,c \in G$
2. **Identity**: there exists $e \in G$ with $ea = ae = a$ for all $a$
3. **Inverses**: for each $a \in G$, there exists $a^{-1} \in G$ with $aa^{-1} = a^{-1}a = e$

A group is **abelian** if $ab = ba$ for all $a, b$.

---

## Basic Examples

| Group | Operation | Identity | Abelian? |
|-------|-----------|----------|----------|
| $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$ | addition | $0$ | Yes |
| $(\mathbb{R}^\times, \cdot)$ | multiplication | $1$ | Yes |
| $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ | matrix multiplication | $I$ | No (for $n \geq 2$) |
| $S_n$ (symmetric group) | composition | identity permutation | No (for $n \geq 3$) |
| $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, +)$ | addition mod $n$ | $0$ | Yes |

> [!example] Checking the axioms for $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$
> Matrix multiplication is associative, $I$ is the identity, and every invertible matrix has an inverse by definition — so $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ is a group, but not abelian once $n \geq 2$ since matrix multiplication doesn't commute.

---

## Elementary Consequences

- The identity is **unique**.
- Inverses are **unique**.
- $(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1}a^{-1}$ (order reverses).
- **Cancellation**: $ab = ac \implies b = c$.

---

## Order of a Group

$|G|$ denotes the number of elements of $G$. A group with $|G| < \infty$ is a **finite group**; otherwise it's **infinite**.

> [!note] Groups vs. monoids
> Dropping the inverse axiom gives a **monoid**. Dropping associativity gives structures like **loops**, rarely used outside specialized algebra.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify that $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, +)$ satisfies all three group axioms.
2. Show that the identity element of a group is unique.
3. Prove $(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1}a^{-1}$.
4. Is the set of $2\times 2$ matrices with determinant $1$ under multiplication a group? Justify.
5. Give an example of a monoid that is not a group.
