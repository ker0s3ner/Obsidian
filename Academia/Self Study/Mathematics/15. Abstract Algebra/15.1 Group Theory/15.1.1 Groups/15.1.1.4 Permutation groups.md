## Definition

The **symmetric group** $S_n$ is the group of all bijections (permutations) of $\{1, \ldots, n\}$ under composition. $|S_n| = n!$.

---

## Cycle Notation

A permutation is written as a product of disjoint **cycles**. E.g. $(1\,3\,2)$ sends $1\to3$, $3\to2$, $2\to1$, fixing everything else.

**Example**: in $S_5$, $(1\,2\,3)(4\,5)$ is the permutation $1\to2, 2\to3, 3\to1, 4\to5, 5\to4$.

---

## Even and Odd Permutations

Every permutation decomposes into a product of **transpositions** (2-cycles). The parity of the number of transpositions is well-defined, giving the **sign**:

$$\text{sgn}(\sigma) = (-1)^{\#\text{transpositions}}$$

The **alternating group** $A_n = \ker(\text{sgn})$ consists of all even permutations, with $|A_n| = n!/2$.

> [!warning] Cycle length vs. parity
> A $k$-cycle is a product of $k-1$ transpositions, so it's **even** iff $k$ is odd. Don't confuse cycle length with parity directly.

---

## Order of a Permutation

The order of a permutation equals the **lcm of its cycle lengths**.

**Example**: $(1\,2\,3)(4\,5)$ has cycles of length 3 and 2, so its order is $\text{lcm}(3,2) = 6$.

## Practice Problems

1. Write $(1\,2\,3\,4)$ as a product of transpositions and determine its parity.
2. Find the order of $(1\,2)(3\,4\,5\,6)$ in $S_6$.
3. Show that $A_n$ is a subgroup of $S_n$ of index 2.
4. Prove that every permutation can be written as a product of transpositions.
5. Show that $S_n$ is non-abelian for $n \geq 3$.
