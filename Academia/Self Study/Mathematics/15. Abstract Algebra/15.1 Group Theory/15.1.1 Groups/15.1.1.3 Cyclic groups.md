## Definition

A group $G$ is **cyclic** if $G = \langle g \rangle = \{g^n : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ for some $g \in G$, called a **generator**.

**Examples**: $(\mathbb{Z}, +) = \langle 1 \rangle$; $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, +) = \langle 1 \rangle$; the $n$-th roots of unity under multiplication.

---

## Classification

Every cyclic group is isomorphic to exactly one of:

$$\mathbb{Z} \quad \text{(infinite cyclic)} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \quad \text{(finite cyclic of order } n\text{)}$$

> [!tip] Cyclic groups are the simplest groups
> Since a cyclic group is fully determined by the order of its generator, everything about it reduces to arithmetic in $\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

---

## Generators of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$

An element $k \in \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ generates the group iff $\gcd(k, n) = 1$. The number of generators is $\varphi(n)$ (Euler's totient function).

**Example**: $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ has generators $1$ and $5$ (since $\gcd(1,6)=\gcd(5,6)=1$), so $\varphi(6) = 2$.

---

## Subgroups of Cyclic Groups

Every subgroup of a cyclic group is cyclic. For $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, there is exactly **one subgroup of each order $d$ dividing $n$**, generated by $n/d$.

---

## Practice Problems

1. List all generators of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Show that every subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ has the form $n\mathbb{Z}$ for some $n \geq 0$.
3. Prove that a cyclic group of order $n$ has exactly one subgroup of each divisor $d$ of $n$.
4. Show that any two cyclic groups of the same order are isomorphic.
5. Is every abelian group cyclic? Give a counterexample if not.
