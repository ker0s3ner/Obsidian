## Definition

The **order** of $g \in G$ is the smallest positive integer $n$ such that $g^n = e$, written $|g| = n$. If no such $n$ exists, $g$ has **infinite order**.

$\langle g \rangle$, the cyclic subgroup generated by $g$, has exactly $|g|$ elements.

---

## Order Divides Group Order

By [[15.1.2 Subgroups & cosets/15.1.2.1 Lagrange theorem]], since $\langle g \rangle \leq G$, the order of every element divides $|G|$:

$$|g| \mid |G|$$

> [!example] Consequence: Fermat's Little Theorem
> In $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times$ for prime $p$, every nonzero element has order dividing $p-1$, so $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$.

---

## Order Under Powers

$$|g^k| = \frac{|g|}{\gcd(|g|, k)}$$

**Example**: if $|g| = 12$, then $|g^8| = 12/\gcd(12,8) = 12/4 = 3$.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Find the order of $5$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}, +)$.
2. Prove $|g^k| = |g|/\gcd(|g|,k)$.
3. Show that if $|g| = n$ and $g^m = e$, then $n \mid m$.
4. In a group of order 15, what are the possible orders of elements?
5. Prove Fermat's Little Theorem using the order-divides-group-order fact.
