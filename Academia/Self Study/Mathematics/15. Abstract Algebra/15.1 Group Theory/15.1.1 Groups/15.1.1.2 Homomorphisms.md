## Definition

A **group homomorphism** is a map $\varphi: G \to H$ between groups satisfying:

$$\varphi(ab) = \varphi(a)\varphi(b) \quad \text{for all } a, b \in G$$

It automatically follows that $\varphi(e_G) = e_H$ and $\varphi(a^{-1}) = \varphi(a)^{-1}$.

---

## Isomorphisms

A homomorphism that is also a bijection is an **isomorphism**; $G \cong H$ means the groups are structurally identical, differing only in labeling.

**Example**: $\log: (\mathbb{R}^+, \times) \to (\mathbb{R}, +)$ is an isomorphism since $\log(xy) = \log x + \log y$.

---

## Kernel and Image

| Object | Definition | Key Fact |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| $\ker \varphi$ | $\{g \in G : \varphi(g) = e_H\}$ | Always a **normal** subgroup of $G$ |
| $\operatorname{im} \varphi$ | $\{\varphi(g) : g \in G\}$ | Always a subgroup of $H$ |

> [!tip] Injectivity test
> $\varphi$ is injective if and only if $\ker \varphi = \{e_G\}$ — a direct consequence of $\varphi(a) = \varphi(b) \iff \varphi(ab^{-1}) = e_H$.

---

## First Isomorphism Theorem (preview)

$$G / \ker\varphi \; \cong \; \operatorname{im}\varphi$$

Every homomorphism factors through its quotient by the kernel — full treatment in [[15.1.3 Normal subgroups/15.1.3.1 Quotient groups]].

## Examples

**Sign homomorphism**: $\text{sgn}: S_n \to \{\pm 1\}$, sending a permutation to $+1$ if even, $-1$ if odd. $\ker(\text{sgn}) = A_n$, the alternating group.

**Determinant**: $\det: GL_n(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathbb{R}^\times$ is a homomorphism with kernel $SL_n(\mathbb{R})$.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $\varphi(e_G) = e_H$ follows from the homomorphism property.
2. Prove that $\ker \varphi$ is always a subgroup of $G$.
3. Show $\text{sgn}: S_n \to \{\pm1\}$ is a homomorphism and identify its kernel.
4. Give an example of a homomorphism that is surjective but not injective.
5. Prove that the composition of two homomorphisms is a homomorphism.
