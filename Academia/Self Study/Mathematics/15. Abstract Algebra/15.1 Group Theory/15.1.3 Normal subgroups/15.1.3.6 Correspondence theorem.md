## Statement

Let $N \trianglelefteq G$. There is a bijection between:

- subgroups $H$ with $N \leq H \leq G$, and
- subgroups $\bar H \leq G/N$

given by $H \mapsto H/N$. This bijection preserves inclusion, index, and normality: $H \trianglelefteq G \iff H/N \trianglelefteq G/N$.

---

## Why This Matters

It says the subgroup lattice of $G/N$ is exactly the "slice" of $G$'s subgroup lattice sitting above $N$ — no information is lost or invented by quotienting.

> [!example] Applying it to $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$
> Subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ correspond exactly to subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$ containing $12\mathbb{Z}$, i.e. $d\mathbb{Z}$ for $d \mid 12$ — matching the divisor lattice of 12.

## Practice Problems

1. Use the correspondence theorem to list all subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Prove that $H \trianglelefteq G \iff H/N \trianglelefteq G/N$ for $N \leq H \leq G$.
3. Show the correspondence preserves index: $|G/N : H/N| = |G:H|$.
4. Apply the theorem to $S_4$ and its normal subgroup $V_4$ (if $V_4 \trianglelefteq S_4$ — check first).
5. Explain why $N$ itself must map to the trivial subgroup under this correspondence.
