## Equivalent Definitions

$N \leq G$ is normal iff any of the following hold:

1. $gNg^{-1} = N$ for all $g \in G$
2. $gNg^{-1} \subseteq N$ for all $g \in G$ (automatically forces equality)
3. Every left coset equals the corresponding right coset: $gN = Ng$
4. $N$ is the kernel of some homomorphism from $G$
5. $N_G(N) = G$ (its normalizer is the whole group)

---

## Useful Sufficient Conditions

- **Index 2**: any subgroup of index 2 is automatically normal.
- **Center**: any subgroup of $Z(G)$ is normal.
- **Kernel**: $\ker\varphi$ is always normal, for any homomorphism $\varphi$.

> [!warning] Normality is not transitive
> $K \trianglelefteq N \trianglelefteq G$ does **not** imply $K \trianglelefteq G$. Classic counterexample lives inside $D_4$.

## Practice Problems

1. Prove that $gNg^{-1} \subseteq N$ for all $g$ forces $gNg^{-1} = N$.
2. Show every subgroup of an abelian group is normal.
3. Find subgroups $K \trianglelefteq N \trianglelefteq G$ with $K$ not normal in $G$.
4. Verify $\langle r \rangle \trianglelefteq D_n$ directly using the conjugation definition.
5. Show that the intersection of two normal subgroups is normal.
