## Second Isomorphism Theorem

If $H \leq G$ and $N \trianglelefteq G$, then $HN \leq G$, $H \cap N \trianglelefteq H$, and:

$$H / (H \cap N) \;\cong\; HN / N$$

---

## Third Isomorphism Theorem

If $K \trianglelefteq G$ and $N \trianglelefteq G$ with $N \leq K$, then $K/N \trianglelefteq G/N$ and:

$$(G/N) \big/ (K/N) \;\cong\; G/K$$

> [!tip] Mnemonic
> The third isomorphism theorem "cancels" $N$ the way fractions cancel: $\dfrac{G/N}{K/N} = \dfrac{G}{K}$.

---

## Example

Take $G = \mathbb{Z}$, $N = 6\mathbb{Z}$, $K = 2\mathbb{Z}$. Then $(\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})/(2\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, matching $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ directly.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the third isomorphism theorem example with $N = 12\mathbb{Z}$, $K = 4\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Prove $HN$ is a subgroup of $G$ when $N \trianglelefteq G$.
3. Show $H \cap N \trianglelefteq H$ in the second isomorphism theorem.
4. Apply the second isomorphism theorem to $H = \langle (1\,2) \rangle$, $N = A_3$ inside $S_3$.
5. Explain the "fraction cancellation" analogy rigorously — why does it actually work?
