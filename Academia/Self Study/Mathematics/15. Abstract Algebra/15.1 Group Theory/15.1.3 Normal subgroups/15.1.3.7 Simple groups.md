## Definition

A group $G$ is **simple** if its only normal subgroups are $\{e\}$ and $G$ itself. Simple groups cannot be "broken down" via quotients.

---

## Examples

- $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ for prime $p$ — simple, since it has no nontrivial subgroups at all.
- $A_n$ for $n \geq 5$ — simple; this is the key fact behind the **unsolvability of the general quintic by radicals**.

> [!warning] $A_4$ is not simple
> $A_4$ has a normal subgroup $V_4 = \{e, (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23)\}$ of order 4 — simplicity of $A_n$ only kicks in at $n \geq 5$.

---

## Role in Structure Theory

Every finite group has a **composition series** with simple quotients (Jordan–Hölder), making simple groups the "atoms" from which all finite groups are built via extensions.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ has no proper nontrivial subgroups.
2. Show $V_4 \trianglelefteq A_4$, so $A_4$ is not simple.
3. Explain the connection between simplicity of $A_5$ and insolvability of the quintic.
4. Is $S_n$ ever simple, for $n \geq 3$? Why or why not?
5. Look up the classification of finite simple groups — what are the main families?
