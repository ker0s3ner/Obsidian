## Statement

For a homomorphism $\varphi: G \to H$:

$$G / \ker\varphi \;\cong\; \operatorname{im}\varphi$$

via the map $gN \mapsto \varphi(g)$, where $N = \ker\varphi$.

---

## Why It's Well-Defined and an Isomorphism

- **Well-defined**: if $gN = g'N$, then $g^{-1}g' \in N = \ker\varphi$, so $\varphi(g) = \varphi(g')$.
- **Injective**: if $\varphi(g) = \varphi(g')$, then $g^{-1}g' \in \ker\varphi$, so $gN = g'N$.
- **Surjective onto $\operatorname{im}\varphi$** and a homomorphism by construction.

---

## Example

$\det: GL_n(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathbb{R}^\times$ has kernel $SL_n(\mathbb{R})$ and image $\mathbb{R}^\times$, so:

$$GL_n(\mathbb{R}) / SL_n(\mathbb{R}) \;\cong\; \mathbb{R}^\times$$

> [!tip] The "universal" factoring picture
> Every homomorphism $\varphi: G \to H$ factors as $G \twoheadrightarrow G/\ker\varphi \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ } \operatorname{im}\varphi \hookrightarrow H$ — surjection, then isomorphism, then inclusion.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $S_n / A_n \cong \{\pm 1\}$ using the first isomorphism theorem.
2. Prove the map $gN \mapsto \varphi(g)$ is well-defined.
3. Use the theorem to identify $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ as a quotient via a suitable homomorphism from $\mathbb{Z}$.
4. Show $(\mathbb{R}, +) / \mathbb{Z} \cong$ the circle group (unit complex numbers under multiplication).
5. Explain why the first isomorphism theorem requires $N$ to be normal.
