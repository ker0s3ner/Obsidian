## Definition

A subgroup $N \leq G$ is **normal**, written $N \trianglelefteq G$, if $gNg^{-1} = N$ for all $g \in G$ — equivalently, left and right cosets coincide: $gN = Ng$.

When $N \trianglelefteq G$, the cosets form a group, the **quotient group**:

$$G/N = \{gN : g \in G\}, \qquad (gN)(hN) = (gh)N$$

---

## Well-Definedness Requires Normality

The product $(gN)(hN) = (gh)N$ only makes sense independent of coset representatives when $N$ is normal — this is exactly why quotients require normal subgroups, not just any subgroup.

> [!example] $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ as a quotient
> $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is literally the quotient group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ in this sense: cosets of $n\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{Z}$, with addition of cosets matching ordinary modular addition.

---

## Order

$$|G/N| = |G:N| = \frac{|G|}{|N|}$$

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $(gN)(hN) = (gh)N$ is well-defined when $N \trianglelefteq G$.
2. Show that $A_n \trianglelefteq S_n$ and describe $S_n / A_n$.
3. Construct $D_4 / \langle r^2 \rangle$ and identify it up to isomorphism.
4. Show that if $G/N$ is abelian, then $N$ contains the commutator subgroup of $G$.
5. Give an example where $gN \neq Ng$ for a non-normal $N$.
