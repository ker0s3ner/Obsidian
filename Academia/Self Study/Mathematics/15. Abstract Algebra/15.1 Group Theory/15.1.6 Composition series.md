## Definition

A **composition series** for $G$ is a chain of subgroups:

$$\{e\} = G_0 \trianglelefteq G_1 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq G_n = G$$

where each **factor** $G_i/G_{i-1}$ is a **simple group**.

---

## Jordan–Hölder Theorem

Every finite group has a composition series, and it's **essentially unique**: any two composition series have the same length and the same composition factors, up to reordering and isomorphism.

> [!tip] Simple groups as "atoms"
> Composition factors play the role that prime factors play for integers — the Jordan–Hölder theorem is the group-theoretic analogue of unique prime factorization.

---

## Example

For $S_4$: $\{e\} \trianglelefteq V_4 \trianglelefteq A_4 \trianglelefteq S_4$, with factors $\mathbb{Z}/2, \mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2 / \mathbb{Z}/2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$... more precisely the refined chain $\{e\} \trianglelefteq \langle(12)(34)\rangle \trianglelefteq V_4 \trianglelefteq A_4 \trianglelefteq S_4$ has all factors $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$.

---

## Solvable Groups

$G$ is **solvable** if all composition factors are abelian (equivalently cyclic of prime order). $S_n$ is solvable for $n \leq 4$ but **not** for $n \geq 5$, since $A_n$ is simple and non-abelian there — the algebraic reason the quintic has no radical solution.

## Practice Problems

1. Find a composition series for $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ and identify its factors.
2. Show that $S_4$ is solvable by exhibiting its composition series.
3. Explain why $A_5$ being simple and non-abelian makes $S_5$ not solvable.
4. State the Jordan–Hölder theorem precisely and explain the uniqueness claim.
5. Find two different composition series for $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ and verify they have the same factors.
