## Definition

The **free group** $F(S)$ on a set $S$ consists of all finite reduced words in $S \cup S^{-1}$ (no adjacent $s s^{-1}$ or $s^{-1}s$), under concatenation with reduction. It satisfies no relations beyond the group axioms.

**Universal property**: any function $S \to G$ (for a group $G$) extends uniquely to a homomorphism $F(S) \to G$.

---

## Presentations

Every group $G$ is a quotient of a free group: $G \cong F(S)/N$ for some normal subgroup $N$ generated by a set of **relations** $R$. This is written:

$$G = \langle S \mid R \rangle$$

**Example**: $D_n = \langle r, s \mid r^n, s^2, srsr \rangle$ (i.e. $r^n = e$, $s^2 = e$, $srs = r^{-1}$).

> [!tip] Free groups are "presentation-free"
> $F(S)$ itself has the presentation $\langle S \mid \, \rangle$ — no relations at all, which is exactly why it's called free.

---

## Rank and Uniqueness

$F(S)$ depends only on $|S|$ up to isomorphism; the **rank** is $|S|$. Free groups of different finite rank are never isomorphic to each other, and $F(1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

## Practice Problems

1. Write a presentation for $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ using one generator and one relation.
2. Show $F(1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.
3. State the universal property of free groups precisely and explain why it determines $F(S)$ uniquely.
4. Give a presentation for $S_3$ using generators and relations.
5. Explain why every group can be written as a quotient of some free group.
