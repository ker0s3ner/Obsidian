## Orbit and Stabilizer

For a group $G$ acting on a set $X$ and $x \in X$:

$$\text{Orb}(x) = \{g \cdot x : g \in G\}, \qquad \text{Stab}(x) = \{g \in G : g \cdot x = x\}$$

$\text{Stab}(x)$ is always a subgroup of $G$.

---

## Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem

$$|G| = |\text{Orb}(x)| \cdot |\text{Stab}(x)|$$

Equivalently, $|\text{Orb}(x)| = |G : \text{Stab}(x)|$ — orbit size equals the index of the stabilizer.

**Proof idea**: the map $g\,\text{Stab}(x) \mapsto g\cdot x$ is a well-defined bijection from cosets of $\text{Stab}(x)$ to $\text{Orb}(x)$.

---

## Example

$G = S_4$ acts on $X = \{1,2,3,4\}$ naturally. $\text{Orb}(1) = X$ (the action is transitive), and $\text{Stab}(1) \cong S_3$ (permutations fixing 1), so $|S_4| = 4 \cdot |S_3| = 4 \cdot 6 = 24$. ✓

> [!tip] Counting via orbit-stabilizer
> This theorem is the workhorse for counting symmetric configurations — e.g. the number of distinct colorings of a cube's faces up to rotation.

## Practice Problems

1. Let $D_4$ act on the vertices of a square. Find $\text{Orb}$ and $\text{Stab}$ of one vertex.
2. Prove that $\text{Stab}(x)$ is always a subgroup.
3. Use orbit-stabilizer to find $|G|$ given $|\text{Orb}(x)| = 6$ and $|\text{Stab}(x)| = 4$.
4. Show that two elements in the same orbit have conjugate stabilizers.
5. Apply orbit-stabilizer to conjugation: relate conjugacy classes to centralizers.
