## Definition

An action of $G$ on $X$ is **transitive** if $X$ has a single orbit — for any $x, y \in X$, there exists $g \in G$ with $g \cdot x = y$.

---

## Transitive Actions as Coset Spaces

Every transitive action of $G$ on $X$ is isomorphic (as a $G$-set) to $G$ acting on $G/H$ by left multiplication, where $H = \text{Stab}(x)$ for any fixed $x \in X$.

$$X \cong G/\text{Stab}(x)$$

This is orbit-stabilizer again, but read as a structural classification: **every transitive $G$-set is a coset space**.

> [!example] $S_n$ on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$
> This action is transitive, and $\text{Stab}(1) \cong S_{n-1}$, matching $X \cong S_n / S_{n-1}$, a set of size $n$.

---

## $k$-Transitivity

An action is **$k$-transitive** if $G$ can map any ordered $k$-tuple of distinct points to any other. $S_n$ is $n$-transitive on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$; $A_n$ is $(n-2)$-transitive.

## Practice Problems

1. Show that $\text{Stab}(x)$ and $\text{Stab}(y)$ are conjugate when the action is transitive.
2. Verify $S_4$ acts transitively on $\{1,2,3,4\}$ and identify the stabilizer of a point.
3. Show every transitive $G$-set is isomorphic to some coset space $G/H$.
4. Is the action of $D_4$ on the 4 vertices of a square transitive? On the 2 diagonals?
5. Show $2$-transitivity implies transitivity, but not conversely.
