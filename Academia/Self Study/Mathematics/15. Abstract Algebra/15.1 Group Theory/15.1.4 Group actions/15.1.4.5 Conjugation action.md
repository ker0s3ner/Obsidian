## The Action

$G$ acts on itself by $g \cdot x = gxg^{-1}$. This is a genuine group action (checked directly from associativity), and it's the source of conjugacy classes, centralizers, and — acting on the set of subgroups instead — normalizers.

---

## Conjugation on Subgroups

$G$ also acts on its set of subgroups by $g \cdot H = gHg^{-1}$. The stabilizer of $H$ under this action is exactly $N_G(H)$, its normalizer, so by orbit-stabilizer:

$$\#\{\text{conjugates of } H\} = |G : N_G(H)|$$

> [!tip] This is exactly how Sylow's theorems count subgroups
> The number of Sylow $p$-subgroups is the size of a single conjugacy orbit of subgroups, computed via $|G : N_G(P)|$.

---

## Fixed Points Are the Center

$x$ is fixed by every conjugation iff $gxg^{-1} = x$ for all $g$, i.e. $x \in Z(G)$ — the center is exactly the fixed-point set of the conjugation action on $G$ itself.

## Practice Problems

1. Show conjugation on $G$ is a valid group action.
2. Prove $\text{Stab}(H)$ under conjugation-on-subgroups equals $N_G(H)$.
3. Find all conjugates of $\langle (1\,2) \rangle$ in $S_3$.
4. Show that fixed points of the conjugation action on $G$ are exactly $Z(G)$.
5. Use conjugation to show all Sylow $p$-subgroups of a group are conjugate (statement only, no need to prove Sylow's theorem itself).
