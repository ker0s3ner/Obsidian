## Definition

A **group action** of $G$ on a set $X$ is a map $G \times X \to X$, $(g,x) \mapsto g\cdot x$, satisfying:

1. $e \cdot x = x$ for all $x$
2. $(gh)\cdot x = g \cdot (h \cdot x)$ for all $g,h \in G$, $x \in X$

Equivalently, an action is a homomorphism $G \to \text{Sym}(X)$ (each $g$ acts as a permutation of $X$).

---

## Types of Actions

| Property | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **Transitive** | single orbit: $\text{Orb}(x) = X$ for any $x$ |
| **Faithful** | the homomorphism $G \to \text{Sym}(X)$ is injective |
| **Free** | $\text{Stab}(x) = \{e\}$ for all $x$ |

> [!example] Cayley's theorem as an action
> $G$ acting on itself by left multiplication is always faithful — this is exactly Cayley's theorem in the language of actions.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify the two axioms hold for $S_n$ acting on $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ by permutation.
2. Show that an action is equivalent to a homomorphism $G \to \text{Sym}(X)$.
3. Give an example of a transitive but not free action.
4. Give an example of a faithful action that is not transitive.
5. Show that the kernel of the associated homomorphism is $\bigcap_x \text{Stab}(x)$.
