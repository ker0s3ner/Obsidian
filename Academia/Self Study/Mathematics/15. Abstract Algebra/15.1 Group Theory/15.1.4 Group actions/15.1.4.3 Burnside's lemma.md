## Statement

For a finite group $G$ acting on a finite set $X$, the number of **orbits** is:

$$|X/G| = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} |X^g|$$

where $X^g = \{x \in X : g \cdot x = x\}$ is the set of points fixed by $g$.

---

## Interpretation

Average the number of fixed points over all group elements — this average always equals the number of orbits, a surprisingly clean counting identity.

**Example — colorings of a square's 4 corners with 2 colors under $D_4$:**

| $g$ | fixed colorings |
|-----|------------------|
| $e$ | $2^4 = 16$ |
| rotations by 90°, 270° | $2$ each |
| rotation by 180° | $2^2 = 4$ |
| 2 reflections through vertices | $2^3 = 8$ each |
| 2 reflections through edges | $2^2 = 4$ each |

$$\frac{16 + 2 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 8 + 4 + 4}{8} = \frac{48}{8} = 6 \text{ distinct colorings}$$

> [!tip] Also called the "orbit-counting theorem"
> Burnside's name is attached historically, but the result was known earlier to Cauchy and Frobenius.

## Practice Problems

1. Use Burnside's lemma to count distinct 3-colorings of a triangle's vertices up to rotation ($C_3$ acting).
2. Verify the corner-coloring computation above by listing all 6 colorings explicitly.
3. Count necklaces of 4 beads in 2 colors, up to rotation (cyclic group $C_4$).
4. Prove Burnside's lemma using the orbit-stabilizer theorem and double counting.
5. How does the count change if reflections are also allowed (full $D_4$ vs. just rotations $C_4$)?
