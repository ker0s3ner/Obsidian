## Definition

A **permutation representation** of $G$ is a homomorphism $\rho: G \to \text{Sym}(X)$ for some set $X$ — exactly the data of a group action, viewed as an assignment of permutations to group elements.

---

## Degree and Faithfulness

The **degree** of the representation is $|X|$. The representation is **faithful** iff $\ker\rho = \{e\}$, i.e. the action has trivial kernel $\bigcap_x \text{Stab}(x)$.

> [!example] Regular representation
> $G$ acting on itself by left multiplication gives the **regular representation**, always faithful, of degree $|G|$ — the representation underlying Cayley's theorem.

---

## Decomposing Representations

If $X$ splits into orbits $X = X_1 \sqcup \cdots \sqcup X_k$, then $\rho$ decomposes as a direct combination of the transitive representations on each $X_i \cong G/\text{Stab}(x_i)$.

## Practice Problems

1. Describe the permutation representation of $D_4$ on the vertices of a square; find its degree and kernel.
2. Show that the regular representation is always faithful.
3. Find the smallest faithful permutation representation of $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ (degree less than 6, if possible).
4. Decompose the action of $S_3$ on $\{1,2,3\} \times \{1,2,3\}$ (by acting on both coordinates) into orbits.
5. Explain why every finite group has a faithful permutation representation of degree $|G|$.
