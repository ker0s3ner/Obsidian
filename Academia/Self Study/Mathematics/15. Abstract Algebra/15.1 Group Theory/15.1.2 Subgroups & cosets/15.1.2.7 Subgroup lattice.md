## Definition

The **subgroup lattice** of $G$ is the partially ordered set of all subgroups of $G$, ordered by inclusion, with $\{e\}$ at the bottom and $G$ at the top.

---

## Lattice Operations

For subgroups $H, K \leq G$:

- **Meet** $H \wedge K = H \cap K$ — always a subgroup
- **Join** $H \vee K = \langle H \cup K \rangle$ — the smallest subgroup containing both

> [!warning] $H \cup K$ is usually not a subgroup
> Unless one contains the other, $H \cup K$ fails closure. The join must be generated, not simply unioned.

---

## Example — $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$

Subgroups correspond exactly to divisors of 12: orders $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$, forming a lattice isomorphic to the divisor lattice of 12.

---

## Correspondence with Quotients

For $N \trianglelefteq G$, subgroups of $G/N$ correspond exactly to subgroups of $G$ containing $N$ — this is the **correspondence theorem**, refining the subgroup lattice picture.

## Practice Problems

1. Draw the subgroup lattice of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Show $H \cap K$ is always a subgroup, for any $H, K \leq G$.
3. Find $\langle H \cup K \rangle$ for $H = \langle (1\,2) \rangle$, $K = \langle (1\,3) \rangle$ in $S_3$.
4. Draw the subgroup lattice of $D_4$.
5. Explain why the subgroup lattice of a cyclic group of order $n$ mirrors the divisor lattice of $n$.
