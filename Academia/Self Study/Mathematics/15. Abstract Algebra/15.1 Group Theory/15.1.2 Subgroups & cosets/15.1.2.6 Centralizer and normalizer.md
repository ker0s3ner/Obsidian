## Centralizer

For $S \subseteq G$, the **centralizer** is:

$$C_G(S) = \{g \in G : gs = sg \ \text{for all } s \in S\}$$

Always a subgroup. When $S = G$, $C_G(G) = Z(G)$, the **center** of $G$.

---

## Normalizer

$$N_G(S) = \{g \in G : gSg^{-1} = S\}$$

$N_G(S)$ is the largest subgroup of $G$ in which $S$ (as a set) is normal. Always $C_G(S) \leq N_G(S)$.

---

## Key Fact — for a subgroup $H$

$H \trianglelefteq N_G(H)$, and $N_G(H)$ is the largest subgroup of $G$ in which $H$ is normal. If $H \trianglelefteq G$, then $N_G(H) = G$.

> [!example] Conjugation and normalizers
> The number of distinct conjugates of $H$ in $G$ equals $|G : N_G(H)|$ — a special case of the orbit-stabilizer theorem applied to the conjugation action.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $Z(D_4)$.
2. Show that $C_G(S)$ is always a subgroup of $G$.
3. Prove $H \trianglelefteq N_G(H)$ for any subgroup $H$.
4. Find $N_{S_4}(\langle (1\,2) \rangle)$.
5. Show that if $H \leq Z(G)$, then $H$ is normal in $G$.
