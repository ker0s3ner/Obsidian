## Generated Subgroups

For $S \subseteq G$, the **subgroup generated by $S$**, denoted $\langle S \rangle$, is the smallest subgroup containing $S$ — equivalently, the set of all finite products of elements of $S$ and their inverses.

$$\langle S \rangle = \bigcap_{S \subseteq H \leq G} H$$

---

## Cyclic Subgroups

For a single element, $\langle g \rangle = \{g^n : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ — always abelian, isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ (see [[15.1.1 Groups/15.1.1.3 Cyclic groups]]).

---

## Generating Sets

A group may need more than one generator. $G = \langle S \rangle$ means every element of $G$ is a finite word in $S \cup S^{-1}$.

**Example**: $S_n$ is generated by the transpositions $\{(1\,2), (1\,3), \ldots, (1\,n)\}$, or even more economically by $\{(1\,2), (1\,2\,\ldots\,n)\}$.

> [!example] Minimal generating sets
> $D_n = \langle r, s \rangle$ needs exactly two generators — a rotation and a reflection — despite having $2n$ elements.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $\langle S \rangle$ equals the set of all finite products of elements of $S \cup S^{-1}$.
2. Verify that $S_4$ is generated by $(1\,2)$ and $(1\,2\,3\,4)$.
3. Find the subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ generated by $\{6, 10\}$.
4. Prove that every subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ is generated by a single element.
5. What is $\langle (1\,2\,3), (1\,2) \rangle$ inside $S_3$?
