## Definition

The **index** of $H \leq G$, written $|G:H|$, is the number of left cosets of $H$ in $G$. For finite $G$:

$$|G:H| = \frac{|G|}{|H|}$$

The index can be finite even when $G$ is infinite: e.g. $|\mathbb{Z} : n\mathbb{Z}| = n$.

---

## Multiplicativity

For subgroups $K \leq H \leq G$:

$$|G:K| = |G:H| \cdot |H:K|$$

**Example**: if $H \leq G$ has index 2, $H$ is automatically normal, since the two cosets $H$ and $G \setminus H$ must coincide from the left and right.

> [!tip] Index-2 subgroups are always normal
> With only two cosets, $gH$ is either $H$ (when $g \in H$) or $G \setminus H$ (when $g \notin H$) — and the same holds for $Hg$, so $gH = Hg$ always.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute $|S_4 : A_4|$.
2. Prove the multiplicativity formula $|G:K| = |G:H|\cdot|H:K|$.
3. Show any subgroup of index 2 is normal.
4. Find $|\mathbb{Z} : 5\mathbb{Z}|$ and list its cosets.
5. If $|G| = 30$ and $|G:H| = 5$, what is $|H|$?
