## Statement

**Lagrange's Theorem**: if $H$ is a subgroup of a finite group $G$, then $|H|$ divides $|G|$:

$$|G| = |G : H| \cdot |H|$$

where $|G:H|$ is the **index** of $H$ — the number of cosets of $H$ in $G$.

---

## Proof Sketch

The left cosets $\{gH : g \in G\}$ partition $G$ into disjoint sets, each of size $|H|$ (since $x \mapsto gx$ is a bijection $H \to gH$). Counting elements gives $|G| = |G:H| \cdot |H|$.

---

## Consequences

- The order of every element divides $|G|$ (apply to $H = \langle g \rangle$).
- Any group of **prime order** $p$ is cyclic — it has no nontrivial proper subgroups, so any non-identity element generates the whole group.

> [!warning] The converse fails
> A divisor $d$ of $|G|$ need not correspond to an actual subgroup of order $d$. Example: $A_4$ has order 12 but no subgroup of order 6.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Use Lagrange's theorem to show a group of order 7 is cyclic.
2. Prove that $A_4$ (order 12) has no subgroup of order 6.
3. Show that the order of an element divides the order of the group.
4. If $|G| = 35$, what are the possible orders of subgroups?
5. Prove that every group of prime order is abelian.
