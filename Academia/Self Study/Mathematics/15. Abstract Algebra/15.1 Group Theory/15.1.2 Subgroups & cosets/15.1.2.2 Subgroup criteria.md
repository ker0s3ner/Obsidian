## Definition

A subset $H \subseteq G$ is a **subgroup**, written $H \leq G$, if $H$ is itself a group under $G$'s operation: closed under the operation and inverses, containing the identity.

---

## The One-Step Subgroup Test

$H \subseteq G$ nonempty is a subgroup iff:

$$ab^{-1} \in H \quad \text{for all } a, b \in H$$

This single condition packages closure, identity, and inverses together.

For **finite** $H$, closure under the operation alone suffices (no need to separately check inverses).

---

## Examples

- $n\mathbb{Z} \leq \mathbb{Z}$ for any integer $n$.
- $SL_n(\mathbb{R}) \leq GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ (matrices of determinant 1).
- The center $Z(G) = \{z \in G : zg = gz \ \forall g \in G\}$ is always a subgroup.

> [!example] Checking $SL_n(\mathbb{R})$
> If $\det A = \det B = 1$, then $\det(AB^{-1}) = \det A \cdot \det(B)^{-1} = 1$, so $SL_n(\mathbb{R})$ passes the one-step test.

## Practice Problems

1. Use the one-step test to verify $n\mathbb{Z}$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$.
2. Show that the center $Z(G)$ is always a subgroup.
3. Prove the finite subgroup test: closure alone suffices when $H$ is finite.
4. Is the set of odd integers a subgroup of $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$? Explain.
5. Show that the intersection of two subgroups is a subgroup.
