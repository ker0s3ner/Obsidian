## Definition

A sequence of modules and homomorphisms:

$$\cdots \to M_{i-1} \xrightarrow{f_{i-1}} M_i \xrightarrow{f_i} M_{i+1} \to \cdots$$

is **exact at $M_i$** if $\operatorname{im} f_{i-1} = \ker f_i$. **Exact everywhere** means this holds at every position.

---

## Short Exact Sequences

$$0 \to A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$$

is exact iff $f$ is injective, $g$ is surjective, and $\operatorname{im} f = \ker g$. Then $C \cong B/A$ (identifying $A$ with $\operatorname{im} f$).

> [!example] Splitting
> If a short exact sequence **splits** ($B \cong A \oplus C$), then $\operatorname{Hom}(-,C)$ and similar functors behave well; splitting always happens when $C$ is projective or $A$ is injective.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times n} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \to 0$ is exact.
2. Prove that exactness at $M_i$ means $\operatorname{im} f_{i-1} = \ker f_i$ captures "no information lost."
3. Show the sequence in problem 1 does not split.
4. Give an example of a split short exact sequence and verify $B \cong A \oplus C$.
5. Show that if $P$ is projective, every short exact sequence ending in $P$ splits.
