## Statement

Every finitely generated module over a PID $R$ decomposes as:

$$M \cong R^r \oplus R/(d_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus R/(d_k)$$

where $r \geq 0$ is the **rank** (free part) and $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$ are non-unit, non-zero elements of $R$ (the **invariant factors**).

---

## Special Case: $R = \mathbb{Z}$

Every finitely generated abelian group is:

$$\mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_k\mathbb{Z}, \qquad d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k$$

This subsumes the classification of finite abelian groups from [[15.1 Group Theory/15.1.1 Groups/15.1.1.6 Direct products]] as the case $r=0$.

> [!tip] Two equivalent forms
> The **invariant factor** decomposition (divisibility chain) and the **primary** decomposition (prime-power cyclic factors) describe the same group differently — convert between them via the Chinese Remainder Theorem.

## Practice Problems

1. Classify all finitely generated abelian groups of rank 0 and order 360.
2. Convert the invariant-factor form $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ to primary form.
3. State the structure theorem for $R = k[x]$ (a PID) and relate it to Jordan normal form.
4. Show $\mathbb{Z}^2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is not isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.
5. Why does the theorem require $R$ to be a PID?
