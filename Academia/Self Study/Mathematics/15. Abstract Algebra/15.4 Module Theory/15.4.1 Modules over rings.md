## Definition

A (left) **$R$-module** $M$ over a ring $R$ is an abelian group with a scalar action $R \times M \to M$ satisfying, for $r,s\in R$, $m,n\in M$:

$$r(m+n)=rm+rn, \quad (r+s)m=rm+sm, \quad (rs)m=r(sm), \quad 1m=m$$

A module is exactly a vector space, except the scalars come from a ring rather than a field.

---

## Examples

| $R$ | $R$-module example |
|-----|----------------------|
| Field $F$ | any $F$-vector space |
| $\mathbb{Z}$ | any abelian group (unique way!) |
| $R$ itself | $R$ acting on itself by multiplication |
| $R = k[x]$ | vector space with a linear operator (multiplication by $x$) |

> [!tip] Modules over $\mathbb{Z}$ = abelian groups
> Every abelian group is automatically a $\mathbb{Z}$-module in exactly one way ($n\cdot m$ means adding $m$ to itself $n$ times) — module theory generalizes group theory here, not just linear algebra.

---

## Submodules

$N \subseteq M$ is a **submodule** if it's a subgroup closed under scalar multiplication — the module analogue of a subspace.

---

## Practice Problems

1. Verify every abelian group is a $\mathbb{Z}$-module.
2. Show that an $R$-module where $R$ is a field is exactly a vector space.
3. Find all submodules of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-module.
4. Show $R$ acting on itself (as an $R$-module) has submodules exactly the ideals of $R$.
5. Is $\mathbb{Q}$ a $\mathbb{Z}$-module? Is it finitely generated?
