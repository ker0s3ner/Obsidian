## Local Rings

A ring is **local** if it has a unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$. Modules over local rings behave especially well — many results from linear algebra extend cleanly.

---

## Nakayama's Lemma

If $M$ is a finitely generated module over a local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$ and $M = \mathfrak m M$, then $M = 0$.

> [!tip] Why this matters
> Nakayama's lemma is the tool for lifting statements about $M/\mathfrak m M$ (a vector space over the residue field $R/\mathfrak m$) back up to statements about $M$ itself — e.g. minimal generating sets correspond to bases of $M/\mathfrak m M$.

---

## Free Modules over Local Rings

A finitely generated **projective** module over a local ring is automatically **free** — projectivity and freeness coincide locally, even though they can differ globally.

## Practice Problems

1. State and prove Nakayama's lemma (may cite the determinant-trick proof).
2. Show $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ (integers localized at $p$) is a local ring and identify its maximal ideal.
3. Show a f.g. projective module over a local ring is free.
4. Give an example of a projective module over a non-local ring that is not free.
5. Use Nakayama's lemma to show a surjective endomorphism of a finitely generated module is an isomorphism.
