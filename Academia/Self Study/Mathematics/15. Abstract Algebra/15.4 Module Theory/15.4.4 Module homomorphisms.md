## Definition

An $R$-module homomorphism $\varphi: M \to N$ satisfies $\varphi(m+m')=\varphi(m)+\varphi(m')$ and $\varphi(rm) = r\varphi(m)$ — additive and $R$-linear.

$\text{Hom}_R(M,N)$, the set of all such maps, is itself an $R$-module (when $R$ is commutative).

---

## Kernel, Image, Isomorphism Theorems

Exactly as for groups and rings: $\ker\varphi$ and $\operatorname{im}\varphi$ are submodules, and:

$$M/\ker\varphi \;\cong\; \operatorname{im}\varphi$$

> [!tip] Same theorems, different objects
> The isomorphism theorems (first, second, third) hold verbatim for modules, replacing "normal subgroup"/"ideal" with "submodule" — a genuinely universal pattern across algebra.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $\text{Hom}_R(M,N)$ is an $R$-module when $R$ is commutative.
2. Verify the first isomorphism theorem for the map $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ viewed as $\mathbb{Z}$-modules.
3. Find $\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})$ for various $n,m$.
4. Show $\ker\varphi$ is always a submodule.
5. Compute $\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}, M)$ for any $\mathbb{Z}$-module $M$.
