## Definition

For $R$-modules $M, N$, the **tensor product** $M \otimes_R N$ is generated by symbols $m\otimes n$, subject to bilinearity:

$$(m+m')\otimes n = m\otimes n + m'\otimes n, \quad m\otimes(n+n') = m\otimes n + m\otimes n', \quad rm\otimes n = m \otimes rn = r(m\otimes n)$$

---

## Universal Property

Bilinear maps $M \times N \to P$ correspond exactly to linear maps $M \otimes_R N \to P$ — tensor product "linearizes" bilinear data.

---

## Base Change / Extension of Scalars

$M \otimes_R S$ turns an $R$-module into an $S$-module, for a ring map $R \to S$.

**Example**: $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} = 0$, since every element is killed by $n$ but $n$ is invertible in $\mathbb{Q}$.

> [!warning] Tensor is not always "exact"
> $M \otimes_R -$ preserves surjections but not necessarily injections — the failure of exactness on the left is exactly what the **Tor** functor measures (see [[15.6 Homological Algebra/15.6.3 Ext and Tor]]).

## Practice Problems

1. Show $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q} = 0$.
2. Compute $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.
3. State the universal property of the tensor product precisely.
4. Show $R \otimes_R M \cong M$.
5. Give an example where $0 \to A \to B$ is exact but $0 \to A\otimes M \to B \otimes M$ is not.
