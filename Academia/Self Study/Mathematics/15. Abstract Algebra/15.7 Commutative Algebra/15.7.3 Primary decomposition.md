## Primary Ideals

$Q$ is **primary** if $ab \in Q$ implies $a \in Q$ or $b^n \in Q$ for some $n$ — a relaxation of "prime" that allows nilpotent obstruction. $\sqrt Q$ is always prime.

---

## Primary Decomposition Theorem

In a Noetherian ring, every ideal $I$ can be written as a finite intersection:

$$I = Q_1 \cap \cdots \cap Q_n$$

of primary ideals — the ring-theoretic analogue of factoring an integer into prime powers, but for ideals.

> [!example] $\mathbb{Z}$ analogy
> $(12) = (4) \cap (3)$ where $(4)$ is primary with radical $(2)$ and $(3)$ is prime (hence primary) — matching $12 = 2^2\cdot 3$.

---

## Associated Primes

The radicals $\sqrt{Q_i}$ appearing in a (minimal) primary decomposition are the **associated primes** of $I$ — these are well-defined even though the $Q_i$ themselves aren't always unique.

## Practice Problems

1. Find a primary decomposition of $(12)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ and verify it.
2. Show $\sqrt Q$ is always prime for a primary ideal $Q$.
3. Find the primary decomposition of $(x^2, xy)$ in $k[x,y]$.
4. Explain the geometric meaning of associated primes for an ideal defining a variety.
5. Show every prime ideal is primary.
