## Recap and Extension

Localization $T^{-1}R$ (introduced in [[15.2 Ring Theory/15.2.1 Rings/15.2.1.7 Localization]]) is a fundamental tool for studying rings "locally" — near a prime ideal.

---

## Local Properties

A property $P$ of a module or ring is **local** if it holds globally exactly when it holds after localizing at every prime (equivalently, every maximal ideal).

**Example**: "flat" is local — $M$ is a flat $R$-module iff $M_P$ is flat over $R_P$ for every prime $P$.

> [!tip] Why this is powerful
> Local rings (from localizing at a prime) are much simpler to analyze — unique maximal ideal, often regular — so reducing a global question to local ones is a standard strategy throughout commutative algebra and algebraic geometry.

---

## Localization Is Exact

The localization functor $T^{-1}(-)$ is **exact** — it preserves all exact sequences, unlike most functors (compare $\otimes$, which is only right-exact in general).

## Practice Problems

1. Show localization preserves exact sequences.
2. Explain what it means for "being zero" to be a local property.
3. Show $M = 0$ iff $M_P = 0$ for every maximal ideal $P$.
4. Localize $k[x,y]$ at the prime $(x)$ and describe elements of the result.
5. Give an example of a property that is NOT local.
