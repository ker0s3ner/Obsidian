## Definition

$R$ is **Noetherian** if every ideal is finitely generated — equivalently, every ascending chain of ideals stabilizes (ACC: ascending chain condition).

---

## Hilbert's Basis Theorem

If $R$ is Noetherian, so is $R[x]$. By induction, $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is Noetherian for any field $k$ — the single most important source of Noetherian rings in algebraic geometry.

> [!tip] Why Noetherian matters
> Finitely generated ideals mean induction/finiteness arguments work throughout commutative algebra — without ACC, basic tools like primary decomposition and dimension theory break down.

---

## Equivalent Formulations

1. Every ideal is finitely generated
2. ACC on ideals
3. Every nonempty collection of ideals has a maximal element

## Practice Problems

1. Show ACC implies every ideal is finitely generated.
2. Verify $\mathbb{Z}$ is Noetherian directly.
3. Show a quotient of a Noetherian ring is Noetherian.
4. State Hilbert's basis theorem and its significance for algebraic geometry.
5. Give an example of a non-Noetherian ring (e.g. polynomials in infinitely many variables).
