## Definition

A **graded ring** $R = \bigoplus_{n\geq0} R_n$ decomposes as a direct sum of abelian groups with $R_m \cdot R_n \subseteq R_{m+n}$. A **graded module** decomposes compatibly.

**Example**: $k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ graded by total degree — the coordinate ring underlying projective space.

---

## Homogeneous Ideals

An ideal $I$ is **homogeneous** if it's generated by homogeneous elements; then $R/I$ inherits a grading. Homogeneous ideals correspond exactly to **projective varieties** (zero sets in $\mathbb{P}^n$), connecting back to [[4. Geometry/4.13 Algebraic Geometry (Classical)/4.13.4 Projective geometry]].

> [!tip] Hilbert function
> For a graded module $M = \bigoplus M_n$, the **Hilbert function** $n \mapsto \dim M_n$ eventually agrees with a polynomial (the Hilbert polynomial) — encoding dimension and degree of the associated projective variety.

## Practice Problems

1. Verify $k[x,y]$ is graded by total degree and check $R_1 \cdot R_2 \subseteq R_3$.
2. Show $(x^2, xy)$ is a homogeneous ideal in $k[x,y]$.
3. Compute the Hilbert function of $k[x,y]/(x^2)$.
4. Explain why homogeneous ideals correspond to projective (not affine) varieties.
5. Is $(x^2 + y)$ a homogeneous ideal in $k[x,y]$? Why or why not?
