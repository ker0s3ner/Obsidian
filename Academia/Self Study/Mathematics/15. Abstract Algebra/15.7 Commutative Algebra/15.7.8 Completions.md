## Definition

For an ideal $I \trianglelefteq R$, the **$I$-adic completion** is:

$$\hat R = \varprojlim_n R/I^n$$

elements are compatible sequences of residues mod $I, I^2, I^3, \ldots$ — a formal-power-series-style limit.

---

## Example — $p$-adic Integers

Completing $\mathbb{Z}$ at $I=(p)$ gives $\mathbb{Z}_p$, the **$p$-adic integers** — the foundational example, expanded on in [[17. Number Theory/17.4 p-adic Analysis]].

## Example — Formal Power Series

Completing $k[x]$ at $(x)$ gives $k[[x]]$, formal power series — turning polynomial approximation into literal convergence in a new topology.

> [!tip] Completions "add limits"
> Just as $\mathbb{R}$ completes $\mathbb{Q}$ with respect to the usual metric, $\hat R$ completes $R$ with respect to the $I$-adic metric — same idea, different notion of "close."

## Practice Problems

1. Describe an element of $\mathbb{Z}_5$ as a compatible sequence mod $5, 25, 125,\ldots$
2. Show $k[[x]]$ is the completion of $k[x]$ at $(x)$.
3. Explain the analogy between $\mathbb{R}$ completing $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_p$ completing $\mathbb{Z}$.
4. Is $\mathbb{Z}_p$ an integral domain?
5. Show that $1+x+x^2+\cdots$ makes sense in $k[[x]]$ but not in $k[x]$.
