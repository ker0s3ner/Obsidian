## Definition

The **Krull dimension** of $R$ is the supremum of lengths $n$ of chains of prime ideals:

$$P_0 \subsetneq P_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq P_n$$

**Examples**: $\dim(\text{field}) = 0$; $\dim \mathbb{Z} = 1$; $\dim k[x_1,\ldots,x_n] = n$.

---

## Geometric Meaning

For $R = k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I$ (coordinate ring of a variety), Krull dimension matches the geometric notion of dimension — a point has dimension 0, a curve has dimension 1, etc.

> [!tip] Why chains of primes encode dimension
> Each strict inclusion $P_i \subsetneq P_{i+1}$ corresponds to passing to a smaller, more special subvariety — a chain of primes is literally a chain of nested subvarieties.

---

## Height and Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem

The **height** of a prime $P$ is the length of the longest chain of primes below it. Krull's theorem: in a Noetherian ring, an ideal generated by $n$ elements has height at most $n$.
