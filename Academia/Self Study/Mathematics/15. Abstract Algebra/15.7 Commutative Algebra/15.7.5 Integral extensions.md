## Definition

$\alpha$ is **integral** over $R$ if it satisfies a **monic** polynomial with coefficients in $R$. The set of all elements integral over $R$ (in some extension) is the **integral closure**.

$R$ is **integrally closed** if it equals its own integral closure inside its fraction field.

---

## Analogy with Algebraic Extensions

"Integral" over a ring plays the role "algebraic" plays over a field, with the monic condition replacing arbitrary polynomials — this is exactly why $\mathbb{Z}$ (integrally closed in $\mathbb{Q}$) contrasts with rings like $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt5]$ which may fail to be integrally closed.

> [!example] The ring of integers
> The **ring of integers** $\mathcal{O}_K$ of a number field $K$ is defined as exactly the elements of $K$ integral over $\mathbb{Z}$ — the central object of algebraic number theory.

---

## Lying Over and Going Up

If $S/R$ is an integral extension, primes of $R$ "lift" to primes of $S$ (lying over), and chains of primes in $R$ extend to chains in $S$ (going up) — the algebraic engine behind dimension theory for integral extensions.

## Practice Problems

1. Show $\sqrt2$ is integral over $\mathbb{Z}$ by exhibiting a monic polynomial.
2. Show $\mathbb{Z}$ is integrally closed in $\mathbb{Q}$.
3. Find the ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$ (careful: it's not just $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt5]$).
4. Show that if $S/R$ is integral and $S$ is a field, then $R$ is a field.
5. State the "lying over" theorem informally.
