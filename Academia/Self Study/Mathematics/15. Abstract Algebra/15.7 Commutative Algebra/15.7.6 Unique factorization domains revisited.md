## Recap

A **UFD** is an integral domain where every nonzero non-unit factors uniquely (up to order and units) into irreducibles. Every PID is a UFD; the converse fails.

---

## Regular Local Rings

A **regular local ring** (dimension $n$ local ring whose maximal ideal is generated by exactly $n$ elements) is always a UFD — the algebraic incarnation of "smoothness."

> [!example] $k[x,y]$ vs. a singular ring
> $k[x,y]_{(x,y)}$ is regular, hence a UFD; but $k[x,y]/(y^2-x^3)$ localized at the singular point (origin, a cusp) is **not** a UFD — factorization breaks down exactly at the singularity.

---

## Krull's Theorem on UFDs

$R$ is a UFD iff every height-1 prime ideal is principal — a dimension-theoretic reformulation connecting back to [[15.7.4 Krull dimension]].
