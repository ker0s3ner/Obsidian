## Polar Coordinates

A point is described by $(r, \theta)$: distance $r$ from the origin and angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis.

$$x = r\cos\theta, \qquad y = r\sin\theta$$

$$r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}, \qquad \theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$$

---

## Common Polar Curves

| Equation | Shape |
|----------|-------|
| $r = a$ | Circle of radius $a$ |
| $r = a\cos\theta$ | Circle through the origin |
| $r = a(1 + \cos\theta)$ | Cardioid |
| $r = a\cos(k\theta)$ | Rose curve with $k$ or $2k$ petals |

> [!tip] Same point, many representations
> $(r, \theta)$ and $(r, \theta + 2\pi)$ describe the same point, and so do $(-r, \theta + \pi)$ and $(r, \theta)$.

---

## Converting Equations

**Example — convert $x^2 + y^2 = 4x$ to polar:**

$$r^2 = 4r\cos\theta \implies r = 4\cos\theta$$
