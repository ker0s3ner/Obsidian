## Overview

Classical algebraic geometry studies the geometry of solution sets of polynomial equations — **algebraic curves** — using tools that bridge algebra and geometry: intersection theory, projective coordinates, and invariants like genus.

## Central Thread

Every topic here circles back to one question: **how does the genus of a curve control its geometry?** Genus 0 curves are rational and parametrizable; genus 1 curves carry a group structure; higher genus curves resist both, which is why Faltings' theorem (finitely many rational points) only kicks in at $g \geq 2$.

> [!tip] Where This Leads
> This classical picture generalizes to modern algebraic geometry — schemes, sheaves, and cohomology — covered in [[4. Geometry/Algebraic Geometry]] and later in [[14.4 Sheaf Theory]].
