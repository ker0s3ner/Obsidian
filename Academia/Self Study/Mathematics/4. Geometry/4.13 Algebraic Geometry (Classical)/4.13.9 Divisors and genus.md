## Definition

A **divisor** on a curve $C$ is a finite formal sum of points with integer coefficients:

$$D = \sum_{P \in C} n_P \cdot P, \qquad n_P \in \mathbb{Z}, \text{ finitely many nonzero}$$

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| $\deg D = \sum n_P$ | Degree of the divisor |
| $D \geq 0$ | Effective divisor — all $n_P \geq 0$ |
| $\text{div}(f)$ | Principal divisor of zeros/poles of a rational function $f$ |

---

## Genus via the Degree-Genus Formula

For a **smooth** plane curve of degree $d$:

$$g = \frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}$$

| Degree $d$ | Genus $g$ | Example |
|------------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | 0 | Line |
| 2 | 0 | Conic |
| 3 | 1 | Elliptic curve |
| 4 | 3 | Smooth quartic |

> [!note] Singular Curves
> When $C$ has singularities, the **geometric genus** drops below this formula's value — each singularity of a certain type subtracts a correction term $\delta_P$ (see [[4.13.7 Rational curves]] for the nodal/cuspidal cubic case).

## Riemann–Roch Theorem

For a divisor $D$ on a smooth curve of genus $g$:

$$\ell(D) - \ell(K - D) = \deg D - g + 1$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| $\ell(D)$ | Dimension of the space of functions with poles bounded by $D$ |
| $K$ | Canonical divisor, $\deg K = 2g - 2$ |

**Example — genus 0 ($\mathbb{P}^1$):** every divisor of degree $\geq 0$ has $\ell(D) = \deg D + 1$, which is why $\mathbb{P}^1$ has such a rich supply of rational functions — consistent with [[4.13.7 Rational curves]].

**Example — genus 1 (elliptic curve):** $\deg K = 0$, and Riemann–Roch explains why the only effective divisors of degree 1 are single points, giving the group law of [[4.13.8 Elliptic curves]] its structure.

## Principal Divisors

A divisor $D$ is **principal** if $D = \text{div}(f)$ for some rational function $f$. Key fact:

$$\deg(\text{div}(f)) = 0$$

Two divisors are **equivalent**, $D \sim D'$, if their difference is principal. The **class group** $\text{Cl}(C) = \text{Div}(C)/\sim$ organizes these equivalence classes; for elliptic curves, $\text{Cl}^0(C)$ (degree-0 classes) is isomorphic to the curve itself under the group law.

## Practice Problems

1. Compute the genus of a smooth quintic ($d = 5$) plane curve.
2. Show that $\deg(\text{div}(f)) = 0$ for $f = x/y$ on a smooth conic.
3. Use Riemann–Roch to show $\ell(D) \geq \deg D - g + 1$ always holds.
4. Explain why every degree-0 divisor on $\mathbb{P}^1$ is principal.
5. For an elliptic curve, show that two points $P, Q$ give equivalent divisors $P \sim Q$ only when $P = Q$.
