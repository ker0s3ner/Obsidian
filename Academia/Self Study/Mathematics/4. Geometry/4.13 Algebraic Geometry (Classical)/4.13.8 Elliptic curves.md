## Definition

An **elliptic curve** is a smooth projective cubic curve of genus 1, together with a chosen point $O$ at infinity. Over a field of characteristic $\neq 2, 3$, it can be written in **Weierstrass form**:

$$y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| $a, b$ | Curve coefficients |
| $O$ | Point at infinity, serves as identity for the group law |
| $\Delta = -16(4a^3 + 27b^2)$ | Discriminant — the curve is smooth iff $\Delta \neq 0$ |

> [!warning] Singular Weierstrass Curves
> If $\Delta = 0$, the cubic has a node or cusp and is **not** an elliptic curve — it's a rational curve in disguise (see [[4.13.7 Rational curves]]).

## The Group Law

Any line meets a smooth cubic in exactly 3 points by [[4.13.5 Bezout theorem]]. This gives elliptic curves a natural abelian group structure:

$$P + Q + R = O \quad \text{whenever } P, Q, R \text{ are collinear}$$

**Geometric addition of $P$ and $Q$:**

1. Draw the line through $P$ and $Q$; it meets the curve at a third point $R'$
2. Reflect $R'$ across the $x$-axis to get $P + Q$

**Example — doubling a point:**

$$2P = P + P$$

is computed using the **tangent line** at $P$ instead of a secant.

## Explicit Coordinates

For $P = (x_1, y_1)$ and $Q = (x_2, y_2)$ with $P \neq \pm Q$:

$$\lambda = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}, \qquad x_3 = \lambda^2 - x_1 - x_2, \qquad y_3 = \lambda(x_1 - x_3) - y_1$$

For doubling ($P = Q$):

$$\lambda = \frac{3x_1^2 + a}{2y_1}$$

**Example — on $y^2 = x^3 - x$**, add $P = (0,0)$ is the 2-torsion point since $2P = O$ (tangent at $P$ is vertical).

## Genus and Why Elliptic Curves Aren't Rational

By the genus formula from [[4.13.7 Rational curves]], a smooth cubic has:

$$g = \frac{(3-1)(3-2)}{2} = 1$$

Since $g \neq 0$, the curve admits **no rational parametrization** — unlike conics or singular cubics.

---

## Applications

- **Cryptography**: elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) uses the group law over finite fields for discrete-log-based security
- **Number theory**: Mordell–Weil theorem — the group of rational points is finitely generated
- **Wiles' proof of Fermat's Last Theorem**: relies on the modularity of elliptic curves

1. Verify that $(0,0)$ lies on $y^2 = x^3 - x$ and find its order in the group law.
2. Compute $\Delta$ for $y^2 = x^3 + 1$ and determine whether the curve is smooth.
3. Using the explicit addition formulas, compute $P + Q$ for $P = (1,1)$, $Q = (2,3)$ on $y^2 = x^3 - x + 3$.
4. Explain geometrically why the point at infinity acts as the identity of the group law.
5. Why does the group law fail for singular cubics?
